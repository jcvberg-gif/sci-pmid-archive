## ⭐ Top 3 & High Tier

### 1. ⭐⭐ TOP 3 #1 — **Trismus in H&N cancer survivors: comprehensive rehabilitation-focused review**
PMID [41887725](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41887725/)
*BMJ Supportive & Palliative Care* · 2026 | CR07 · CR13

**Key outcomes:** Trismus affects 23–28% of HNC survivors post-radiotherapy; profoundly impairs nutrition, communication, oral hygiene, and QoL. Early structured rehabilitation reduces incidence and improves long-term mouth-opening outcomes. Prophylactic jaw exercises and device-assisted therapy (TheraBite, Dynasplint) are first-line.

**Methods:** Comprehensive narrative review synthesizing evidence across trismus epidemiology, assessment tools, first-line conservative strategies (prophylactic exercise, progressive device stretch), and pharmacological adjuncts; survivorship and functional recovery perspective. (Narrative review — no single RCT N.)

**Rehab take:** Trismus is under-recognized in standard HNC survivorship protocols. Physiatrists should advocate for: (1) prophylactic jaw exercise initiation at radiotherapy start, (2) prospective inter-incisor measurement (goal ≥35 mm) at each survivorship visit, and (3) automatic escalation to device-assisted therapy when mouth-opening <30 mm post-RT. Obtain RT completion dates to set structured rehab surveillance triggers.

**Team take:**
- 🗣️ **SLP** — Central role: jaw-opening measurement at every swallowing visit; integrate trismus and dysphagia management; monitor TheraBite compliance; co-treat with PT for combined trismus/dysphagia programs.
- 🦿 **PT** — Device-assisted jaw mobilization; manual therapy for cervical/shoulder RT sequelae; coordinate exercise timing with meal schedules.
- ✋ **OT** — Assess compensatory feeding strategies, adaptive utensils, mealtime positioning when oral aperture is limited.
- 🩺 **Oncology Nursing** — Flag trismus at post-RT follow-up; educate on home jaw exercise compliance; include mouth-opening assessment in survivorship nursing protocol.
- 🧠 **Psych-Oncology** — Trismus-related changes to eating and communication significantly impact body image and social participation; early referral when patient avoids mealtimes or social settings.

### 2. ⭐⭐ TOP 3 #2 — **CDT significantly improves single-task and dual-task gait speed in cancer lower-limb lymphedema**
PMID [39688240](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39688240/)
*Lymphatic Research and Biology* · 2025 | CR03 · CR11

**Key outcomes:** Single-task gait speed (p = 0.002) and dual-task gait speed (p < 0.001) both improved significantly after CDT; dual-task cost decreased (p = 0.001), indicating improved cognitive-motor integration during walking.

**Methods:** Quasi-experimental pre-post study; N = 24, stage 2 lower-limb lymphedema (mean age 56.0 ± 14.2 years); cancer-related etiology; standard CDT protocol; single-task and dual-task 10-m walk tests. Authors: Gökçe E, Gün N, Düzlü Öztürk Ü, Dalyan M.

**Rehab take:** Extends CDT indication beyond volume reduction to include functional gait and fall-risk reduction. Physiatrists ordering CDT for pelvic/gynecologic cancer survivors should add gait speed and dual-task walk assessments alongside limb-volume measurements.

**Team take:**
- 🦿 **PT** — Add dual-task gait testing (TUG + cognitive secondary task) at CDT initiation and discharge; supports fall-prevention billing alongside lymphedema billing; incorporate functional mobility goals into CDT treatment planning.
- 🩺 **Oncology Nursing** — Lymphedema garment adherence carries fall-risk implications beyond swelling; reinforce compression compliance at infusion visits with a functional mobility framing.
- ✋ **OT** — Home fall-hazard assessment for patients with lower-limb lymphedema; link garment education to functional independence and home safety.
- 🗣️ **SLP** — Not directly applicable.
- 🧠 **Psych-Oncology** — Improved gait confidence may re-engage patients in social participation; probe fall-related fear at psychosocial screening.

### 3. ⭐⭐ TOP 3 #3 — **CIPN rehab scoping review: PT/OT referral pathways underutilized despite evidence across 26 RCTs**
PMID [40648551](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40648551/)
*Healthcare (Basel)* · 2025 Jun | CR02

**Key outcomes:** CIPN affects up to 65% of patients on neurotoxic regimens; raises fall risk; commonly drives dose reduction or discontinuation. Rehabilitation programs remain underutilized despite evidence from 26 RCTs covering balance/gait training, sensory re-education, and multimodal exercise.

**Methods:** Scoping review; 26 RCTs, 2017–2025; cancer types: breast, colorectal, hematologic, multiple myeloma; agents: paclitaxel, oxaliplatin, bortezomib. Authors: Al-Ajlouni YA, Al Ta'ani O, Zweig SA, Bak M, Tanashat M, Gabr A, Khamis Z, Al-Bitar F, Islam M.

**Rehab take:** Standardized CIPN screening using combined PRO (FACT/GOG-Ntx, PRO-CTCAE) and objective measures (10-m walk, monofilament) should trigger automatic PT/OT referral. Physiatrists should lead CIPN protocol development with oncology, tying referral timing to neurotoxic chemotherapy cycles.

**Team take:**
- 🦿 **PT** — Balance/gait training and sensory re-education are highest-evidence; standardize CIPN screening at each visit; document fall history; consider aquatic therapy when land-based balance is unsafe.
- ✋ **OT** — Fine motor adaptation (adaptive equipment, grips) for upper-extremity CIPN; energy conservation; driving safety assessment for severe sensory loss.
- 🩺 **Oncology Nursing** — PRO screening before each infusion cycle; flag dose-reduction candidates for physiatry consult before chemotherapy is reduced or stopped; educate on non-slip footwear and lighting.
- 🧠 **Psych-Oncology** — CIPN causes anxiety about falling, treatment stopping, and permanent disability; motivational interviewing to sustain rehab engagement when symptoms feel permanent.
- 🗣️ **SLP** — Not directly applicable.

### 4. ⭐ HIGH — **Exercise during cancer treatment reframed as a treatment-adherence strategy**
PMID [39982316](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39982316/)
*Exercise & Sport Sciences Reviews* · 2025 Jul | CR09

**Key outcomes:** Structured exercise concurrent with cancer treatment can reduce treatment-related toxicity and improve completion of chemotherapy, immunotherapy, and radiotherapy. Standardized terminology for "treatment completion" is urgently needed across oncology exercise research.

**Methods:** Narrative mechanistic evidence synthesis; multiple cancer types; aerobic, resistance, and combined exercise; concurrent with active treatment. Conceptual model: exercise → reduced toxicity → improved treatment adherence. Authors: Catalá-Vilaplana I, Cao SE, Zadravec K, LeVasseur N, Kimple RJ, Lim AJ, Courneya KS, Campbell KL.

**Rehab take:** Reframes exercise oncology as a treatment-adherence strategy, not just a QoL supplement. Physiatrists should advocate for exercise referral concurrent with chemotherapy initiation, not deferred to survivorship.

**Team take:**
- 🦿 **PT** — Establish baseline fitness at chemotherapy start; design exercise dosing around infusion cycle schedule; report treatment interruptions to oncology team.
- ✋ **OT** — Energy pacing and activity modification to sustain exercise capacity across cycles; monitor for functional decline between cycles.
- 🩺 **Oncology Nursing** — Include exercise adherence question in infusion-day nursing assessment; use infusion chair time for home exercise education between cycles.
- 🧠 **Psych-Oncology** — Low exercise motivation during treatment is often anxiety-driven; behavioral activation and motivational interviewing can unlock exercise engagement.
- 🗣️ **SLP** — Not directly applicable.

*Cureus* · 2025 Jul | CR11

**Key outcomes:** Structured PT (prehabilitation, exercise, CDT) significantly reduces pain, fatigue, lymphedema, and deconditioning; improves QoL across all cancer care phases. Authors: Baladaniya M, Baldania S.

**Rehab take:** Useful framework for phase-appropriate PT referral criteria; supports physician documentation of PT medical necessity at each care phase.

**Team take:**

### 6. MEDIUM — **OT services improve functional mobility in inpatient cancer: 5-year NCI hospital data**
PMID [40463783](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40463783/)
*Archives of Rehabilitation Research and Clinical Translation* · 2025 | CR12

**Key outcomes:** Significant AM-PAC 6-Clicks Basic Mobility improvement from first OT session to discharge (N = 1,949 paired; unadjusted); non-significant in adjusted regression, indicating case-mix complexity moderates the net effect.

**Methods:** Retrospective observational; NCI hospital, Richmond VA; 5 years; 6 cancer diagnoses. Authors: McNichols CC, Peterson AK, Reynolds S.

**Rehab take:** Supports early OT referral in standing orders for cancer patients with functional decline.

**Team take:**
- ✋ **OT** — AM-PAC 6-Clicks is the key outcome measure; document at every session for before/after functional comparison that supports OT authorization and demonstrates value to oncology teams; consider proactive consultation protocols with hematologic and GI cancer services.

### 7. MEDIUM — **Cancer-related fatigue disrupts occupational identity; 80% of patients have low fatigue self-efficacy**
PMID [41743610](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41743610/)
*British Journal of Occupational Therapy* · 2026 | CR01 · CR12

**Key outcomes:** 59% severe fatigue; 80% low fatigue self-efficacy; leisure, social, and high-demand activities most affected. Themes: loss of meaningful occupation, reactive/proactive coping, disrupted occupational identity.

**Methods:** Mixed-methods; N = 17; semi-structured interviews + Activity Card Sort–UK; diverse cancer types; ongoing CRF. Authors: Brooks C, Rossiter L.

**Rehab take:** Identity disruption from CRF is not captured by fatigue severity scores alone; occupational-engagement assessment at IDT rounds surfaces burdens that symptom scales miss.

**Team take:**
- ✋ **OT** — Activity Card Sort (ACS-UK) is validated for this population; use during evaluation to guide energy conservation goals; specifically query meaningful high-demand activities patients may have silently discontinued.
- 🧠 **Psych-Oncology** — Identity disruption from CRF drives adjustment disorder and depression; at IDT rounds, flag patients withdrawing from valued activities as higher-risk for psychological distress; pair OT referral with motivational interviewing.

### 8. MEDIUM — **Only 28.8% of H&N cancer patients with dysphagia received SLP services; disparities by race and insurance**
PMID [40224296](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40224296/)
*OTO Open* · 2025 | CR07 · CR13

**Key outcomes:** Of 269,629 HNC patients, 28.8% had dysphagia; significant SLP utilization disparities by race, insurance, and geography; underutilization greatest in non-White and uninsured patients.

**Methods:** Retrospective cohort; TriNetX (~125M de-identified EHRs); oral, oropharyngeal, laryngeal, nasopharyngeal cancers; 2004–2024.

**Rehab take:** SLP referral for HNC dysphagia should be automatic, not request-based; advocate for standing SLP consultation orders in HNC post-treatment protocols; a local referral-equity audit is warranted.

**Team take:**
- 🗣️ **SLP** — Data support a proactive population-level SLP outreach model; propose standardized dysphagia screening at tumor board or HNC multidisciplinary clinic.
- 🩺 **Oncology Nursing** — Implement standardized nursing dysphagia screening (3-oz water swallow) to generate SLP referrals before patients self-report significant symptoms.

### 9. MEDIUM — **Nursing-led BCRL strategies: from perioperative prevention to bioimpedance surveillance**
PMID [41783598](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41783598/)
*Journal of Multidisciplinary Healthcare* · 2026 | CR03 · CR14

**Key outcomes:** Nurses function as central coordinators across the BCRL care continuum; nursing-led strategies encompass early risk stratification, perioperative prevention, compression garment education, psychosocial support, and integration of bioimpedance spectroscopy (BIS) and wearable monitoring.

**Rehab take:** Supports a structured oncology nursing BCRL protocol: pre-surgical risk screening, standardized arm measurements at defined timepoints, garment education at discharge, and defined CDT escalation criteria.

**Team take:**
- 🩺 **Oncology Nursing** — Build a BCRL nursing checklist (circumferential arm measurements at baseline, 3 months, 6 months post-axillary surgery); consider adopting BIS in outpatient oncology nursing.
- 🦿 **PT** — Define local circumferential threshold (typically >2 cm difference) that triggers automatic CDT referral; ensure nursing-to-PT handoff pathway is explicit in the MFB protocol.

### 10. MEDIUM — **Distress Thermometer problem load predicts depression severity; younger and non-White patients most at risk**
PMID [39939111](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39939111/)
*Journal of Psychiatric and Mental Health Nursing* · 2025 Feb | CR15

**Key outcomes:** Higher DT Problem List endorsement independently predicts higher distress and depression scores; younger, non-White, and breast cancer patients endorsed highest problem loads.

**Methods:** Cross-sectional; psycho-oncology service patients; DT + Problem List, PHQ-9. Authors: Amin SM, Mohamed MAE, Metwally El-Sayed M, El-Ashry AM.

**Rehab take:** DT Problem List adds clinical value beyond the numeric score; communicate problem-list category findings to psych-oncology at IDT.

**Team take:**
- 🧠 **Psych-Oncology** — At IDT, present problem-list categories (practical, family, emotional, physical, spiritual) rather than the numeric score alone; younger and non-White patients are higher-risk and may need active outreach.
- 🩺 **Oncology Nursing** — Ensure the full Problem List is documented and communicated to the interdisciplinary team, not just the numeric score.

### 11. MEDIUM — **SIOG consensus: prehabilitation for older cancer patients requires frailty-adjusted exercise prescription**
PMID [40096764](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40096764/)
*Journal of Geriatric Oncology* · 2025 Apr | CR10

**Key outcomes:** Prehabilitation improves functional capacity and surgical outcomes in older cancer patients; frailty and comorbidity must inform exercise intensity; multimodal prehabilitation (exercise + nutrition + psychological support) is most effective; challenges: polypharmacy, sarcopenia, cognitive impairment. Authors: Papadopoulos E, Brick R, Sirois A, et al. (SIOG Rehabilitation Group).

**Rehab take:** Establish a frailty-screen-to-prehab pathway for older patients anticipated for major oncologic surgery; use FRAIL scale, Timed Up and Go, and grip strength at pre-surgical consultation to guide exercise intensity prescription.

### 12. MEDIUM — **Mirels, SINS, and emerging AI models for pathological fracture risk in bone metastases**
PMID [40558252](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40558252/)
*Current Oncology* · 2025 May | CR04

**Key outcomes:** 50–70% of advanced cancer patients develop bone metastases; Mirels score (long bones) and SINS (spine) remain standard fracture-risk tools; emerging CT-based finite element analysis and AI-assisted imaging models show improved prediction accuracy. Authors: Altsitzioglou P, Tsukamoto S, Errani C, Tanaka Y, Mavrogenis AF.

**Rehab take:** Routinely document Mirels/SINS scores in cancer rehab evaluations; fracture-risk score should explicitly dictate weight-bearing status orders and precautions communicated to PT/OT at IDT rounds.

### 13. MEDIUM — **Redefining pediatric oncology survivorship: life-threatening late effects demand proactive rehab follow-up**
PMID [40215428](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40215428/)
*ASCO Educational Book* · 2025 Jun | CR08

**Key outcomes:** Therapeutic advances have created a large and growing childhood cancer survivor population; a substantial proportion develop life-threatening chronic conditions (subsequent neoplasms, cardiovascular disease, neurocognitive deficits); survivorship care must be proactively structured. Authors: Armenian SH, Kremer L, Tonorezos ES, Bhatia S.

**Rehab take:** Updated COG Long-Term Follow-Up Guidelines (v6.0, JAMA Oncology May 2025) benchmark late-effect surveillance including functional and neurodevelopmental outcomes. Physiatrists caring for pediatric/AYA cancer survivors should reference COG LTFU v6.0 for rehabilitation referral thresholds (deconditioning, neuropathy, musculoskeletal effects from radiation or steroid use).

### 14. LOW — **Aerobic exercise attenuates radiation-induced pulmonary fibrosis in a rat model**
PMID [40225789](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40225789/)
*Clinical and Translational Radiation Oncology* · 2025 | CR05

**Key outcomes:** Regular aerobic exercise significantly reduced radiation-induced pulmonary fibrosis severity in rats; anti-inflammatory and anti-fibrotic mechanisms implicated (reduced TGF-β, inflammatory cytokines). Authors: Ko DB, Cha S, Jeon JY, Kim SS, Kim W, Lee SW, Min J, Song SY, Lee SH. Asan Medical Center, Seoul.

**Rehab take:** Animal study only — not ready for clinical protocol change. Provides mechanistic rationale supporting aerobic exercise programs in patients receiving thoracic radiotherapy (lung, esophageal, mediastinal lymphoma). Monitor for human translation studies.
