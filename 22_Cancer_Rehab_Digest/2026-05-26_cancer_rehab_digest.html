⭐⭐ TOP 3 #1 · CR07 · CR13

Trismus in H&N cancer survivors: comprehensive rehabilitation-focused review

PMID 41887725 · BMJ Supportive & Palliative Care · 2026

Key outcomes: Trismus affects 23–28% of HNC survivors post-radiotherapy; profoundly impairs nutrition, communication, oral hygiene, and QoL. Early structured rehabilitation reduces incidence. Prophylactic jaw exercises and device-assisted therapy (TheraBite, Dynasplint) are first-line.

Methods: Comprehensive narrative review; trismus epidemiology, assessment tools, conservative strategies, pharmacological adjuncts; survivorship and functional recovery perspective.

Rehab take: Physiatrists should advocate for: (1) prophylactic jaw exercise at RT start, (2) prospective inter-incisor measurement (goal ≥35 mm), (3) device-assisted therapy when <30 mm post-RT. Obtain RT completion dates to set rehab surveillance triggers.

Team Take:

🗣️ SLP — Jaw-opening measurement at every swallowing visit; integrate trismus and dysphagia management; TheraBite compliance monitoring; co-treat with PT.

🦿 PT — Device-assisted jaw mobilization; manual therapy for cervical/shoulder RT sequelae; coordinate exercise timing with meal schedules.

✋ OT — Compensatory feeding strategies, adaptive utensils, mealtime positioning when oral aperture is limited.

🩺 Oncology Nursing — Flag trismus at post-RT follow-up; home jaw exercise compliance education; include mouth-opening in survivorship nursing protocol.

🧠 Psych-Oncology — Body image and social participation impacted; early referral when patient avoids mealtimes or social settings.

⭐⭐ TOP 3 #2 · CR03 · CR11

CDT significantly improves single-task and dual-task gait speed in cancer lower-limb lymphedema

PMID 39688240 · Lymphatic Research and Biology · 2025

Key outcomes: Single-task gait speed (p=0.002) and dual-task gait speed (p<0.001) improved significantly after CDT; dual-task cost decreased (p=0.001).

Methods: Quasi-experimental pre-post; N=24, stage 2 lower-limb lymphedema; standard CDT; single/dual-task 10-m walk tests. Authors: Gökçe E, Gün N, Düzlü Öztürk Ü, Dalyan M.

Rehab take: Extends CDT indication to functional gait and fall-risk. Add gait speed and dual-task walk assessments alongside limb-volume measurements for pelvic/gynecologic cancer survivors.

Team Take:

🦿 PT — Dual-task gait testing (TUG + cognitive task) at CDT initiation and discharge; supports fall-prevention billing alongside lymphedema billing.

🩺 Oncology Nursing — Garment adherence has fall-risk implications; reinforce compliance at infusion visits.

✋ OT — Home fall-hazard assessment; link garment education to home safety and functional independence.

🗣️ SLP — Not directly applicable.

🧠 Psych-Oncology — Improved gait confidence may re-engage patients in social participation; probe fall-related fear.

⭐⭐ TOP 3 #3 · CR02

CIPN rehab scoping review: PT/OT referral pathways underutilized despite evidence across 26 RCTs

PMID 40648551 · Healthcare (Basel) · 2025 Jun

Key outcomes: CIPN affects up to 65% on neurotoxic regimens; raises fall risk; drives dose reduction/discontinuation. Rehabilitation underutilized despite 26 RCTs of balance/gait training, sensory re-education, and multimodal exercise.

Methods: Scoping review; 26 RCTs, 2017–2025; breast/colorectal/hematologic/myeloma; paclitaxel/oxaliplatin/bortezomib. Authors: Al-Ajlouni YA et al.

Rehab take: Combined PRO + objective CIPN screening should trigger automatic PT/OT referral. Lead CIPN protocol development with oncology, tying referral to chemotherapy cycle timing.

Team Take:

🦿 PT — Balance/gait training and sensory re-education are highest-evidence; standardize CIPN screening at each visit; consider aquatic therapy when land-based balance is unsafe.

✋ OT — Fine motor adaptation for upper-extremity CIPN; driving safety assessment for severe sensory loss.

🩺 Oncology Nursing — PRO screening before each infusion cycle; flag dose-reduction candidates for physiatry consult; educate on non-slip footwear and lighting.

🧠 Psych-Oncology — CIPN anxiety about falling, treatment stopping, permanent disability; motivational interviewing to sustain rehab engagement.

🗣️ SLP — Not directly applicable.

⭐ HIGH · CR09

Exercise during cancer treatment reframed as a treatment-adherence strategy

PMID 39982316 · Exercise & Sport Sciences Reviews · 2025 Jul

Key outcomes: Structured exercise during treatment can reduce toxicity and improve completion of chemotherapy, immunotherapy, and radiotherapy. Standardized reporting terminology is urgently needed.

Methods: Narrative mechanistic synthesis; multiple cancer types; aerobic/resistance/combined exercise; conceptual model: exercise → reduced toxicity → improved adherence. Authors: Catalá-Vilaplana I, Cao SE, Courneya KS, Campbell KL, et al.

Rehab take: Reframes exercise oncology as a treatment-adherence strategy. Advocate for exercise referral concurrent with chemotherapy initiation, not deferred to survivorship.

Team Take:

🦿 PT — Baseline fitness at chemotherapy start; exercise dosing around infusion cycle schedule; report treatment interruptions to oncology team.

✋ OT — Energy pacing to sustain exercise capacity across cycles; monitor functional decline between cycles.

🩺 Oncology Nursing — Exercise adherence question in infusion-day assessment; use infusion chair time for home exercise education.

🧠 Psych-Oncology — Low exercise motivation often anxiety-driven; behavioral activation and motivational interviewing.

🗣️ SLP — Not directly applicable.

MEDIUM · CR12

OT services improve functional mobility in inpatient cancer: 5-year NCI hospital data

PMID 40463783 · Archives of Rehabilitation Research and Clinical Translation · 2025 | McNichols CC, Peterson AK, Reynolds S

Key outcomes: Significant AM-PAC 6-Clicks Basic Mobility improvement with OT (N=1,949 paired; unadjusted); non-significant in adjusted regression reflecting case-mix.
Rehab take: Include OT in standing orders for cancer patients with functional decline.

✋ OT — AM-PAC 6-Clicks is the key outcome measure; document at every session; supports OT authorization; consider proactive consultation protocols with hematologic and GI cancer services.

MEDIUM · CR01 · CR12

Cancer-related fatigue disrupts occupational identity; 80% of patients have low fatigue self-efficacy

PMID 41743610 · British Journal of Occupational Therapy · 2026 | Brooks C, Rossiter L

Key outcomes: 59% severe fatigue; 80% low fatigue self-efficacy; leisure, social, high-demand activities most affected. Themes: loss of meaningful occupation, disrupted identity.
Methods: Mixed-methods; N=17; Activity Card Sort–UK; diverse cancer types.
Rehab take: Identity disruption from CRF is not captured by severity scores alone; occupational-engagement assessment surfaces hidden burdens.

✋ OT — Activity Card Sort (ACS-UK) validated for this population; specifically query meaningful high-demand activities patients may have silently discontinued.

🧠 Psych-Oncology — Identity disruption drives adjustment disorder/depression; flag patients withdrawing from valued activities as higher-risk; pair OT referral with motivational interviewing.

MEDIUM · CR07 · CR13

Only 28.8% of H&N cancer patients with dysphagia received SLP services; disparities by race and insurance

PMID 40224296 · OTO Open · 2025

Key outcomes: Of 269,629 HNC patients, 28.8% had dysphagia; significant SLP utilization disparities by race, insurance, geography.
Methods: Retrospective cohort; TriNetX (~125M EHRs); oral/oropharyngeal/laryngeal/nasopharyngeal cancers; 2004–2024.
Rehab take: SLP referral for HNC dysphagia should be automatic; advocate for standing SLP consultation orders; local referral-equity audit warranted.

🗣️ SLP — Propose standardized dysphagia screening at tumor board or HNC multidisciplinary clinic.

🩺 Oncology Nursing — Standardized nursing dysphagia screening (3-oz water swallow) to generate SLP referrals before patients self-report symptoms.

MEDIUM · CR03 · CR14

Nursing-led BCRL strategies: from perioperative prevention to bioimpedance surveillance

PMID 41783598 · Journal of Multidisciplinary Healthcare · 2026

Key outcomes: Nurses as central coordinators across BCRL continuum; nursing-led strategies include risk stratification, perioperative prevention, garment education, psychosocial support, bioimpedance spectroscopy (BIS), wearable monitoring.
Rehab take: Supports structured oncology nursing BCRL protocol with standardized arm measurements and CDT escalation criteria.

🩺 Oncology Nursing — BCRL nursing checklist: circumferential arm measurements at baseline, 3 months, 6 months post-axillary surgery; consider adopting BIS.

🦿 PT — Define circumferential threshold (>2 cm) triggering automatic CDT referral; ensure explicit nursing-to-PT handoff pathway.

MEDIUM · CR15

Distress Thermometer problem load predicts depression severity; younger and non-White patients most at risk

PMID 39939111 · Journal of Psychiatric and Mental Health Nursing · 2025 Feb | Amin SM et al.

Key outcomes: Higher DT Problem List endorsement independently predicts distress and depression; younger, non-White, and breast cancer patients endorsed highest problem loads.
Methods: Cross-sectional; psycho-oncology service patients; DT + Problem List, PHQ-9.
Rehab take: DT Problem List adds clinical value beyond numeric score; communicate problem-list categories to psych-oncology at IDT.

🧠 Psych-Oncology — Present problem-list categories (practical, family, emotional, physical, spiritual) at IDT, not just the score; younger and non-White patients need active outreach.

🩺 Oncology Nursing — Ensure the full Problem List is documented and communicated to the IDT, not just the numeric score.

MEDIUM · CR10

SIOG consensus: prehabilitation for older cancer patients requires frailty-adjusted exercise prescription

PMID 40096764 · Journal of Geriatric Oncology · 2025 Apr | Papadopoulos E, Brick R, Sirois A, et al. (SIOG Rehabilitation Group)

Key outcomes: Prehabilitation improves functional capacity and surgical outcomes in older cancer patients; frailty and comorbidity must inform exercise intensity; multimodal prehabilitation most effective; challenges include polypharmacy, sarcopenia, cognitive impairment.
Rehab take: Establish frailty-screen-to-prehab pathway; use FRAIL scale, TUG, and grip strength at pre-surgical consultation.

MEDIUM · CR04

Mirels, SINS, and emerging AI models for pathological fracture risk in bone metastases

PMID 40558252 · Current Oncology · 2025 May | Altsitzioglou P, Tsukamoto S, Errani C, Tanaka Y, Mavrogenis AF

Key outcomes: 50–70% of advanced cancer patients develop bone metastases; Mirels score (long bones) and SINS (spine) remain standard; emerging CT-based and AI-assisted models show improved accuracy.
Rehab take: Routinely document Mirels/SINS scores; fracture-risk score should dictate weight-bearing status orders communicated to PT/OT at IDT rounds.

MEDIUM · CR08

Redefining pediatric oncology survivorship: life-threatening late effects demand proactive rehab follow-up

PMID 40215428 · ASCO Educational Book · 2025 Jun | Armenian SH, Kremer L, Tonorezos ES, Bhatia S

Key outcomes: Therapeutic advances have created a growing childhood cancer survivor population; substantial proportion develop life-threatening chronic conditions (subsequent neoplasms, cardiovascular disease, neurocognitive deficits).
Rehab take: Updated COG LTFU Guidelines v6.0 (JAMA Oncology May 2025) benchmark late-effect surveillance including functional and neurodevelopmental outcomes; reference for rehabilitation referral thresholds in pediatric/AYA survivors.

LOW · CR05

Aerobic exercise attenuates radiation-induced pulmonary fibrosis in a rat model

PMID 40225789 · Clinical and Translational Radiation Oncology · 2025 | Ko DB, Cha S, Jeon JY, et al. (Asan Medical Center, Seoul)

Key outcomes: Regular aerobic exercise significantly reduced radiation-induced pulmonary fibrosis severity in rats; anti-inflammatory and anti-fibrotic mechanisms (reduced TGF-β, inflammatory cytokines).

Rehab take: Animal study only — not ready for clinical protocol change. Mechanistic rationale for aerobic exercise during thoracic radiotherapy (lung, esophageal, mediastinal lymphoma). Monitor for human translation studies.
