🔮 WEEKLY SPOTLIGHT

1. ⭐ HIGH — ISICEM 2025 expert consensus: 6-step evidence-based roadmap for disorders of consciousness management in the ICU

PMID 41396553

Intensive Care Medicine · Dec 2025 T03

Key outcomes: 18 international experts synthesized DoC evidence into a six-step practical roadmap: (1) identify DoC; (2) assess and treat confounders; (3) establish CRS-R/neuroimaging diagnosis; (4) prognosticate; (5) select pharmacologic/non-pharmacologic interventions; (6) communicate with family via shared decision-making. Covers coma, unresponsive wakefulness syndrome (UWS), and minimally conscious state (MCS) from TBI and non-TBI etiologies.

Methods: Expert consensus roundtable at 2025 ISICEM; 18 clinicians and researchers; systematic evidence synthesis covering CRS-R, neuroimaging (MRI, fMRI, EEG), pharmacologic interventions (amantadine, zolpidem), and family communication frameworks.

Rehab take: At IRF admission for DoC patients: verify CRS-R documentation from referring ICU; confirm amantadine initiated (if no contraindication); ensure confounders addressed prior to behavioral assessment; implement formal family communication protocol per step 6. This roadmap is the new standard for IRF DoC admission orders.

🦿 PT — Steps 4–5 include early mobility and sensory stimulation; document CRS-R motor subscale and arousal responses during PT as behavioral data.

✋ OT — CRS-R visual tracking and oromotor subscales inform OT assessment; align OT evaluation timing with CRS-R administration schedule.

🗣️ SLP — CRS-R communication subscale guides SLP; dysphagia screening and AAC assessment are step-3 priorities once reliable volitional motor response established.

🩺 Nursing — Nursing anchors steps 1–2 (DoC recognition, confounder identification) and step 6 (family communication); shift-level CRS-R-informed behavioral tracking is the backbone of diagnosis.

🧠 Neuropsych/Psychology — Step 6 emphasizes psychologist-facilitated family support and shared prognostic communication; embed psychology in ICU-to-IRF transition conversations.

2. MEDIUM — Long-term onabotulinumtoxinA for post-acquired brain injury spasticity: safe and effective over 3+ years (N=224)

PMID 41527032

Toxins · Jan 2026 T06

Key outcomes: No unexpected adverse events over ≥3-year follow-up; repeated injections maintained goal attainment; early initiation (<1 year post-injury) associated with better GAS outcomes; treatment discontinuation rate was low.

Methods: Retrospective single-center cohort; N=224 post-acquired brain injury (primarily post-stroke; spasticity pathophysiology and botulinum toxin mechanism directly extrapolate to TBI); injection cycles tracked 2012–2023; minimum 3-year follow-up.

Rehab take: Long-term onabotulinumtoxinA is safe in brain injury survivors; initiate within first year; track GAS per injection cycle; coordinate with PT/OT for task-specific retraining in post-injection window (weeks 2–6).

🦿 PT — Post-injection window weeks 2–6 optimal for stretching and gait retraining; document MAS and ambulation outcomes pre/post each cycle.

✋ OT — Upper limb injection + OT task-specific practice: document pinch/grip strength, Fugl-Meyer UE, and functional ADL performance pre/post cycle.

Journal of Clinical Medicine · Sep 2025 T01

📅 MONTHLY HIGHLIGHTS

4. ⭐⭐ TOP 3 #1 — Double-blind RCT (N=184): methylphenidate + amantadine combination significantly improves 6-month GOSE versus placebo in blunt TBI

PMID 40619501

Acta Neurologica Belgica · 2025 T10

Key outcomes: GOSE significantly higher in dual-treatment group (6.22±2.06 vs 5.37±2.54, p=0.014); no significant difference in hospital or ICU LOS; no major adverse events with combination; benefit most pronounced at long-term follow-up.

Methods: Double-blinded RCT; N=184 blunt TBI (mean age 40.63±17.46 years, 84.8% male, equal allocation); amantadine 100 mg BID + methylphenidate 20 mg BID vs matched placebo; primary outcome GOSE at long-term follow-up.

Rehab take: First double-blinded RCT of dual-agent neurostimulation in TBI. Consider amantadine 100 mg BID + methylphenidate 20 mg BID for blunt TBI patients with prolonged recovery plateau; monitor for cardiac effects; document ABS baseline and per-shift during first 2 weeks.

🦿 PT — GOSE improvement reflects better functional trajectory; document motor milestones (BBS, TUG, ambulation distance) as surrogate efficacy markers during trial.

✋ OT — Track FIM-Cog and FIM-Motor during medication trial to capture ADL functional trajectory.

🩺 Nursing — Monitor vitals and ABS per shift during first 2 weeks; document paradoxical agitation; hold methylphenidate if HR >110 or new arrhythmia.

🧠 Neuropsych/Psychology — Methylphenidate may accelerate attention/processing speed; delay formal neuropsychological baseline testing ≥2 weeks after medication stabilization.

5. ⭐⭐ TOP 3 #2 — TRACK-TBI multicenter cohort: subacute RPQ score predicts 6-month persistent PCS, GOSE, and QOL in mTBI

PMID 40168631

Neurology · Apr 2025 T01, T02

Key outcomes: Higher subacute RPQ total score strongly and significantly associated with persistent PCS, lower GOSE, and reduced QOLIBRI-OS at 6 months (all p<0.001); 18 US Level 1 trauma center prospective data validates subacute symptom screening as risk-stratification tool.

Methods: Secondary analysis of TRACK-TBI prospective multicenter cohort (2014–2018); 18 US Level 1 trauma centers; GCS 13–15 (mTBI); RPQ, GOSE, and QOLIBRI-OS assessed subacutely and at 6-month follow-up.

Rehab take: Administer RPQ at subacute follow-up (2–4 weeks post-injury); elevated score triggers proactive concussion clinic referral — not generic reassurance; validates early multidisciplinary intervention over watchful waiting.

🦿 PT — Vestibular/balance RPQ items predict persistent deficits; flag high-RPQ patients for vestibular PT consult at discharge or follow-up.

✋ OT — Cognitive RPQ items (concentration, memory) predict lower 6-month GOSE; incorporate compensatory strategy training from first outpatient contact.

🗣️ SLP — Cognitive-linguistic symptoms co-occur with elevated RPQ; include cognitive-communication screening at subacute follow-up.

🩺 Nursing — Provide high-RPQ patients with written symptom diary and recovery timeline; track trajectory at each encounter.

🧠 Neuropsych/Psychology — Emotional RPQ items predict QOLIBRI-OS decline; early psychological screening and CBT/supportive therapy referral for high-risk patients.

6. ⭐ HIGH — Updated amantadine meta-analysis (9 RCTs, N=1,182): DRS improvement confirmed; effect most robust in first 6 weeks post-TBI

PMID 39906329

Frontiers in Neurology · Jan 2025 T10

Key outcomes: 9 RCTs, 1,182 patients; amantadine 87.5–400 mg/day; pooled DRS improvement statistically significant vs placebo in acute-to-subacute window (≤6 weeks); safety acceptable; effect attenuates beyond 6 weeks; most robust in moderate-severe TBI and DOC.

Methods: Systematic review and meta-analysis (updated through March 2024); PubMed, Cochrane, Embase; predominantly moderate-severe TBI and DOC populations; dose and timing heterogeneity analyzed.

Rehab take: Amantadine 100–200 mg BID should be initiated early — the 6-week window is the most evidence-supported interval; document DRS at admission and 4-week mark to track objective medication response.

🦿 PT — Serial FIM-Motor and BBS assessments during amantadine trial window can objectify medication response; optimal PT session timing relative to dose peak may improve arousal for participation.

🩺 Nursing — Monitor for amantadine side effects: livedo reticularis, pedal edema, orthostatic hypotension, paradoxical confusion; document ABS baseline before initiation.

7. MEDIUM — VNS observational cohort (N=102): adjunctive non-invasive vagus nerve stimulation reduces persistent post-concussion symptoms across multiple domains

PMID 40937177

Frontiers in Neurology · Aug 2025 T02

Key outcomes: 34% met responder criteria (≥30% reduction in ≥50% of symptom domains); largest improvements in post-traumatic headache, difficulty concentrating, dizziness, and depression/sadness; no device-related adverse events; time-to-treatment did not influence outcomes.

Methods: Single-center observational cohort; N=102 mTBI patients with persistent PCS receiving adjunctive nVNS; cognitive, affective, somatic, and vestibular domains assessed.

Rehab take: nVNS is low-risk adjunct for persistent multi-domain mTBI PCS; observational design limits causality — reasonable for refractory patients; document domain-specific symptom severity (RPQ or PCSI) before and after trial.

🦿 PT — Vestibular/dizziness domain response supports integration with vestibular PT; document symptom trajectory across sessions during nVNS trial.

🗣️ SLP — Concentration domain improvement has downstream benefit for cognitive-linguistic tasks; SLP workload progression may accelerate during nVNS trial.

🧠 Neuropsych/Psychology — Depression/sadness domain response noted; nVNS may lower barrier to behavioral therapy engagement.

8. MEDIUM — Multicenter mTBI cohort (N=163): catastrophizing and fear-avoidance independently predict persistent post-concussion symptoms at 3 and 6 months

PMID 40129898

Neurotrauma Reports · Feb 2025 T02, T08, T15

Key outcomes: FAM components correlated significantly at 3 months (r=0.40–0.72, all p<0.01); catastrophizing and fear-avoidance independently predicted PCS at 3 and 6 months; depression co-varied with all FAM elements; biopsychosocial model validated in multicenter mTBI cohort.

Methods: Prospective, longitudinal, multicenter cohort; N=163 mTBI (GCS 13–15); FAM elements assessed at 2 weeks, 3 months, 6 months post-mTBI.

Rehab take: Screen for catastrophizing and fear-avoidance at 2-week follow-up; patients with elevated scores receive psychology referral at that visit — not deferred; early CBT with graduated activity exposure is mechanistically supported.

🩺 Nursing — Brief catastrophizing screen (TSK-TBI or BSI-18) at 2-week visit; avoid reinforcing avoidance through overly protective messaging; flag for psychology referral.

🧠 Neuropsych/Psychology — FAM validated in mTBI: CBT with fear-reduction and graduated activity resumption is the mechanistically indicated intervention; MI to address avoidance should be an explicit treatment target.

9. MEDIUM — Neuroendocrine review 2025: acute illness confounds pituitary diagnosis — true post-TBI hypopituitarism requires testing at 3–6 months

PMID 40592688

Best Practice & Research Clinical Endocrinology & Metabolism · May 2025 T07

Key outcomes: Post-TBI hypopituitarism prevalence 5–70%; acute illness independently alters hypothalamic-pituitary axis; adrenal insufficiency and ADH deficiency are life-threatening; GHD and gonadotropin deficiency most common long-term deficits; testing recommended at 3–6 months.

Methods: Narrative/systematic review; pathophysiology, diagnostic algorithms, timing, and clinical significance of neuroendocrine dysfunction in acute and chronic TBI phases.

Rehab take: Screen for unexplained fatigue, poor rehab progress, hyponatremia, hypoglycemia, cold intolerance, low libido: send AM cortisol, TSH, IGF-1, LH/FSH, testosterone/estradiol; defer dynamic testing to ≥3 months post-injury; refer to endocrinology for abnormal results.

10. MEDIUM — Pilot RCT (N=10): high-intensity treadmill gait training significantly improves endurance vs low-intensity PT in TBI over 4 weeks

PMID 39882311

Neurotrauma Reports · Jan 2025 T11

Key outcomes: HIGT group (n=5) significantly improved 6-minute walk test vs control (n=5); trend toward improved 10-m walk gait speed; both groups significantly improved MoCA at 4 weeks and 1-month follow-up; no adverse events in HIGT.

Methods: Pilot RCT; N=10 adults with TBI (18–65 years); HIGT vs low-intensity PT; 3 sessions/week × 4 weeks (1 hr each); 10-m walk, 6-min walk, BBS, five-times sit-to-stand, TUG, cognitive TUG, MoCA assessed at baseline, 2 weeks, 4 weeks, 1-month follow-up.

Rehab take: HIGT (3×/week, 1 hr, 4 weeks) feasible and safe at pilot scale; endurance gains significant vs low-intensity PT; MoCA improved in both groups supporting aerobic exercise as cognitive rehab adjunct.

🦿 PT — HIGT significantly improved 6-min walk; recommend 3×/week intensity-matched treadmill protocol for ambulatory TBI patients; track BBS, TUG, and 6-min walk as primary progress measures.

🧠 Neuropsych/Psychology — MoCA improved in both exercise groups; aerobic exercise is a cognitive rehab adjunct — reinforce exercise adherence regardless of PT modality.

11. MEDIUM — OT systematic review: vocational/work-hardening rehabilitation + multidisciplinary team yields highest return-to-work rates after TBI

PMID 39973647

Archives of Physical Medicine and Rehabilitation · Apr 2025 T09, T12

Key outcomes: Most effective RTW interventions: vocational/work-hardening rehab, multidisciplinary model, cognitive-mental health focused interventions, and MOHO; multidisciplinary approaches consistently outperformed unimodal; baseline RTW rate in TBI ~40%.

Methods: Systematic review; OT-led RTW interventions in adults with TBI; studies evaluated for intervention type, team composition, and RTW outcomes.

Rehab take: Initiate vocational goal-setting at IRF admission; order co-evaluation (OT + neuropsychology + vocational rehabilitation counselor) for all RTW-goal patients; multidisciplinary RTW planning from admission outperforms deferred referral.

✋ OT — MOHO-based work simulation and community integration planning are evidence-based; begin workplace accommodation assessment and cognitive skills inventory during inpatient stay.

🧠 Neuropsych/Psychology — Neuropsychological evaluation is a key RTW enabler; CBT targeting cognitive-mental health barriers to RTW is a recommended intervention component.

12. LOW — SLP educator survey (N=25): TBIBank Grand Rounds positively received as clinical education resource for cognitive-communication disorders

PMID 39883550

SLP Education Literature · 2025 T13

Key outcomes: 73% of TBI inpatients seen by SLP have cognitive-communication disorders; TBIBank Grand Rounds rated positively by 25 educators for content, design, and education utility; multimedia format supports training on discourse, memory, attention, and executive function.

Rehab take: TBIBank Grand Rounds is a free, validated SLP education resource; recommend for department-level SLP training and IDT orientation on cognitive-communication expectations for TBI inpatients.

🗣️ SLP — TBIBank Grand Rounds provides case-based education on cognitive-communication assessment and treatment planning; supports consistent IDT documentation vocabulary for discourse, memory, and executive function.
