## WEEKLY SPOTLIGHT

### 1. ⭐ HIGH — **ISICEM 2025 expert consensus: 6-step evidence-based roadmap for disorders of consciousness management in the ICU**

PMID [41396553](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41396553/)
*Intensive Care Medicine* · Dec 2025 | T03

**Key outcomes:** 18 international experts synthesized DoC evidence into a six-step practical roadmap: (1) identify DoC; (2) assess and treat confounders; (3) establish CRS-R/neuroimaging diagnosis; (4) prognosticate; (5) select pharmacologic/non-pharmacologic interventions; (6) communicate with family via shared decision-making. Coverage spans coma, unresponsive wakefulness syndrome (UWS), and minimally conscious state (MCS) from structural (TBI, stroke) and non-structural etiologies.
**Methods:** Expert consensus roundtable at 2025 International Symposium on Intensive Care and Emergency Medicine (ISICEM); 18 clinicians and researchers; systematic evidence synthesis covering DoC epidemiology, behavioral assessment (CRS-R), neuroimaging (MRI, fMRI, EEG), pharmacologic interventions (amantadine, zolpidem), and family communication frameworks.
**Rehab take:** At IRF admission for DoC patients: verify CRS-R documentation from referring ICU; confirm amantadine initiated (if no contraindication); ensure confounders (residual sedation, metabolic derangements, subclinical seizures) addressed prior to behavioral assessment; implement formal family communication protocol using shared decision-making language per step 6. This roadmap is the new standard for IRF DoC admission orders.
**Team take:**
  - 🦿 **PT** Steps 4–5 include early mobility and sensory stimulation as interventions; document CRS-R motor subscale and arousal responses during PT as behavioral data informing diagnosis and prognosis.
  - ✋ **OT** CRS-R visual tracking and oromotor subscales directly inform OT assessment domains; align OT evaluation timing with formal CRS-R administration schedule to capture best response.
  - 🗣️ **SLP** CRS-R communication subscale guides SLP; dysphagia screening and AAC assessment are step-3 priorities once reliable volitional motor response established; document oral motor and swallowing findings in CRS-R language.
  - 🩺 **Nursing** Nursing anchors steps 1–2 (DoC recognition, confounder identification) and step 6 (family communication); consistent CRS-R-informed nursing observation is the backbone of behavioral diagnosis — use shift-level behavioral tracking.
  - 🧠 **Neuropsych/Psychology** Step 6 emphasizes psychologist-facilitated family support and shared prognostic communication; psychology should be embedded in ICU-to-IRF transition conversations and provide longitudinal family adjustment support.

### 2. MEDIUM — **Long-term onabotulinumtoxinA for post-acquired brain injury spasticity: safe and effective over 3+ years (N=224)**

PMID [41527032](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41527032/)
*Toxins* · Jan 2026 | T06

**Key outcomes:** No unexpected adverse events over ≥3-year follow-up; repeated injections maintained goal attainment; early treatment initiation (<1 year post-injury) associated with better goal attainment scaling (GAS) outcomes; treatment discontinuation rate was low; cumulative dosing showed no novel safety signals.
**Methods:** Retrospective single-center cohort; N=224 post-acquired brain injury (primarily post-stroke; spasticity pathophysiology and botulinum toxin mechanism are shared with TBI and directly extrapolate); injection cycles tracked 2012–2023 with minimum 3-year follow-up; GAS and safety outcomes assessed per cycle.
**Rehab take:** Long-term onabotulinumtoxinA is safe for spasticity in brain injury survivors; initiate within first year; track GAS at each injection cycle as the primary functional outcome; coordinate with PT/OT for task-specific retraining in the post-injection window (weeks 2–6) to maximize functional benefit of each cycle.
**Team take:**
  - 🦿 **PT** Post-injection window (weeks 2–6) is optimal for stretching and gait retraining; document Modified Ashworth Scale (MAS) and ambulation outcomes pre/post each injection cycle.
  - ✋ **OT** Upper limb injection + OT task-specific practice: document pinch/grip strength, Fugl-Meyer upper extremity, and functional ADL task performance pre/post each injection cycle.

## MONTHLY HIGHLIGHTS

### 4. ⭐⭐ TOP 3 #1 — **Double-blind RCT (N=184): methylphenidate + amantadine combination significantly improves 6-month GOSE versus placebo in blunt TBI**

PMID [40619501](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40619501/)
*Acta Neurologica Belgica* · 2025 | T10

**Key outcomes:** GOSE significantly higher in dual-treatment group (6.22±2.06 vs 5.37±2.54, p=0.014); no significant difference in hospital or ICU LOS; no major adverse events reported with combination; functional benefit appeared most pronounced at long-term follow-up.
**Methods:** Double-blinded RCT; N=184 blunt TBI patients (mean age 40.63±17.46 years, 84.8% male, equal allocation to treatment vs placebo); amantadine 100 mg BID + methylphenidate 20 mg BID vs matched placebo; primary outcome GOSE at long-term follow-up; no statistically significant GCS severity stratification reported separately.
**Rehab take:** This is the first double-blinded RCT of dual-agent neurostimulation in TBI. Consider amantadine 100 mg BID + methylphenidate 20 mg BID for blunt TBI patients with prolonged recovery plateau; monitor for cardiac effects (amantadine: QTc prolongation, edema; methylphenidate: HR >110, BP elevation); document baseline and ongoing ABS scores before and after initiation.
**Team take:**
  - 🦿 **PT** GOSE improvement indicates better functional recovery trajectory; align PT session intensity with medication trial; document motor milestones (BBS, TUG, ambulation distance) as surrogate efficacy markers during trial period.
  - ✋ **OT** Higher GOSE correlates with better ADL independence and cognitive participation; track FIM-Cog and FIM-Motor during medication trial to capture functional trajectory.
  - 🩺 **Nursing** Monitor vitals and ABS score per shift during first 2 weeks of dual-therapy; document paradoxical agitation (would prompt reassessment); hold methylphenidate if HR >110 or new arrhythmia and notify physician.
  - 🧠 **Neuropsych/Psychology** Methylphenidate may accelerate information processing speed and attention; delay formal neuropsychological baseline testing ≥2 weeks after medication stabilization to avoid confounded scores.

### 5. ⭐⭐ TOP 3 #2 — **TRACK-TBI multicenter cohort: subacute RPQ score predicts 6-month persistent post-concussion symptoms, GOSE, and QOL in mTBI**

PMID [40168631](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40168631/)
*Neurology* · Apr 2025 | T01, T02

**Key outcomes:** Higher subacute RPQ (Rivermead Post-Concussion Questionnaire) total score strongly associated with persistent PCS, lower GOSE, and reduced QOLIBRI-OS at 6 months (all p<0.001); data from 18 US Level 1 trauma centers validates subacute symptom screening as a practical risk-stratification tool.
**Methods:** Secondary analysis of TRACK-TBI (Transforming Research and Clinical Knowledge in TBI) prospective multicenter cohort (2014–2018); 18 US Level 1 trauma centers; GCS 13–15 (mTBI); Rivermead Post-Concussion Questionnaire (RPQ), GOSE, and QOLIBRI-OS assessed subacutely and at 6-month follow-up.
**Rehab take:** Administer RPQ at subacute follow-up (2–4 weeks post-injury) and use score to risk-stratify mTBI patients; elevated RPQ should trigger proactive concussion clinic referral rather than generic reassurance and watchful waiting; this large multicenter dataset validates early multidisciplinary intervention for symptomatic mTBI.
**Team take:**
  - 🦿 **PT** Vestibular/balance RPQ items at subacute stage predict persistent vestibular deficits; flag high-RPQ patients for vestibular PT consult and proprioceptive evaluation at discharge or follow-up.
  - ✋ **OT** Cognitive RPQ items (concentration, memory) predict lower 6-month GOSE; incorporate cognitive compensatory strategy training from first outpatient contact for high-RPQ patients.
  - 🗣️ **SLP** Cognitive-linguistic symptoms (word-finding, comprehension, processing speed) co-occur with elevated RPQ; include cognitive-communication screening at subacute follow-up visit.
  - 🩺 **Nursing** Provide high-RPQ patients with written symptom diary and recovery timeline; document patient-reported symptom trajectory at each encounter to detect worsening early.
  - 🧠 **Neuropsych/Psychology** Emotional RPQ items (depression, irritability, anxiety) predict QOLIBRI-OS decline; early psychological screening and CBT/supportive therapy referral is indicated for high-risk patients at subacute follow-up.

### 6. ⭐ HIGH — **Updated amantadine meta-analysis (9 RCTs, N=1,182): DRS improvement confirmed; effect most robust in first 6 weeks post-TBI**

PMID [39906329](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39906329/)
*Frontiers in Neurology* · Jan 2025 | T10

**Key outcomes:** 9 RCTs, 1,182 total patients; amantadine doses 87.5–400 mg/day; pooled DRS improvement statistically significant vs placebo in acute-to-subacute window (≤6 weeks post-injury); GCS improvement trend supported; safety profile acceptable; effect attenuates beyond 6 weeks; findings most robust for moderate-severe TBI and DOC populations.
**Methods:** Systematic review and meta-analysis (updated through March 2024); PubMed, Cochrane, Embase searched; predominantly moderate-severe TBI and DOC populations; dose and timing heterogeneity analyzed; authors Félix et al.
**Rehab take:** Amantadine 100–200 mg BID should be initiated early in moderate-severe TBI and DOC — the 6-week post-injury window is the most evidence-supported interval for functional benefit; document DRS at admission and at 4-week mark to track objective medication response; review candidate list at IRF admission.
**Team take:**
  - 🦿 **PT** Serial FIM-Motor and BBS assessments during the amantadine trial window can objectify medication response; optimal PT session timing relative to dose peak may improve arousal for therapy participation.
  - 🩺 **Nursing** Monitor for amantadine side effects: livedo reticularis, pedal edema, orthostatic hypotension, paradoxical confusion; document ABS baseline before initiation and per shift during first week.

### 7. MEDIUM — **VNS observational cohort (N=102): adjunctive non-invasive vagus nerve stimulation reduces persistent post-concussion symptoms across cognitive, affective, vestibular, and somatic domains**

PMID [40937177](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40937177/)
*Frontiers in Neurology* · Aug 2025 | T02

**Key outcomes:** 34% of patients met responder criteria (≥30% reduction in ≥50% of assessed symptom domains); largest improvements in post-traumatic headache, difficulty concentrating, dizziness, and depression/sadness; no device-related adverse events; time from injury to treatment initiation did not significantly influence outcomes.
**Methods:** Single-center observational cohort; N=102 mTBI patients with persistent PCS receiving adjunctive nVNS as part of standard care; cognitive, affective, somatic, and vestibular symptom domains assessed; authors Ament M, Leonard E, Staats PS, Ingram NT.
**Rehab take:** nVNS is a low-risk adjunct for persistent multi-domain mTBI PCS unresponsive to standard concussion therapy; observational design limits causality — not ready for routine protocol change; reasonable to consider for refractory patients; document domain-specific symptom severity with RPQ or PCSI before and after trial.
**Team take:**
  - 🦿 **PT** Vestibular/dizziness domain response with nVNS supports integration with vestibular PT protocol; document symptom trajectory across PT sessions during nVNS trial.
  - 🗣️ **SLP** Concentration domain improvement has downstream benefit for cognitive-linguistic workload; SLP therapy progression may be better tolerated during nVNS trial.
  - 🧠 **Neuropsych/Psychology** Depression/sadness domain response noted (observational); nVNS does not replace psychological intervention but may lower barrier to behavioral therapy engagement.

### 8. MEDIUM — **Multicenter mTBI cohort (N=163): catastrophizing and fear-avoidance independently predict persistent post-concussion symptoms at 3 and 6 months**

PMID [40129898](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40129898/)
*Neurotrauma Reports* · Feb 2025 | T02, T08, T15

**Key outcomes:** Fear-avoidance model (FAM) components correlated significantly at 3 months post-mTBI (r=0.40–0.72, all p<0.01); catastrophizing and fear-avoidance behavior independently predicted PCS severity at 3 and 6 months; depressive symptoms co-varied with all FAM elements; biopsychosocial model validated in multicenter ED/neurology-based mTBI cohort.
**Methods:** Prospective, longitudinal, multicenter cohort; N=163 mTBI (GCS 13–15) recruited from ED and neurology departments within 2 weeks of injury; FAM elements (catastrophizing, fear-avoidance behavior, depression, PCS) assessed at 2 weeks, 3 months, 6 months; authors Hecker L, King S, Wijenberg M, Geusgens C, Stapert S, Verbunt J, Van Heugten C.
**Rehab take:** Screen for catastrophizing and fear-avoidance at 2-week post-mTBI follow-up; patients with elevated scores should receive psychology referral at that visit — not deferred; early CBT with graduated activity exposure is mechanistically supported by these findings.
**Team take:**
  - 🩺 **Nursing** Brief catastrophizing screen (e.g., TSK-TBI or BSI-18) at 2-week outpatient visit; avoid inadvertently reinforcing avoidance through overly protective messaging; flag elevated scores for psychology referral.
  - 🧠 **Neuropsych/Psychology** FAM validated in mTBI: CBT with fear-reduction and graduated activity resumption is the mechanistically indicated intervention; motivational interviewing (MI) to address activity avoidance should be an explicit treatment target.

### 9. MEDIUM — **Neuroendocrine review 2025: acute illness confounds pituitary diagnosis — true post-TBI hypopituitarism requires testing at 3–6 months**

PMID [40592688](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40592688/)
*Best Practice & Research Clinical Endocrinology & Metabolism* · May 2025 | T07

**Key outcomes:** Post-TBI hypopituitarism prevalence 5–70%; acute illness independently alters hypothalamic-pituitary axis function beyond structural damage; adrenal insufficiency and ADH deficiency are life-threatening if unrecognized; GHD and gonadotropin deficiency are most common long-term deficits; routine endocrine testing at 3–6 months post-TBI recommended; acute-phase hormone values are unreliable for chronic diagnosis.
**Methods:** Narrative/systematic review; comprehensive analysis of pathophysiology (mechanical damage, neuroinflammation, BBB disruption, autoimmunity), diagnostic algorithms, timing considerations, and clinical significance of neuroendocrine dysfunction in acute and chronic TBI phases; author Feldt-Rasmussen U et al.
**Rehab take:** Screen IRF patients with unexplained fatigue, poor rehab progress, hyponatremia, hypoglycemia, cold intolerance, or low libido: send AM cortisol, TSH, IGF-1, LH/FSH, testosterone/estradiol as initial screen; do not rely on acute-phase ICU values; defer dynamic stimulation testing to ≥3 months post-injury; refer to endocrinology for abnormal results.

### 10. MEDIUM — **Pilot RCT (N=10): high-intensity treadmill gait training significantly improves endurance vs low-intensity PT in TBI over 4 weeks**

PMID [39882311](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39882311/)
*Neurotrauma Reports* · Jan 2025 | T11

**Key outcomes:** HIGT group (n=5) significantly improved 6-minute walk test (endurance) vs low-intensity PT control (n=5); trend toward improved 10-m walk gait speed in HIGT; both groups significantly improved MoCA at 4 weeks and 1-month follow-up; no adverse events in HIGT group.
**Methods:** Pilot RCT; N=10 adults with TBI (18–65 years); HIGT (n=5) vs low-intensity PT control (n=5); 3 sessions/week × 4 weeks (1 hr each); 10-m walk test, 6-min walk, BBS, five-times sit-to-stand, TUG, cognitive TUG, MoCA; assessed at baseline, 2 weeks, 4 weeks, 1-month follow-up; authors Shick T, Perkins C, Paul A, Martinez M, Joyce J, Beach K, Swahlan J, Weppner J.
**Rehab take:** HIGT (3×/week, 1 hr, 4 weeks) is feasible and safe at pilot scale in TBI; endurance gains are significant vs low-intensity PT; MoCA improved in both groups, supporting aerobic exercise as cognitive rehabilitation adjunct; these findings justify an adequately powered RCT.
**Team take:**
  - 🦿 **PT** HIGT significantly improved 6-min walk (endurance); recommend 3×/week intensity-matched treadmill protocol for ambulatory TBI patients with cardiac safety; track BBS, TUG, and 6-min walk as primary progress measures.
  - 🧠 **Neuropsych/Psychology** MoCA improved in both exercise groups regardless of intensity; aerobic exercise is a cognitive rehabilitation adjunct — reinforce exercise adherence regardless of PT modality choice.

### 11. MEDIUM — **OT systematic review: vocational/work-hardening rehabilitation + multidisciplinary team yields highest return-to-work rates after TBI**

PMID [39973647](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39973647/)
*Archives of Physical Medicine and Rehabilitation* · Apr 2025 | T09, T12

**Key outcomes:** Most effective OT-led RTW interventions: vocational/work-hardening rehabilitation, multidisciplinary model (PM&R + OT + neuropsychology + vocational rehabilitation counselor), cognitive-mental health focused interventions, and Model of Occupational Self-Efficacy (MOHO); holistic multidisciplinary approaches consistently outperformed unimodal interventions; baseline RTW rate in TBI ~40%.
**Methods:** Systematic review; databases searched for OT-led RTW interventions in adults with TBI; studies evaluated for intervention type, team composition, and RTW outcomes; authors Mullins A, Scalise O, Carpio-Paez B, DeShaw V, Jennings K, Kitchens R, Hilton C, Mani K.
**Rehab take:** Initiate vocational goal-setting during IRF admission; order co-evaluation (OT + neuropsychology + vocational rehabilitation counselor) for all patients with RTW goals; multidisciplinary RTW planning from admission outperforms deferred referral to outpatient services.
**Team take:**
  - ✋ **OT** MOHO-based work simulation and community integration planning are evidence-based; begin workplace accommodation assessment and cognitive skills inventory during inpatient stay to inform RTW planning.
  - 🧠 **Neuropsych/Psychology** Neuropsychological evaluation is a key RTW enabler for determining cognitive work capacity; CBT targeting cognitive-mental health barriers to RTW (self-efficacy, depression, anxiety) is a recommended intervention.

### 12. LOW — **SLP educator survey (N=25): TBIBank Grand Rounds positively received as clinical education resource for cognitive-communication disorders**

PMID [39883550](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39883550/)
*Brain Injury / SLP Education literature* · 2025 | T13

**Key outcomes:** 73% of TBI rehabilitation inpatients seen by SLP have cognitive-communication disorders; TBIBank Grand Rounds rated positively by 25 educators for content quality, design, and clinical education utility; multimedia case-based format supports training on discourse, memory, attention, and executive function deficits post-TBI.
**Methods:** Online user survey; N=25 speech pathology educators; assessed design, content relevance, and clinical education utility of TBIBank Grand Rounds multimedia educational resource.
**Rehab take:** TBIBank Grand Rounds is a free, validated SLP education resource relevant for IDT training on cognitive-communication expectations for TBI inpatients; recommended for department-level SLP training sessions and orientation programs.
**Team take:**
  - 🗣️ **SLP** TBIBank Grand Rounds provides case-based education on cognitive-communication assessment and treatment planning; supports consistent cognitive-communication documentation vocabulary across IDT rounds — particularly for discourse, memory, and executive function.
