⭐⭐ TOP 3 #1 Best UE rehab strategies for hemiparesis: CIMT + trunk restraint leads systematic review evidence

PMID 42037657  |  Cardiology and Cardiovascular Medicine · 2025  |  S02 S09 S12

Key outcomes: CIMT + trunk restraint (less-affected arm constrained 4–6 h/day) ranked highest for FMA-UE and ARAT improvement among all interventions assessed. CIMT targets unilateral motor control and ADL independence; HABIT targets bimanual coordination. NMES and robotic-assisted rehab ranked as adjunct options.

Methods: Comprehensive literature review of SRs, RCTs, and clinical guidelines; interventions assessed: task-specific training, CIMT, NMES, mirror therapy, VR, bilateral arm training, pharmacological, and robotic-assisted rehab for post-stroke UE hemiparesis. (PMC13108678)

Rehab take: Endorse structured CIMT (4–6 h/day constraint) as first-line UE intervention; sequence to HABIT for bimanual task retraining; NMES adjunct when cortical drive is insufficient.

🦿 PT · CIMT lower extremity equivalents support constraint-based gait training; FES for foot drop as adjunct; document gait speed (10MWT) as CIMT progress metric.

✋ OT · CIMT + trunk restraint is the strongest UE evidence; transition to HABIT for bimanual ADL retraining per MAL goals; track ARAT at admission and discharge.

🩺 Nursing · Clarify which arm is constrained during CIMT periods; support ADL practice during constraint hours; document any skin issues under constraint device.

🧠 Psychology · High-repetition CIMT tasks can be motivationally demanding — use brief MI strategies (change talk, decisional balance) to sustain engagement with therapy schedule.

⭐⭐ TOP 3 #2 Swallowing rehab dosing: Higher intensity + higher frequency optimizes oral intake recovery in acute post-stroke dysphagia

PMID 40057664  |  Dysphagia · 2025 (Vol 40:5, pp. 1132–1144)  |  S04 S13 S14

Key outcomes: Patients ≥65 yr with post-stroke dysphagia receiving swallowing rehab within 3 days — High-intensity + High-frequency sessions in the first 7 days were associated with the best oral intake recovery and lowest dysphagia rate at discharge across all four dosing groups.

Methods: Retrospective cohort; Japanese DPC database (Apr 2020 – Mar 2021); patients stratified into 4 groups: long vs. short per-session time × high vs. low proportion of days treated; primary outcomes = dysphagia present at discharge + total oral intake recovery at discharge.

Rehab take: Order swallowing therapy within 3 days of admission; target daily sessions (high frequency) with adequate per-session duration (high intensity) for patients ≥65 with new post-stroke dysphagia; both dosing axes matter independently.

🗣️ SLP · Prescribe daily swallowing therapy rather than 3×/week when feasible; document session duration alongside frequency; dysphagia orders should specify both intensity (duration) and frequency (daily vs. QOD).

🩺 Nursing · Oral care Q4H, HOB ≥30°, aspiration monitoring between SLP sessions; family instruction on safe feeding techniques before any unsupervised meal.

⭐ HIGH Earlier BoNT-A timing may improve motor recovery trajectory in post-stroke spasticity

PMID 41295873  |  [Journal not confirmed at search time] · November 2025  |  S05 S11 S14

Key outcomes: Secondary analysis of multicenter longitudinal cohort; earlier first BoNT-A injection (subacute vs. chronic phase) associated with improved motor recovery outcomes in patients naïve to BoNT-A; functional benefits under ongoing investigation but recovery trajectory favors early treatment.

Methods: Secondary analysis of multicenter, open-label, longitudinal cohort study; post-stroke spasticity patients naïve to BoNT-A treatment; compares early vs. late timing of first injection on motor recovery course.

Rehab take: Initiate BoNT-A referral before chronic phase (target <3 months post-stroke); align injection timing with therapy intensification — maximum motor learning window is weeks 2–8 post-injection; coordinate spasticity management with PT/OT team.

🦿 PT · Intensify stretching and functional mobility training in weeks 2–8 after BoNT-A; document MAS scores pre/post injection.

✋ OT · Schedule serial casting, dynamic splinting, and hand/arm functional tasks within the tone-reduction window post-injection.

🩺 Nursing · Monitor for focal weakness at injection site; document Modified Ashworth Scale scores at each nursing assessment; reinforce positioning to prevent contracture.

⭐ HIGH 4 sessions of motivational interviewing reduces early post-stroke depression

PMID 40097963  |  BMC Psychiatry · March 2025  |  S06 S15

Key outcomes: MI intervention (1 face-to-face + 3 telephone sessions) effectively reduced depression scores compared to routine nursing care alone in patients with early PSD.

Methods: Parallel two-group quasi-experimental study; patients with early PSD recruited from neurology department, Southeast China; allocated by ward to MI intervention or control (routine nursing + standard follow-up). (PMC11917151)

Rehab take: Brief structured MI (4 sessions) is feasible and effective for PSD; consider embedding MI brief intervention training for nursing staff to extend reach beyond psychology sessions.

🧠 Psychology · Protocol transferable to IRF: 1 structured in-person session + 3 brief telephone follow-ups; targets ambivalence about recovery, ADL engagement, and medication adherence.

🩺 Nursing · Core MI skills (OARS: open questions, affirmations, reflective listening, summaries) can reinforce psychological gains between formal psychology contacts; screen for PSD with PHQ-2 at admission.

⭐⭐ TOP 3 #3 MI + home rehab RCT: PHQ-9 drops 7.3 pts, FSS drops 10.7 pts, Barthel gains 10 pts

PMID 39610664  |  International Journal of Nursing Studies Advances · 2024 (online Oct 2024; indexed 2025)  |  S06 S14 S15

Key outcomes: PHQ-9: 9.1 → 1.8 (Δ −7.3, p<0.001); Fatigue Severity Scale: 28.5 → 17.8 (Δ −10.7, p<0.001); Barthel Index: 58.8 → 68.8 (Δ +10.0, p<0.001) — all significantly superior to standard care.

Methods: RCT; Vietnam National Geriatric Hospital, 2021–2022; N=92 stroke survivors (46 intervention, 46 control); ages >45; intervention = MI + structured home-based rehabilitation; control = standard care. (PMC11602558)

Rehab take: Combined MI + structured home exercise at discharge produces clinically meaningful gains in depression, fatigue, and functional independence; discharge planning at MFB IRF should incorporate written home rehab protocol + brief MI session.

🧠 Psychology · MI is a core component of comprehensive recovery; 10-pt Barthel gain shows functional recovery couples with psychological wellbeing — depression management IS functional rehabilitation.

🦿 PT · Prescribe written structured home exercise protocol; outcomes data shows functional gains achievable post-IRF with home-based reinforcement; caregiver education is essential.

✋ OT · Home ADL retraining component; prescribe home modification checklist and caregiver instruction; OT home visit referral if indicated.

🩺 Nursing · Family education on home rehab compliance; medication adherence for antidepressants; teach caregiver early warning signs of PSD relapse.

⭐ HIGH Post-thrombectomy patients face 25% higher recurrent stroke risk beyond 90 days (N=3,934 real-world)

PMC 12074927 (PMID not confirmed at search time)  |  Frontiers in Neurology · April 30, 2025  |  S01 S07

Key outcomes: Thrombectomy patients: recurrent stroke 63.3% vs. 53.1% controls (HR 1.25); 2-year stroke/palliative-free survival 36.6% vs. 45.8%; median follow-up 775 days.

Methods: Real-world analysis; Oracle Real-World Data (139 health systems, ~1.4 billion encounters); Jan 2016 – Jan 2023; N=3,934 thrombectomy patients vs. 3,934 propensity-matched controls; outcomes tracked >90 days post-index admission. DOI: 10.3389/fneur.2025.1543101

Rehab take: Post-thrombectomy patients require aggressive secondary prevention at IRF: confirm BP <130/80, antiplatelet/anticoagulation, AF management, lipid therapy; long-term recurrence risk should be explicit in discharge planning conversations.

🩺 Nursing · Secondary prevention teaching for post-thrombectomy patients: BP self-monitoring, medication adherence, stroke symptom recognition (F.A.S.T.), and risk factor modification education before discharge.

⭐ HIGH tDCS leads emerging PSCI therapies: MoCA gain +4.56 pts across 22 RCTs, 5,100 patients

PMC 12150855 (PMID not confirmed at search time)  |  Frontiers in Neurology · May 27, 2025  |  S06 S15

Key outcomes: tDCS most effective novel PSCI intervention (MoCA MD +4.56, 95% CI 3.19–5.93); pharmacological interventions also effective; 22 RCTs show meaningful but modest overall benefit; cognitive impairments persist in 30–70% of survivors despite current treatments.

Methods: SR, PRISMA/PROSPERO (CRD42024621445); PubMed + EMBASE; RCTs past 5 years with MoCA primary outcome; 755 articles identified → 22 RCTs, 5,100 participants. (Kreiger K, Weiss E, Fluri F; DOI: 10.3389/fneur.2025.1569329)

Rehab take: MoCA is the appropriate cognitive tracking tool at IRF admission and discharge; consider tDCS referral for patients with documented PSCI where available.

🧠 Psychology · tDCS + cognitive training is emerging evidence-based option; SR supports MoCA tracking at admission, mid-stay, and discharge; individualize cognitive rehab goals by MoCA domain deficits.

🩺 Nursing · Administer MoCA at admission per protocol; flag scores <26 for formal neuropsychological consult; monitor safety implications of cognitive impairment (falls, medication management).

⭐ HIGH Dysphagia recovery risk stratification: 156,000-patient meta-analysis identifies 6 unfavorable predictors

PMID 40241863  |  [Journal not confirmed at search time] · 2025  |  S04 S13 S14

Key outcomes: Dysphagia recovery trajectory: 13.53% at 1 week → 95% at 6 months. Unfavorable predictors: age ≥70, low BMI, bilateral stroke, high NIHSS score, tracheal intubation, aspiration. (PMC11997685)

Methods: SR/MA; 34 studies, N=156,309 patients; databases: CNKI, CBMdisc, VIP, WanFang, PubMed, Embase, CINAHL, Web of Science, Cochrane, Scopus; Jan 2013 – Dec 2023; NOS + PROBAST quality assessment; RevMan5.3 + Stata15.1.

Rehab take: Apply admission risk stratification: bilateral stroke + high NIHSS + age ≥70 + aspiration = highest risk for prolonged dysphagia → plan early for enteral nutrition, PEG consultation, and intensive SLP dosing.

🗣️ SLP · Build admission dysphagia risk checklist: age ≥70, bilateral lesion, high NIHSS, tracheal intubation history, observed aspiration → triggers intensive daily protocol and early tube feeding discussion.

🩺 Nursing · Monitor BMI weekly; aspiration precautions for high-risk patients; document coughing/wet voice quality after meals; HOB ≥30° during and 30 min after feeding.

MEDIUM Antihypertensive SR/MA: ARB+diuretic or ACEi+CCB best for secondary stroke prevention; target <130/80 mmHg

PMID 39780283  |  European Journal of Medical Research · January 9, 2025  |  S07 S14

Key outcomes: Antihypertensive therapy significantly reduces stroke recurrence, CV deaths, and MI vs. placebo; ARB+thiazide and ACEi+CCB ranked as most effective drug combinations; BP target <130/80 mmHg per 2025 AHA/JSH guidelines. (PMC11715515; DOI: 10.1186/s40001-024-02226-3)

Methods: SR/MA of RCTs; PubMed, Embase, Cochrane, Web of Science searched through Jan 2024.

Rehab take: Confirm antihypertensive class/combination at IRF admission; prefer ARB+thiazide or ACEi+CCB; document BP and confirm target <130/80 mmHg.

🩺 Nursing · Daily BP monitoring ×2; teach target <130/80; patient education on home BP self-monitoring and medication adherence; document BP trends for physician review.

MEDIUM ICH management 2025: 5 pivotal advances — BP <140, hemostasis evidence, reversal, surgery thresholds

PMID 40235043  |  Annals of Indian Academy of Neurology · March 2025  |  S08

Key outcomes: (1) Hematoma expansion prediction via imaging biomarkers; (2) BP target <140 mmHg (INTERACT3 goal-directed bundle improves outcomes); (3) rFVIIa: modest hematoma reduction but NO functional benefit + ↑ thrombotic events (FAST RCT); (4) Anticoagulation reversal = urgent priority; (5) Surgical thresholds evolving — minimally invasive gaining ground. Ongoing trials: INTRINSIC, TICH-3, FASTEST, TIME-ICH. (PMC12049219)

Methods: Narrative update review; Phung JYH, Yogendrakumar V, Dowlatshahi D.

Rehab take: ICH patients at IRF: confirm BP target maintained <140 mmHg; document anticoagulation reversal status; confirm surgical intervention details from acute care records.

MEDIUM Neuropsychiatric triad 3–4 years post-stroke: PSF + PSD dominate and drive ADL dependency

PMC 12230960 (PMID not confirmed at search time)  |  BMJ Open · July 6, 2025  |  S06 S15

Key outcomes: PSF, PSD, and PSCI all highly prevalent at 3–4 years post-stroke; PSF and PSD overlap and correlate; PSF independently associated with ADL dependency and worse HRQoL; FAS (fatigue), PHQ-9 (depression), MoCA (cognition) used as primary outcome measures. (Aked J et al.; DOI: 10.1136/bmjopen-2024-096908)

Methods: Population-based cohort; Swedish university hospital catchment area; N=202 first-ever stroke survivors; median age 72, 40% female; assessed at clinic, home visit, or telephone.

Rehab take: PSF is the dominant driver of long-term ADL dependency — address fatigue via energy conservation and pacing training; do not rely on depression screening alone to identify the neuropsychiatric triad.

🧠 Psychology · PSF-PSD co-occurrence is the rule, not the exception; assess fatigue (FAS or FSS) at admission alongside PHQ-9; integrated behavioral treatment for fatigue-depression dyad; counsel patients on realistic 3–4 year recovery trajectory.

MEDIUM AF stroke prevention 2025: DOAC for virtually all patients; ABC pathway; LAA occlusion for anticoag-intolerant

PMID 40481780  |  European Journal of Clinical Investigation · June 7, 2025  |  S07 S10 S14

Key outcomes: ABC pathway reduces mortality, stroke recurrence, and bleeding; DOACs for all AF patients unless mechanical valve/mod-severe MS/ESRD; LAA occlusion viable for anticoag-unsuitable; 4S-AF framework guides individualized treatment; digital health tools enable early AF detection. (Askarinejad A, Lane DA, Lip GYH; PMC12362057; DOI: 10.1111/eci.70082)

Rehab take: Confirm DOAC initiated in all post-stroke AF patients at IRF admission; screen for undiagnosed AF if cardioembolic etiology suspected; document LAA occlusion candidacy for anticoag-intolerant patients.

🩺 Nursing · DOAC adherence counseling: correct timing, dose, no missed doses; daily monitoring for bleeding signs; patient education on AF symptoms and emergency response.

MEDIUM Aphasia treatment in linguistically diverse populations: equity gap demands adapted protocols and certified interpreters

PMID 40893850  |  [Journal not confirmed at search time] · 2025  |  S03 S13

Key outcomes: Significant equity gaps in aphasia treatment quality/accessibility for linguistically diverse patients; standard assessment and treatment protocols insufficiently adapted; highlights need for bilingual SLP resources, culturally adapted assessments, and policy changes.

Methods: Narrative review/perspective on current and future directions; synthesis of existing literature on aphasia treatment in diverse language populations.

🗣️ SLP · Screen all patients for language background at admission; use culturally adapted aphasia assessments (BESA, BNT adaptations) for non-English-dominant patients; medical certified interpreters required — family members are not adequate substitutes for aphasia assessments.

MEDIUM Early multidisciplinary "brain-heart manager" rehab improves functional + psychosocial outcomes in hemorrhagic stroke (N=216)

PMC 12675196 (PMID not confirmed at search time)  |  Frontiers in Neurology · November 2025  |  S08 S11

Key outcomes: Intervention group (n=108) receiving structured multidimensional rehab directed by certified brain-heart health managers showed significantly better functional, psychological, and QoL outcomes vs. standard care controls (n=108). (Shi Y, Yan L, Yang M; DOI: 10.3389/fneur.2025.1683579)

Methods: Retrospective cohort; single center (Second People's Hospital of Hunan Province); 216 consecutive ICH patients; intervention = standard care + psychological support + individualized PT + nutritional counseling + continuous care coordination.

Rehab take: Comprehensive team-based rehab for hemorrhagic stroke beyond standard care aligns with MFB IRF IDT model; psychological support must begin day 1, not deferred.

🦿 PT · Early mobilization and individualized physical rehab from day 1 of IRF admission for ICH patients; document daily mobility targets.

🧠 Psychology · Proactive psychological support from IRF admission day 1 for all ICH patients; early adjustment disorder and catastrophizing respond to CBT-based approaches.

LOW CIMT activates contralesional red nucleus plasticity — mechanistic evidence from rat large-area stroke model

PMC 11955289  |  Behavioural Neurology · March 23, 2025  |  S09

Key outcomes: CIMT promoted motor recovery; ↑ GAP43 + PSD95 in contralesional (not ipsilesional) red nucleus; ↑ bilateral motor cortex-to-red nucleus projections; mechanism = compensatory subcortical plasticity via contralesional pathway. (Liu P et al.; DOI: 10.1155/bn/3631524)

Methods: Animal study (rat large-area stroke model); CIMT vs. no-CIMT group; immunohistochemistry for neuroplasticity markers GAP43 and PSD95.

⚠️ Preclinical animal model — mechanistic context only; no direct clinical application at this time.

LOW rTMS + CIMT combined for post-stroke movement disorders: synergistic theory, minimal clinical RCT data

PMC 12009840  |  Frontiers in Human Neuroscience · April 7, 2025  |  S09 S11

Key outcomes: Narrative review: rTMS primes cortical excitability → CIMT capitalizes on enhanced neuroplasticity window; both modalities separately improve motor outcomes; combined clinical RCT evidence limited to date. (Liu Z et al.; DOI: 10.3389/fnhum.2025.1578258)

Methods: Narrative review without meta-analysis.

⚠️ Narrative review — clinical translation pending adequately powered RCTs.

🦿 PT · Watch for emerging RCTs on rTMS+CIMT combinations; conceptually supports scheduling CIMT sessions in the hours following any rTMS protocol if patient receives both.
