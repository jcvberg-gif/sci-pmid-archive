### 1. ⭐⭐ TOP 3 #1 — **Best UE rehab strategies for hemiparesis: CIMT + trunk restraint leads systematic review evidence**
PMID [42037657](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42037657/)
*Cardiology and Cardiovascular Medicine* · 2025 | S02 · S09 · S12

**Key outcomes:** CIMT + trunk restraint (less-affected arm constrained 4–6 h/day) ranked highest for FMA-UE and ARAT improvement among all interventions assessed; CIMT achieves unilateral motor control and ADL independence; HABIT targets bimanual coordination. NMES and robotic-assisted rehab ranked as adjunct options.
**Methods:** Comprehensive literature review of SR, RCTs, and clinical guidelines; interventions assessed: task-specific training, CIMT, NMES, mirror therapy, VR, bilateral arm training, pharmacological, and robotic-assisted rehab for post-stroke UE hemiparesis. (PMC13108678)
**Rehab take:** Endorse structured CIMT (4–6 h constraint/day) as first-line UE intervention for IRF patients with sufficient motor activity in the affected arm; sequence HABIT for bimanual task retraining; NMES adjunct when cortical drive insufficient.
**Team take:**
  - 🦿 **PT** · CIMT principles applicable to lower extremity constraint-based gait; FES for foot drop as adjunct; document gait speed (10MWT) as CIMT progress metric.
  - ✋ **OT** · CIMT + trunk restraint is the strongest evidence for hemi-UE recovery; transition to HABIT for bimanual ADL tasks per MAL functional goals; track ARAT at admission and discharge.
  - 🩺 **Nursing** · Clarify which arm is constrained during CIMT hours to prevent staff confusion; support unsupervised ADL practice during constraint periods.
  - 🧠 **Psychology** · High CIMT task repetition can be motivationally demanding — use brief MI strategies (change talk, decisional balance) to sustain engagement with therapy schedule.

### 2. ⭐⭐ TOP 3 #2 — **Swallowing rehab dosing: Higher intensity + higher frequency optimizes oral intake recovery in acute post-stroke dysphagia**
PMID [40057664](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40057664/)
*Dysphagia* · 2025 (Vol 40, No. 5, pp. 1132–1144; online March 8, 2025) | S04 · S13 · S14

**Key outcomes:** Patients ≥65 years with post-stroke dysphagia receiving swallowing rehab within 3 days of admission — high-intensity + high-frequency sessions in the first 7 days were associated with best oral intake recovery and lowest dysphagia rate at discharge across all four dosing groups.
**Methods:** Retrospective cohort; Japanese Diagnosis Procedure Combination database, Apr 2020 – Mar 2021; acute-care hospitals; patients stratified into 4 groups: long vs. short per-session time (intensity) × high vs. low proportion of days (frequency); primary outcomes = dysphagia present at discharge + total oral intake recovery at discharge.
**Rehab take:** Order swallowing therapy within 3 days of admission; target daily sessions (high frequency) with adequate per-session duration (high intensity) for all patients ≥65 with new post-stroke dysphagia; both axes of dosing matter independently.
**Team take:**
  - 🗣️ **SLP** · Prescribe daily swallowing therapy rather than 3×/week when feasible; document session duration alongside frequency; dysphagia order should specify both intensity (duration goal) and frequency (daily vs. QOD).
  - 🩺 **Nursing** · Oral care Q4H, head-of-bed elevation ≥30°, and vigilant aspiration monitoring between SLP sessions; family instruction on safe feeding techniques before any unsupervised meals.

### 3. ⭐ HIGH — **Earlier BoNT-A timing may improve motor recovery trajectory in post-stroke spasticity**
PMID [41295873](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41295873/)
*[Journal not confirmed at search time]* · November 2025 | S05 · S11 · S14

**Key outcomes:** Secondary analysis of multicenter longitudinal cohort; earlier first BoNT-A injection (subacute vs. chronic phase) associated with improved motor recovery outcomes in naïve post-stroke spasticity patients; authors note functional benefits remain under investigation but trajectory data favors early treatment.
**Methods:** Secondary analysis of multicenter, open-label, longitudinal cohort study; patients naïve to BoNT-A; investigates whether timing of first BoNT-A injection influences post-stroke motor recovery course; comparison of early vs. late injection cohorts.
**Rehab take:** Initiate BoNT-A referral before chronic phase (target <3 months post-stroke); align injection scheduling with therapy intensification — maximum motor learning window is weeks 2–8 post-injection; coordinate spasticity management with PT/OT team.
**Team take:**
  - 🦿 **PT** · Intensify stretching and functional mobility training in the 2–8 weeks after BoNT-A injection; document MAS scores pre/post injection as objective metric.
  - ✋ **OT** · Schedule serial casting, dynamic splinting, and hand/arm functional tasks to capitalize on the tone-reduction window post-injection.
  - 🩺 **Nursing** · Monitor for focal weakness at injection site; document spasticity on Modified Ashworth Scale at each nursing assessment; reinforce positioning to prevent contracture.

### 4. ⭐ HIGH — **4 sessions of motivational interviewing reduces early post-stroke depression**
PMID [40097963](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40097963/)
*BMC Psychiatry* · March 2025 | S06 · S15

**Key outcomes:** MI intervention (1 face-to-face + 3 telephone sessions) effectively reduced depression scores compared to routine nursing care alone in patients with early post-stroke depression (PSD).
**Methods:** Parallel two-group quasi-experimental study; patients with early PSD recruited from neurology department, Southeast China; allocated by ward to MI intervention group or control (routine nursing + standard follow-up). (PMC11917151)
**Rehab take:** Brief structured MI (4 sessions) is feasible and effective for PSD during and after acute hospitalization; consider embedding MI brief intervention training in nursing staff education to extend reach beyond psychology sessions.
**Team take:**
  - 🧠 **Psychology** · Protocol is directly transferable to IRF: 1 structured in-person session + 3 brief telephone follow-ups; targets ambivalence about recovery, ADL engagement, and medication adherence.
  - 🩺 **Nursing** · Nurses trained in core MI components (empathy, OARS: open questions, affirmations, reflective listening, summaries) can reinforce psychological gains between formal psychology contacts; screen for PSD with PHQ-2 at admission.

### 5. ⭐⭐ TOP 3 #3 — **MI + home rehab RCT: PHQ-9 drops 7.3 points, FSS drops 10.7 points, Barthel gains 10 points**
PMID [39610664](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39610664/)
*International Journal of Nursing Studies Advances* · 2024 (online Oct 2024; indexed 2025) | S06 · S14 · S15

**Key outcomes:** PHQ-9: 9.1 → 1.8 (p<0.001, Δ −7.3); Fatigue Severity Scale: 28.5 → 17.8 (p<0.001, Δ −10.7); Barthel Index: 58.8 → 68.8 (p<0.001, Δ +10.0) — all significantly superior to standard care.
**Methods:** RCT; Vietnam National Geriatric Hospital, 2021–2022; N=92 stroke survivors (46 intervention, 46 control); ages >45, stroke diagnosis confirmed; intervention = MI + structured home-based rehabilitation; control = standard care. (PMC11602558)
**Rehab take:** Combined MI + structured home exercise protocol at discharge produces clinically meaningful gains in depression, fatigue, and functional independence; discharge planning at MFB IRF should incorporate written home rehab protocol and brief MI session before discharge.
**Team take:**
  - 🧠 **Psychology** · This RCT confirms MI as a core component of comprehensive post-stroke recovery; 10-point Barthel gain shows functional recovery couples with psychological wellbeing — counsel team that depression management IS functional rehabilitation.
  - 🦿 **PT** · Prescribe written structured home exercise protocol before discharge; outcomes data shows functional gains are achievable post-IRF with home-based reinforcement; caregiver education is essential.
  - ✋ **OT** · Home ADL retraining component included; prescribe home modification checklist and caregiver instruction; OT home visit referral if indicated.
  - 🩺 **Nursing** · Family education on home rehab compliance; medication adherence monitoring for any antidepressants initiated during IRF stay; teach caregiver early warning signs of PSD relapse.

### 6. ⭐ HIGH — **Post-thrombectomy patients face 25% higher recurrent stroke risk beyond 90 days in real-world N=3,934**
PMC [12074927](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12074927/) *(PMID not confirmed at search time)*
*Frontiers in Neurology* · April 30, 2025 | S01 · S07

**Key outcomes:** Thrombectomy patients: recurrent stroke 63.3% vs. 53.1% controls (HR 1.25; 95% CI reported); 2-year stroke/palliative-free survival 36.6% vs. 45.8%; median follow-up 775 days post-admission.
**Methods:** Real-world analysis; Oracle Real-World Data (139 health systems, ~1.4 billion encounters); Jan 2016 – Jan 2023; N=3,934 acute ischemic stroke thrombectomy patients vs. 3,934 propensity-matched controls; outcomes tracked >90 days post-index admission. DOI: 10.3389/fneur.2025.1543101
**Rehab take:** Post-thrombectomy patients require aggressive secondary prevention at IRF: confirm BP target (<130/80), antiplatelet/anticoagulation status, AF management, lipid therapy; long-term recurrence risk should inform discharge planning conversations.
**Team take:**
  - 🩺 **Nursing** · Secondary prevention teaching for post-thrombectomy patients is high-priority: BP self-monitoring, medication adherence, stroke symptom recognition (F.A.S.T.), and risk factor modification education before discharge.

### 7. ⭐ HIGH — **tDCS leads emerging PSCI therapies: MoCA gain of 4.56 points across 22 RCTs, 5,100 patients**
PMC [12150855](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12150855/) *(PMID not confirmed at search time)*
*Frontiers in Neurology* · May 27, 2025 | S06 · S15

**Key outcomes:** tDCS most effective novel PSCI intervention (MoCA MD +4.56, 95% CI 3.19–5.93); pharmacological interventions also showed effectiveness; existing treatments across 22 RCTs show meaningful but modest benefits; long-term cognitive impairments persist in 30–70% of survivors despite advances.
**Methods:** SR; PRISMA/PROSPERO registered (CRD42024621445); PubMed + EMBASE; RCTs from past 5 years using MoCA as primary outcome measure; 755 articles identified → 22 RCTs, 5,100 participants included. DOI: 10.3389/fneur.2025.1569329
**Rehab take:** MoCA is the appropriate standardized cognitive tracking tool at IRF admission and discharge; consider tDCS referral for patients with documented PSCI where available; pharmacological options remain an option for moderate-severe PSCI.
**Team take:**
  - 🧠 **Psychology** · tDCS combined with cognitive training is emerging evidence-based option for PSCI; this SR supports MoCA tracking at admission, mid-stay, and discharge; individualized cognitive rehab goals based on MoCA domain deficits.
  - 🩺 **Nursing** · Administer MoCA on admission per protocol; flag patients with scores <26 for formal neuropsychological assessment; monitor for safety implications of cognitive impairment (falls, medication management).

### 8. ⭐ HIGH — **Dysphagia recovery risk stratification: 156,000-patient meta-analysis identifies 6 unfavorable predictors**
PMID [40241863](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40241863/)
*[Journal not confirmed at search time — dysphagia/nursing focus]* · 2025 | S04 · S13 · S14

**Key outcomes:** Dysphagia recovery trajectory: 13.53% at 1 week → 95% at 6 months; unfavorable predictors identified: age ≥70, low BMI, bilateral stroke, high NIHSS score, tracheal intubation, aspiration. Recovery rate driven by combination of predictors.
**Methods:** SR/MA; 34 studies, N=156,309 patients with post-stroke dysphagia; databases searched: CNKI, CBMdisc, VIP, WanFang, PubMed, Embase, CINAHL, Web of Science, Cochrane, Scopus; retrieval period Jan 2013 – Dec 2023; quality assessed with NOS and PROBAST; meta-analysis via RevMan5.3 + Stata15.1. (PMC11997685)
**Rehab take:** Apply admission risk stratification: bilateral stroke + high NIHSS + age ≥70 + documented aspiration = highest risk for prolonged dysphagia → early planning for enteral nutrition, PEG consultation, and intensive SLP dosing; track BMI trajectory.
**Team take:**
  - 🗣️ **SLP** · Build admission dysphagia risk checklist from this evidence base: flag age ≥70, bilateral lesion, high NIHSS, tracheal intubation history, observed aspiration → triggers intensive daily protocol and early tube feeding discussion.
  - 🩺 **Nursing** · Monitor BMI weekly; aspiration precautions for high-risk patients; document coughing/wet voice quality after meals; elevate HOB ≥30° during and 30 min after feeding; reinforce aspiration precautions with all staff caring for the patient.

### 9. MEDIUM — **Antihypertensive SR/MA: ARB + diuretic or ACEi + CCB best combinations for secondary stroke prevention; target <130/80 mmHg**
PMID [39780283](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39780283/)
*European Journal of Medical Research* · January 9, 2025 | S07 · S14

**Key outcomes:** Antihypertensive therapy significantly reduces stroke recurrence, CV deaths, and MI vs. placebo/no treatment; ARB+thiazide/thiazide-like diuretic and ACEi+CCB ranked as most effective combinations; BP target <130/80 mmHg per 2025 AHA and JSH guidelines; ACEi, ARB, CCB, and diuretics all superior to placebo.
**Methods:** SR/MA of RCTs; PubMed, Embase, Cochrane Central, Web of Science; searched through January 12, 2024; identified RCTs comparing antihypertensive drugs to control or intensive vs. standard BP control for secondary stroke prevention. (PMC11715515; DOI: 10.1186/s40001-024-02226-3)
**Rehab take:** Confirm antihypertensive class/combination at IRF admission; prefer ARB+thiazide or ACEi+CCB; document BP at admission and confirm target <130/80 mmHg; address any uncontrolled BP as discharge barrier.
**Team take:**
  - 🩺 **Nursing** · Daily BP monitoring ×2 (morning and evening); patient education on home BP self-monitoring; teach target of <130/80 and importance of medication adherence; document BP trends in nursing notes for physician review.

### 10. MEDIUM — **ICH management 2025 update: 5 pivotal advances — BP target <140, hemostasis evidence, reversal, and surgery thresholds**
PMID [40235043](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40235043/)
*Annals of Indian Academy of Neurology* · March 2025 | S08

**Key outcomes:** (1) Hematoma expansion prediction via imaging biomarkers; (2) BP target <140 mmHg (goal-directed bundle per INTERACT3 improves outcomes); (3) rFVIIa modest hematoma reduction but NO functional benefit + ↑ thrombotic events (FAST RCT); (4) Anticoagulation reversal is urgent priority; (5) Surgical thresholds evolving — minimally invasive approaches gaining ground. Ongoing trials: INTRINSIC, TICH-3, FASTEST, TIME-ICH.

**Rehab take:** ICH patients admitted to IRF: confirm BP target maintained <140 mmHg; document anticoagulation reversal status; confirm surgical intervention/decision from acute care records; watch for late hematoma expansion signs.

### 11. MEDIUM — **Neuropsychiatric triad 3–4 years post-stroke: PSF + PSD dominate, drive ADL dependency, and are grossly underaddressed**
PMC [12230960](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12230960/) *(PMID not confirmed at search time)*
*BMJ Open* · July 6, 2025 | S06 · S15

**Key outcomes:** PSF, PSD, and PSCI all highly prevalent at 3–4 years post-stroke in population-based cohort; PSF and PSD overlap and correlate; PSF independently associated with ADL dependency and worse HRQoL; authors used FAS (fatigue), PHQ-9 (depression), MoCA (cognition) as primary outcome measures.
**Methods:** Population-based cohort; catchment area of Swedish university hospital; N=202 first-ever stroke survivors; median age 72 (IQR 65–79), 40% female; assessed in clinic, via home visit, or telephone; DOI: 10.1136/bmjopen-2024-096908
**Rehab take:** At IRF, PSF is the dominant driver of long-term ADL dependency — address fatigue via energy conservation, activity pacing training, and caregiver education; do not rely on depression screening alone to identify the neuropsychiatric triad.
**Team take:**
  - 🧠 **Psychology** · PSF-PSD co-occurrence is the rule, not the exception; assess fatigue (FAS or FSS) at admission alongside PHQ-9; IBT (integrated behavioral treatment targeting fatigue and depression together) is appropriate for most stroke survivors at IRF; counsel patients on realistic 3–4 year recovery trajectory.

### 12. MEDIUM — **AF stroke prevention 2025: DOAC for virtually all patients; ABC pathway + 4S-AF framework; LAA occlusion for anticoag-intolerant**
PMID [40481780](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40481780/)
*European Journal of Clinical Investigation* · June 7, 2025 | S07 · S10 · S14

**Key outcomes:** ABC pathway (Atrial fibrillation Better Care) significantly reduces mortality, stroke incidence, and bleeding events; DOACs recommended for all AF patients unless: mechanical heart valve, moderate-to-severe mitral stenosis, or ESRD; LAA occlusion viable for patients unsuitable for anticoagulation; digital health tools enable early AF detection; 4S-AF framework guides individualized treatment.
**Methods:** Narrative review; authors: Askarinejad A, Lane DA, Sadeghipour P, Haghjoo M, Lip GYH (leading AF stroke prevention expert); DOI: 10.1111/eci.70082. (PMC12362057)
**Rehab take:** Confirm DOAC initiated and appropriate in all post-stroke AF patients at IRF admission; screen for undiagnosed AF with telemetry monitoring if cardioembolic stroke etiology suspected; document LAA occlusion candidacy for anticoag-intolerant patients.
**Team take:**
  - 🩺 **Nursing** · DOAC adherence education: correct timing, dose, importance of not missing doses; daily monitoring for bleeding signs; patient education on AF symptoms (palpitations, dyspnea, presyncope) and when to seek emergency care.

### 14. MEDIUM — **Early multidisciplinary rehab led by "brain-heart health managers" improves functional + psychosocial outcomes in hemorrhagic stroke (N=216)**
PMC [12675196](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12675196/) *(PMID not confirmed at search time)*
*Frontiers in Neurology* · November 2025 | S08 · S11

**Key outcomes:** Intervention group (n=108) receiving structured multidimensional rehab directed by certified brain-heart health managers showed significantly better functional, psychological, and QoL outcomes vs. standard care (n=108 controls) in ICH survivors.
**Methods:** Retrospective cohort; single center (Second People's Hospital of Hunan Province, China); 216 consecutive ICH patients admitted Jan–Dec 2023; intervention = standard care + comprehensive program including psychological support, individualized PT, nutritional counseling, and continuous care coordination; DOI: 10.3389/fneur.2025.1683579
**Rehab take:** Comprehensive team-based rehab for hemorrhagic stroke is evidence-based and aligns with MFB IRF IDT model; psychological support must begin on day 1, not deferred until PSD is formally diagnosed.
**Team take:**
  - 🦿 **PT** · Early mobilization and individualized physical rehab from day 1 post-ICH admission to IRF; document daily mobility targets.
  - 🧠 **Psychology** · Proactive psychological support — not reactive screening only — from day of IRF admission for all ICH patients; early adjustment disorder and catastrophizing responses are common and respond to CBT-based approaches.

### 15. LOW — **CIMT activates contralesional red nucleus plasticity — mechanistic evidence from rat large-area stroke model**
PMC [11955289](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11955289/)
*Behavioural Neurology* · March 23, 2025 | S09

**Key outcomes:** CIMT promoted motor recovery in large-area stroke; ↑ GAP43 (axon outgrowth marker) + PSD95 (postsynaptic marker) in contralesional (not ipsilesional) red nucleus; ↑ bilateral motor cortex-to-red nucleus projections; mechanism = compensatory subcortical plasticity via contralesional pathway.
**Methods:** Animal study (rat large-area stroke model); CIMT vs. no-CIMT group; immunohistochemistry for neuroplasticity markers GAP43 and PSD95; DOI: 10.1155/bn/3631524
*⚠️ Preclinical animal model — mechanistic context only; no direct clinical application at this time.*

### 16. LOW — **rTMS + CIMT combined for post-stroke movement disorders: synergistic theory, minimal clinical RCT data**
PMC [12009840](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12009840/)
*Frontiers in Human Neuroscience* · April 7, 2025 | S09 · S11

**Key outcomes:** Narrative review demonstrates theoretical synergism: rTMS primes cortical excitability → CIMT capitalizes on enhanced neuroplasticity window; both modalities separately improve motor outcomes; combined approach has limited clinical RCT evidence to date.
**Methods:** Narrative review; authors: Liu Z, Yu Q, Zhou F, et al.; DOI: 10.3389/fnhum.2025.1578258 (PMC12009840)
*⚠️ Narrative review without meta-analysis; clinical translation pending adequately powered RCTs.*
**Team take:**
  - 🦿 **PT** · Watch for emerging RCTs on rTMS+CIMT combinations; conceptually supports scheduling CIMT sessions in the hours following any rTMS protocol if patient receives both.
