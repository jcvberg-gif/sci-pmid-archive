IM01 — Hypertension

1. ⭐⭐ TOP 3 #3 — 2025 AHA/ACC Hypertension Guideline Sets Universal <130/80 Target IM01

PMID 40815242

JACC / Circulation · 2025 | IM01

Key outcomes: Universal BP target reaffirmed at <130/80 mmHg for most adults; PREVENT cardiovascular risk calculator replaces Pooled Cohort Equation; Stage 1 HTN defined at ≥130/80; individualized targets recommended for institutionalized or limited life-expectancy patients.

Methods: Multi-society Clinical Practice Guideline (AHA/ACC + 11 co-societies); systematic evidence review through early 2025; replaces 2017 joint guideline.

Rehab take: The institutionalized-patient exception is directly relevant to IRF — do not reflexively treat BP <140/90 in frail SCI or stroke patients who are near end-stage; use clinical judgment guided by PREVENT risk stratification when optimizing outpatient antihypertensive regimens at discharge planning.

2. MEDIUM — QI Intervention Cuts PRN Antihypertensive Orders in Hospitalized Asymptomatic HTN IM01

PMID 40341299

Journal for Healthcare Quality · 2025 | IM01

Key outcomes: Multimodal QI intervention (EHR order set revision + multidisciplinary education) significantly reduced PRN antihypertensive use in medical patients with asymptomatic elevated BP without increasing adverse events.

Rehab take: IRF teams should adopt similar EHR-level guardrails — reflexive PRN hydralazine or labetalol for asymptomatic readings generates medication burden and orthostatic risk without documented benefit; reserve for hypertensive urgency/emergency criteria.

IM02 — Diabetes Mellitus

3. ⭐ HIGH — ADA Standards of Care 2026: Hospital Management Updated With CGM Endorsement IM02

PMID 41358900

Diabetes Care · 2026 (Supplement 1) | IM02

Key outcomes: Section 16 (Hospital) updates: CGM use in non-ICU inpatients endorsed as reasonable; insulin remains first-line; GLP-1 RAs/SGLT-2 inhibitors addressed for peri-hospitalization; hypoglycemia <70 mg/dL triggers mandatory reassessment; discharge glycemic planning emphasized.

Methods: Annual evidence-based guideline update by ADA Professional Practice Committee; integrates data from DIATEC and multiple CGM inpatient trials published through late 2025.

Rehab take: The 2026 Standards explicitly endorse CGM in non-ICU settings (including IRF); physiatry teams should facilitate CGM continuation for patients with personal devices and work with pharmacy/nursing to integrate CGM-guided insulin titration protocols.

4. ⭐⭐ TOP 3 #2 — DIATEC RCT: CGM-Guided Insulin Titration Improves Inpatient Glycemic Control IM02

PMID 39887698

Diabetes Care · 2025 (Vol 48, No 4) | IM02

Key outcomes: Time-in-range 77.6% (CGM) vs 62.7% (POC), Δ+15 percentage points; time-above-range >10 mmol/L lower in CGM arm (21.1%); prolonged hypoglycemia IRR 0.13 (95% CI 0.04–0.46, p=0.001); coefficient of variation 25.4% vs 28.0% (p=0.024).

Methods: Two-center RCT (DIATEC); n=166 non-ICU inpatients with T2DM; diabetes team-managed insulin titration algorithms based on CGM (FreeStyle Libre/Dexcom G6) vs point-of-care glucometry; primary endpoint: time-in-range 70–180 mg/dL.

Rehab take: Landmark evidence for IRF: CGM-guided insulin protocols improve TiR by 15 pp and dramatically reduce prolonged hypoglycemia events — directly applicable to SCI and stroke patients where therapy-session activity compounds insulin management complexity.

5. MEDIUM — Inpatient Diabetes Care Gaps: Cardioprotective Agent Underuse Highlighted IM02

PMID 41429634

European Journal of Internal Medicine · 2026 | IM02

Key outcomes: EFIM Diabesity Day Survey 2024: widespread gaps in glycemic monitoring and severe underuse of cardioprotective GLAs (GLP-1 RAs, SGLT-2 inhibitors) during hospitalization across European centers.

Rehab take: IRF admission is a high-yield opportunity for medication reconciliation — ensure T2DM patients established on GLP-1 RAs or SGLT-2 inhibitors have these continued or re-initiated prior to discharge per cardiometabolic benefit guidelines.

6. MEDIUM — Inpatient Automated Insulin Delivery: Growing Evidence, Key Operational Barriers IM02

PMID 41764080

Journal of Diabetes Science and Technology · 2025 | IM02

Key outcomes: Narrative review of hospital-initiated AID and personal AID device continuation during admission; data support feasibility and glycemic benefit; key barriers include hospital liability, nursing workflow integration, and lack of standardized protocols.

Rehab take: As IRF admits more T2DM/T1DM patients wearing closed-loop AID systems, rehab teams need policies for device management during therapy sessions, skin integrity checks under adhesives, and hypoglycemia response protocols.

IM03 — AKI / Contrast Nephropathy

7. LOW — AKI-to-CKD Transition: Maladaptive Tubular Repair as Central Mechanism IM03

PMID 42268530

International Urology and Nephrology · 2026 | IM03

Key outcomes: Review establishes maladaptive proximal tubular epithelial repair as central event in AKI-to-CKD progression; even functionally "recovered" AKI may leave persistent transcriptional, structural, and metabolic injury predisposing to CKD.

Rehab take: IRF patients with recent AKI (post-cardiac surgery, post-sepsis) should have BMP checked at admission and periodically; contrast agents and nephrotoxins warrant heightened caution even when serum creatinine has normalized.

IM04 — Heart Failure / Post-MI

8. MEDIUM — Home-Based Rehabilitation Feasible and Effective in Frail Post-ADHF Patients IM04

PMID 41711697

ESC Heart Failure · 2026 | IM04

Key outcomes: Pilot-CRHF study: 80% program completion; clinically meaningful improvements in 6MWT and quality of life at 12 weeks in pre-frail/frail HF patients; supports larger RCT.

Methods: Monocentric single-arm prospective feasibility study; n=30 recently hospitalized ADHF patients classified pre-frail/frail; 12-week home-based individualized program (strength, endurance, balance).

Rehab take: For HF patients discharging to home directly from IRF, a structured 12-week home exercise prescription with explicit cardiovascular precautions is feasible even in frail subgroups — prescribe a graded HEP rather than unstructured activity.

9. MEDIUM — Inpatient vs. Outpatient Cardiac Rehab After MI: Comorbidity Burden Drives Selection IM04

PMID 40364039

Journal of Clinical Medicine · 2025 | IM04

Key outcomes: Population-based Polish registry (n=96,634 MI hospitalizations); DM, HF, CKD, and COPD independently predicted inpatient CR selection over outpatient CR; younger males more likely to be referred to outpatient CR.

Rehab take: This validates IRF/inpatient CR selection logic — the post-MI patients MFB admits are by definition the most comorbid; IM co-management of DM, HF, CKD, and COPD is integral to rehab outcomes, not ancillary.

IM05 — Atrial Fibrillation & Anticoagulation

10. ⭐ HIGH — Timing of Anticoagulation After Cardioembolic Stroke with AFib: Infarct-Size-Based Approach IM05

PMID 41144584

Stroke · 2025 | IM05

Key outcomes: Synthesis of TIMING, ELAN, and OPTIMAS RCTs: early DOAC initiation (within 48h–4 days for small/medium infarcts; 7–14 days for large infarcts) is safe and reduces recurrent stroke without significantly increasing sICH; the "1-3-6-12 day rule" superseded by infarct-size-based approach.

Methods: Neurocardiology advances review; Johansen & Sposato; Stroke, Oct 2025; DOI 10.1161/STROKEAHA.125.049901.

Rehab take: Stroke patients transferred to IRF should have documentation of DOAC initiation date and rationale — for large infarcts, DOAC may appropriately be delayed to day 7–14; IRF teams should verify this is active delay rather than omission by oversight.

11. MEDIUM — LAAC vs. Oral Anticoagulation: Updated Meta-Analysis Supports Non-Inferiority IM05

PMID 40748854

Heart Rhythm · 2025 (Apr;22(4):979–986) | IM05

Key outcomes: Updated systematic review and meta-analysis of RCTs comparing LAAC vs OAC; LAAC demonstrated comparable stroke/systemic embolism prevention with favorable bleeding profile in pooled analyses; long-term mortality data converging.

Rehab take: For high fall-risk SCI or TBI patients with AFib where OAC is prohibitive, LAAC is an evidence-supported option — flag high-bleed-risk AFib patients to cardiology for LAAC consideration rather than defaulting to aspirin monotherapy.

12. MEDIUM — Apixaban Reduces Stroke Risk in Subclinical AFib with Prior Stroke (ARTESiA Subgroup) IM05

PMID 39862882

Lancet Neurology · 2025 | IM05

Key outcomes: ARTESiA pre-specified subgroup (n≈1,000 with prior stroke/TIA and subclinical AFib): apixaban reduced stroke/systemic embolism vs aspirin; major bleeding increased with apixaban; net benefit favored apixaban in prior-stroke subgroup.

Rehab take: Stroke IRF patients identified with subclinical AFib via cardiac monitoring should be flagged for neurology/cardiology consultation regarding apixaban initiation — net benefit is clearer in prior-stroke patients than primary prevention subclinical AFib.

IM06 — COPD / Asthma

13. MEDIUM — COPD Exacerbation Management in ED: Protocol Adherence Gaps Persist IM06

PMID 38602629

Internal and Emergency Medicine · 2024 | IM06

Key outcomes: Retrospective AECOPD analysis: short-acting bronchodilators used in only 56%; systemic steroids in 86%; spirometry underutilized; significant deviation from GOLD guidelines in real-world ED practice.

Rehab take: COPD patients transferred to IRF post-AECOPD may have received suboptimal acute management — verify inhaler technique and regimen at admission, confirm appropriate steroid taper plan, and consider pulmonary rehab referral for eligible patients.

IM07 — Aspiration Pneumonia / Dysphagia

14. ⭐ HIGH — Dysphagia Services Severely Underutilized in Older Adults Hospitalized With Pneumonia IM07

PMC PMC12581205

Journal of the American Geriatrics Society · 2025 | IM07

Key outcomes: N=195,782 older adults (≥60) hospitalized with CAP or aspiration pneumonia (Premier Healthcare Database, April 2022–December 2023); dysphagia services (clinical eval, instrumental eval, therapy) underutilized across all pneumonia subtypes; aspiration PNA patients received more services than CAP, but overall rates remained low; neurodegenerative disease was the strongest utilization predictor.

Methods: Retrospective database study; multilevel generalized linear mixed models adjusting for patient demographics, comorbidities, and hospital characteristics; n=195,782 discharges from US hospitals.

Rehab take: Patients transferred to IRF with aspiration pneumonia have a high probability of unscreened or under-screened dysphagia at the sending facility — IRF protocol should include standardized swallow screen or SLP consultation within 24h of admission regardless of acute-care documentation.

15. MEDIUM — Predischarge EAT-10 Predicts 90-Day Aspiration Pneumonia and Hospital Readmission IM07

PMC PMC12487540

Cureus · 2025 | IM07

Key outcomes: N=511 clinically stable discharged inpatients; elevated EAT-10 correlated with 90-day aspiration pneumonia (r=0.245, p<0.001; OR 1.03) and 90-day readmission (r=0.310, p<0.001; OR 1.01); unresolved dysphagia at discharge is a significant outcome predictor.

Rehab take: Completing the EAT-10 at IRF discharge is a low-burden, high-yield screen; scores >2 should trigger formal SLP evaluation and outpatient swallowing therapy referral before discharge to prevent post-IRF aspiration pneumonia readmissions.

IM08 — Urinary Tract Infection

16. ⭐ HIGH — Nomogram Predicts UTI Risk in Hospitalized SCI Rehab Patients IM08

PMID 38810877

World Neurosurgery · 2024 | IM08

Key outcomes: Three independent UTI risk factors in SCI rehab inpatients: ASIA impairment level, WBC count, and presence of indwelling urinary catheter; nomogram demonstrated good predictive efficiency; indwelling catheter carried highest independent risk.

Methods: Retrospective study of hospitalized SCI rehabilitation patients; multivariate logistic regression; nomogram developed and internally validated.

Rehab take: Every SCI patient admitted to IRF with an indwelling catheter plus elevated WBC should prompt immediate CAUTI prevention audit (catheter necessity, CIC feasibility, catheter care) — the nomogram validates that catheter presence plus systemic inflammation substantially elevates UTI risk warranting proactive management.

17. MEDIUM — Reconceptualizing Recurrent UTI in SCI: Paradigm Shift to Symptom-Based Diagnosis IM08

PMID 39567040

Physical Medicine and Rehabilitation Clinics of North America · 2025 | IM08

Key outcomes: Review of UTI management in neurogenic LUTD; culture-based-only diagnosis drives antibiotic overuse; symptom-based diagnostic reconceptualization advocated; bladder management optimization emphasized as primary prevention.

Rehab take: Do not treat asymptomatic bacteriuria in SCI patients — focus antibiotic initiation on symptom criteria (new AD, change in spasticity, cloudy/malodorous urine plus systemic signs); optimize bladder management (CIC > indwelling catheter) as the primary prevention strategy.

IM09 — Delirium / Dementia

18. ⭐⭐ TOP 3 #1 — Early Cognitive Intervention During Delirium Hospitalization Improves 4-Month Cognition: RCT IM09

PMID 41992853

Journal of the American Geriatrics Society · 2026 | IM09

Key outcomes: Twice-daily in-hospital cognitive training + weekly cognitive rehabilitation (×12 weeks post-discharge) significantly improved global cognition at 4 months (RBANS) vs usual care in adults ≥65 with active delirium; delirium affects 17% of older ED patients and 25% of older hospitalized adults, and is associated with accelerated long-term cognitive decline.

Methods: RCT (NCT04740567); English-speaking inpatients ≥65 with active delirium at enrollment; excluded: end-stage dementia, nursing home residents, hospice; intervention: structured cognitive training (twice daily inpatient) + cognitive rehab (weekly ×12 weeks post-discharge); primary outcome: RBANS at 4 months.

Rehab take: IRF is ideally positioned to deliver the post-acute phase of this intervention — structured cognitive rehabilitation continued through IRF stay and into home/outpatient therapy may substantially reduce the long-term cognitive sequelae of delirium; this is a strong rationale for formal cognitive rehab protocols for all admitted patients with a delirium history.

19. LOW — Delirium Frequently Misses ED Triage When Presenting Atypically IM09

PMID 41731381

BMC Geriatrics · 2026 | IM09

Key outcomes: Retrospective cohort (Geneva); older adults with delirium frequently presented with atypical complaints (falls, generalized weakness, functional decline); emergency triage priority underestimated; diagnosis delayed in a significant proportion.

Rehab take: Patients transferred to IRF with recent hospitalization for "functional decline" or "fall" may have undetected or incompletely resolved delirium — a structured delirium screen (CAM or bCAM) on IRF admission is warranted for all patients ≥65 regardless of referring diagnosis.

IM10 — Polypharmacy / Beers Criteria

20. MEDIUM — Deprescribing Interventions in Older Adults With Polypharmacy: >50% Show Benefit Over Usual Care IM10

PMID 38719530

BMJ · 2024 | IM10

Key outcomes: Narrative review of RCTs: >50% of deprescribing interventions outperformed usual care on primary outcomes; medication review with shared decision-making most effective; polypharmacy (≥5 meds) linked to falls, cognitive impairment, hospitalizations, and mortality; PPI, aspirin, and zolpidem among most commonly deprescribed Beers criteria medications.

Rehab take: IRF admission is one of medicine's highest-yield deprescribing moments — request formal pharmacy medication reconciliation targeting Beers-listed PIMs (benzodiazepines, anticholinergics, sedating antihistamines, PPIs without indication) within 48h of admission, with a goal of reducing medication burden before discharge.
