IM01 — Hypertension
1.
⭐ HIGH
AHA statement: treat the cause before escalating BP meds in hospitalized patients
PMID 38804130  ·  Hypertension · 2024 | IM01
Key outcomes: No existing guidelines define inpatient BP goals or recommend routine pharmacological escalation for asymptomatic moderately elevated BP. For hypertensive emergency (new or worsening target-organ damage), IV antihypertensives are appropriate. For asymptomatic elevation, best evidence supports addressing underlying causes first; IV agents risk overcorrection and iatrogenic harm.
Methods: AHA scientific statement; systematic literature review and expert consensus on inpatient BP management across acute care settings. AHA Writing Group. Hypertension 2024 (DOI 10.1161/HYP.0000000000000238).
Rehab take: Before adding antihypertensives for an unexplained BP spike in an IRF patient, rule out the classic SCI/rehab triggers: autonomic dysreflexia (most urgently), pain, missed home medications, bladder or bowel distension, and anxiety. This statement provides institutional backing for a "treat the cause first" order set at MFB.
IM02 — Diabetes Mellitus

⭐⭐ TOP 3 #1
ADA 2026 endorses inpatient CGM and AID continuation; glycemic target 100–180 mg/dL for non-ICU
PMID 41358892  ·  Diabetes Care · 2026 | IM02
Key outcomes: Non-ICU inpatient glycemic target: 100–180 mg/dL. ICU target: 140–180 mg/dL. Continuation of personal CGM during hospitalization explicitly recommended when clinically appropriate (confirmatory POC glucose required for insulin dosing). AID systems and insulin pumps should be continued under institutional protocols. Discharge diabetes self-management education established as a formal care standard.
Methods: ADA Standards of Care annual update, Chapter 16. Expert systematic evidence review by ADA writing committee. Diabetes Care 49(Suppl 1):S339–S355, January 2026.
Rehab take: ADA 2026 provides the clinical authority MFB needs for a written inpatient CGM/AID protocol. Patients arriving on home CGM or insulin pump systems should continue them with a confirmatory POC glucose workflow for nursing. Discharge glycemic education initiated on the IRF satisfies ADA 2026 and may reduce 30-day readmissions.

IM05 — AFib & Anticoagulation
7.
⭐⭐ TOP 3 #2
RCT meta-analysis confirms early DOAC after AF-related ischemic stroke is safe and non-inferior to delayed strategy
PMID 41697537  ·  February 2026 · 2026 | IM05
Key outcomes: Meta-analysis of RCTs (systematic search through June 2025): early anticoagulation after acute ischemic stroke in AF patients showed composite primary outcome (recurrent ischemic stroke, symptomatic ICH, or death) comparable to delayed initiation. No statistically significant increase in symptomatic ICH with early strategy. Results support early DOAC initiation in patients with predominantly mild-to-moderate ischemic stroke.
Methods: Systematic review and meta-analysis of RCTs. Databases searched: PubMed, Embase, Cochrane CENTRAL, ScienceDirect (through June 2025). Population: adults with acute ischemic stroke + AF. Outcomes: composite of recurrent ischemic stroke, symptomatic ICH, death; functional independence. Published February 2026.
Rehab take: For stroke patients arriving to MFB IRF still in the acute-to-subacute anticoagulation decision window, early DOAC initiation appears safe in mild-to-moderate strokes. Confirm anticoagulation status on every AF + stroke admission; if not started, prompt neurology for a timing discussion before the window closes.

MEDIUM
Subclinical AFib screening detects more arrhythmia but RCTs show no anticoagulation benefit
PMID 42384436  ·  2026 Review · 2026 | IM05
Key outcomes: Device-detected AFib episodes ≥6 minutes elevate stroke risk by 2–2.5× vs. no subclinical AF, but absolute risk remains substantially lower than clinically evident AF. Screening increases detection rates without improving outcomes (LOOP, NOAH-AFNET 6, ARTESiA trials). Routine oral anticoagulation for subclinical AFib showed no consistent clinical benefit across randomized trials.
Rehab take: Continuous cardiac monitoring in the IRF often incidentally detects brief subclinical AFib. Per this review, brief asymptomatic episodes should prompt documentation and cardiology communication, but routine DOAC initiation is not yet evidence-based. Avoid reflexive anticoagulation without shared decision-making and risk-benefit discussion.
IM06 — COPD / Asthma
9.
MEDIUM
All pulmonary rehab delivery models improve COPD outcomes; participation matters more than format
PMID 41461647  ·  2025 Network Meta-Analysis · 2025 | IM06
Key outcomes: 33 studies (N=2,538); 27 studies (N=2,106) included in network meta-analysis. All delivery models — centre-based, home-based, telerehab — improved dyspnea, 6MWT, and health-related QoL vs. no PR. Certainty of evidence rated LOW for all outcomes due to high risk of bias (22/33 studies) and imprecision.
Rehab take: For COPD patients discharged from MFB IRF, any accessible format of pulmonary rehab is better than none. Connect patients to the nearest and most logistically feasible option — telerehab if distance or mobility limits centre-based attendance — rather than delaying enrollment waiting for preferred format availability.

PMID 42500843  ·  International Journal of Language and Communication Disorders · 2026 | IM07

IM08 — Urinary Tract Infection
11.
MEDIUM
UA-to-culture reflex protocol drives high rates of inappropriate ASB treatment despite IDSA guidelines
PMID 40989674  ·  Antimicrobial Stewardship & Healthcare Epidemiology · 2025 | IM08
Key outcomes: Institutional UA-to-UC reflex protocol (UA positive for pyuria >10 WBC/HPF, leukocyte esterase, or nitrites → automatic reflex to culture): only 30.1% of ordered UAs triggered a UC reflex; of those, >50% of UC results were negative. System-wide, 45–83% of patients still received inappropriate antibiotics for asymptomatic bacteriuria (ASB) despite IDSA guideline recommendations against treatment.
Rehab take: SCI patients on MFB IRF routinely have pyuria and bacteriuria without symptoms. Reflex UA→culture ordering patterns drive inappropriate antibiotic treatment and resistance. A symptom-based UTI ordering protocol is an actionable antimicrobial stewardship target — especially in the SCI population where antibiotic overuse accelerates resistant organism colonization.
IM09 — Delirium / Dementia
12.
⭐ HIGH
ABCDEF bundle and family engagement independently reduce ICU delirium: meta-review of 32 systematic reviews
PMID 41272698  ·  Critical Care · 2026 | IM09
Key outcomes: Meta-review of 32 SRs/meta-analyses encompassing 335 primary studies, evaluating 16 non-pharmacological interventions. ABCDEF bundle and multicomponent interventions significantly reduced delirium incidence and duration. Early mobilization independently reduced delirium. Family participation in care independently reduced delirium incidence. Multicomponent interventions associated with mortality benefits.
Methods: Systematic meta-review of systematic reviews and meta-analyses; Part 2 (non-pharmacological + multicomponent interventions). Eight databases searched from inception to August 2025. Critical Care, DOI 10.1186/s13054-025-05726-8, 2026.
Rehab take: IRF is ideally positioned to operationalize ABCDEF elements: structured sleep/wake cycles, pain optimization, delirium screening, early mobilization (a core IRF function), and active family engagement. MFB should embed explicit delirium prevention bundles in admission protocols for high-risk populations — TBI, stroke, post-ICU, and elderly with polypharmacy.
IM10 — Polypharmacy / Beers
13.
⭐⭐ TOP 3 #3
Polypharmacy at IRF discharge independently predicts worse ADL recovery, especially in stroke and deconditioning (N=1,903)
PMID 41408196  ·  BMC Geriatrics · 2025 | IM10
Key outcomes: N=1,903 patients aged ≥65 years in a Japanese convalescent rehabilitation hospital (April 2017–March 2024). 62.1% had polypharmacy (≥6 concurrent prescription medications) at discharge; 76.4% were aged ≥80 years. Polypharmacy at discharge independently and negatively associated with functional recovery (FIM gain, Barthel Index gain), with the strongest association in cerebrovascular disease and disuse syndrome groups.
Methods: Retrospective observational study of older rehabilitation inpatients. Polypharmacy defined as ≥6 regular prescription medications at discharge. Functional outcomes: FIM gain and Barthel Index gain. Subgroup analysis by rehabilitation diagnostic category. BMC Geriatrics 25 (2025).
Rehab take: Every MFB IRF discharge is an opportunity to deprescribe. This study — the largest available specifically in rehabilitation inpatients — directly supports a standing pharmacist consult on admission for all patients ≥80 years with ≥6 medications, targeting stroke and deconditioning patients where polypharmacy most strongly limits ADL gains. Review meds before discharge, not just at admission.
