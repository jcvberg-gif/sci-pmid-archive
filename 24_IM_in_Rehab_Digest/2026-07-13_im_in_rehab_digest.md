## IM01 — Hypertension

### 1. ⭐⭐ TOP 3 #3 — **2025 AHA/ACC Hypertension Guideline Sets Universal <130/80 Target**
PMID [40815242](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40815242/)
*JACC / Circulation* · 2025 | IM01

**Key outcomes:** Universal BP target reaffirmed at <130/80 mmHg for most adults; PREVENT cardiovascular risk calculator replaces Pooled Cohort Equation; Stage 1 HTN defined at ≥130/80; individualized targets recommended for institutionalized or limited life-expectancy patients.
**Methods:** Multi-society Clinical Practice Guideline (AHA/ACC + 11 co-societies); systematic evidence review through early 2025; replaces 2017 joint guideline.
**Rehab take:** The institutionalized-patient exception is directly relevant to IRF — do not reflexively treat BP <140/90 in frail SCI or stroke patients who are near end-stage; use clinical judgment guided by PREVENT risk stratification when optimizing outpatient antihypertensive regimens at discharge planning.

### 2. MEDIUM — **QI Intervention Cuts PRN Antihypertensive Orders in Hospitalized Asymptomatic HTN**
PMID [40341299](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40341299/)
*Journal for Healthcare Quality* · 2025 | IM01

**Key outcomes:** Multimodal QI intervention (EHR order set revision + multidisciplinary education) significantly reduced PRN antihypertensive use in medical patients with asymptomatic elevated BP (systolic ≥130 or diastolic ≥80) without increasing adverse events.
**Rehab take:** IRF teams should adopt similar EHR-level guardrails — reflexive PRN hydralazine or labetalol for asymptomatic readings generates medication burden and orthostatic risk without documented benefit; reserve for hypertensive urgency/emergency criteria.

## IM02 — Diabetes Mellitus

### 3. ⭐ HIGH — **ADA Standards of Care 2026: Hospital Management Updated**
PMID [41358900](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41358900/)
*Diabetes Care* · 2026 (Supplement 1) | IM02

**Key outcomes:** Section 16 (Hospital) updates: CGM use in non-ICU inpatients endorsed as reasonable; insulin remains first-line for inpatient hyperglycemia; GLP-1 RAs and SGLT-2 inhibitors addressed for peri-hospitalization; hypoglycemia target <70 mg/dL triggers mandatory reassessment; discharge glycemic planning emphasized.
**Methods:** Annual evidence-based guideline update by ADA Professional Practice Committee; integrates data from DIATEC and multiple CGM inpatient trials published through late 2025.
**Rehab take:** The 2026 Standards explicitly endorse CGM in non-ICU settings (including IRF); physiatry teams should facilitate CGM continuation for patients admitted with personal devices and work with pharmacy/nursing to integrate CGM-guided insulin titration protocols.

---

### 4. ⭐⭐ TOP 3 #2 — **DIATEC RCT: CGM-Guided Insulin Titration Improves Inpatient Glycemic Control**
PMID [39887698](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39887698/)
*Diabetes Care* · 2025 (Vol 48, No 4) | IM02

**Key outcomes:** Time-in-range 77.6% (CGM) vs 62.7% (POC), Δ+15 pp; time-above-range >10 mmol/L 21.1% (CGM) vs higher in POC arm; prolonged hypoglycemia IRR 0.13 (95% CI 0.04–0.46, p=0.001); coefficient of variation 25.4% vs 28.0% (p=0.024).
**Methods:** Two-center RCT (DIATEC); n=166 non-ICU inpatients with T2DM; diabetes team managed insulin titration using algorithms based on CGM (FreeStyle Libre/Dexcom G6) vs point-of-care glucometry; primary endpoint: time-in-range 70–180 mg/dL over study period.
**Rehab take:** This is landmark evidence for IRF: CGM-guided insulin protocols improve TiR by 15 percentage points and dramatically reduce prolonged hypoglycemia events, directly applicable to SCI and stroke patients where exercise-driven glycemic swings during therapy sessions compound insulin management complexity.

### 5. MEDIUM — **Inpatient Diabetes Care Gaps Persist: Cardioprotective Agent Underuse Highlighted**

**Key outcomes:** EFIM Diabesity Day Survey 2024 data: widespread gaps in glycemic monitoring and severe underuse of cardioprotective glucose-lowering agents (GLP-1 RAs, SGLT-2 inhibitors) during hospitalization across European centers.
**Rehab take:** IRF admission is a high-yield opportunity for medication reconciliation — ensure T2DM patients established on GLP-1 RAs or SGLT-2 inhibitors have these continued or re-initiated prior to discharge per cardiometabolic benefit guidelines.

PMID [41764080](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41764080/)

PMID [42268530](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42268530/)
*International Urology and Nephrology* · 2026 | IM03

**Key outcomes:** Review establishes that maladaptive proximal tubular epithelial repair is the central event in AKI-to-CKD progression; even functionally "recovered" AKI can leave persistent transcriptional, structural, and metabolic injury; biomarker monitoring post-AKI proposed.

## IM04 — Heart Failure / Post-MI

### 8. MEDIUM — **Home-Based Rehabilitation Feasible and Effective in Frail Post-ADHF Patients**
PMID [41711697](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41711697/)
*ESC Heart Failure* · 2026 | IM04

**Key outcomes:** Pilot-CRHF study: 80% program completion rate; clinically meaningful improvements in 6-minute walk test and quality of life at 12 weeks in pre-frail/frail HF patients; supports larger RCT.
**Methods:** Monocentric single-arm prospective feasibility study; n=30 recently hospitalized ADHF patients classified as pre-frail or frail; 12-week home-based individualized exercise program targeting strength, endurance, and balance.
**Rehab take:** For HF patients discharging to home directly from IRF, a structured 12-week home exercise prescription with remote monitoring is feasible even in frail subgroups — physiatrists should prescribe a graded HEP with explicit cardiovascular precautions rather than unstructured activity.

### 9. MEDIUM — **Inpatient vs. Outpatient Cardiac Rehab After MI: Comorbidity Burden Drives Selection**
PMID [40364039](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40364039/)
*Journal of Clinical Medicine* · 2025 | IM04

**Rehab take:** This validates IRF/inpatient CR selection logic — the patients MFB sees post-MI are by definition the most comorbid; IM co-management of DM, HF, CKD, and COPD in this population is integral to rehab success, not ancillary to it.

## IM05 — Atrial Fibrillation & Anticoagulation

### 10. ⭐ HIGH — **Timing of Anticoagulation After Cardioembolic Stroke with AFib: Practical Guidance**
PMID [41144584](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41144584/)
*Stroke* · 2025 | IM05

**Key outcomes:** Synthesis of TIMING, ELAN, and OPTIMAS RCTs: early DOAC initiation (within 48h–4 days for small/medium infarcts, 7–14 days for large infarcts) is safe and reduces recurrent stroke without significantly increasing sICH; "1-3-6-12 day rule" superseded by infarct-size-based approach.
**Methods:** Neurocardiology review article synthesizing recent RCT data; authors Johansen and Sposato (Stroke, Oct 2025); DOI 10.1161/STROKEAHA.125.049901.
**Rehab take:** Stroke patients transferred to IRF should have documentation of DOAC initiation date and justification; for large infarcts, DOAC may be appropriately delayed to day 7–14 — IRF teams should verify timing is active rather than omitted by oversight.

### 11. MEDIUM — **LAAC vs. Oral Anticoagulation: Updated Meta-Analysis Supports Non-Inferiority**
PMID [40748854](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40748854/)
*Heart Rhythm* · 2025 (Apr;22(4):979–986) | IM05

**Key outcomes:** Updated systematic review and meta-analysis of RCTs comparing LAAC (Watchman) vs OAC (primarily DOACs); LAAC demonstrated comparable stroke/systemic embolism prevention with favorable bleeding profile in pooled analyses; long-term data show mortality benefit converging.
**Rehab take:** For high fall-risk SCI or TBI patients with AFib where OAC risk is prohibitive, LAAC is an evidence-supported option — IRF teams should flag high-bleed-risk AFib patients to cardiology for LAAC consideration rather than defaulting to aspirin monotherapy.

### 12. MEDIUM — **Apixaban Reduces Stroke in Subclinical AFib with Prior Stroke, at Cost of Bleeding**
PMID [39862882](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39862882/)
*Lancet Neurology* · 2025 | IM05

**Key outcomes:** ARTESiA subgroup (n≈1,000 prior stroke/TIA with subclinical AFib): apixaban reduced stroke/systemic embolism vs aspirin; major bleeding increased with apixaban; net benefit favored apixaban in prior-stroke subgroup.
**Rehab take:** IRF stroke patients found to have subclinical AFib on cardiac monitoring should be flagged for cardiology/neurology consultation regarding apixaban initiation — the net benefit in prior-stroke patients is clearer than in primary prevention subclinical AFib.

## IM07 — Aspiration Pneumonia / Dysphagia

### 14. ⭐ HIGH — **Dysphagia Services Severely Underutilized in Older Adults Hospitalized With Pneumonia**
PMC [PMC12581205](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12581205/)
*Journal of the American Geriatrics Society* · 2025 | IM07

**Key outcomes:** N=195,782 older adults (≥60) hospitalized with CAP or aspiration pneumonia (April 2022–December 2023); dysphagia clinical evaluations, instrumental evaluations (VFSS/FEES), and therapy utilization varied widely by hospital and diagnosis; aspiration PNA patients had higher service utilization than CAP, but overall rates remained low; neurodegenerative disease was a key utilization predictor.
**Methods:** Retrospective database study (Premier Healthcare Database); multilevel generalized linear mixed models adjusting for patient demographics, comorbidities, and hospital characteristics; n=195,782 discharges from hundreds of US hospitals.
**Rehab take:** Patients transferred to IRF with aspiration pneumonia have a high probability of unscreened or under-screened dysphagia at the sending facility — IRF protocol should include standardized swallow screen or SLP consultation within 24 hours of admission regardless of acute-care documentation.

### 15. MEDIUM — **Predischarge EAT-10 Score Predicts 90-Day Aspiration Pneumonia and Readmission**
PMC [PMC12487540](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12487540/)
*Cureus* · 2025 | IM07

**Key outcomes:** N=511 clinically stable discharged inpatients (Lahore, Jan–Jun 2025); elevated EAT-10 correlated with 90-day aspiration pneumonia (r=0.245, p<0.001; OR 1.03) and readmission (r=0.310, p<0.001; OR 1.01); unresolved dysphagia at discharge is a significant outcome predictor.
**Rehab take:** Completing the EAT-10 at IRF discharge is a low-burden, high-yield screen; scores >2 should trigger formal SLP evaluation and outpatient swallowing therapy referral before discharge to prevent post-IRF aspiration pneumonia readmissions.

## IM08 — Urinary Tract Infection

### 16. ⭐ HIGH — **Nomogram Predicts UTI Risk in Hospitalized SCI Rehab Patients**
PMID [38810877](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38810877/)
*World Neurosurgery* · 2024 | IM08

**Key outcomes:** Three independent UTI risk factors identified in SCI rehab inpatients: ASIA classification level, WBC count, and presence of indwelling urinary catheter; nomogram showed good predictive efficiency; indwelling catheter carried highest independent risk.
**Methods:** Retrospective study of hospitalized SCI rehabilitation patients; multivariate logistic regression; nomogram developed and internally validated.
**Rehab take:** Every SCI patient admitted to MFB IRF with an indwelling catheter plus elevated WBC should prompt immediate CAUTI prevention audit (catheter necessity, aseptic CIC feasibility, catheter care compliance) — the nomogram validates what clinicians already suspect: catheter presence plus systemic inflammatory response substantially elevates UTI risk.

### 17. MEDIUM — **Reconceptualizing Recurrent UTI in SCI: Paradigm Shift Toward Symptom-Based Diagnosis**
PMID [39567040](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39567040/)
*Physical Medicine and Rehabilitation Clinics of North America* · 2025 | IM08

**Key outcomes:** Review of UTI management in neurogenic LUTD; majority of SCI patients with neurogenic bladder will develop "recurrent" UTI by standard definitions; diagnoses based on urine culture alone over-diagnose UTI and drive antibiotic overuse; symptom-based diagnostic reconceptualization advocated.
**Rehab take:** Do not treat asymptomatic bacteriuria in SCI patients — focus antibiotic initiation on symptom criteria (new autonomic dysreflexia, change in spasticity, cloudy/malodorous urine plus systemic signs); bladder management optimization (CIC > indwelling catheter) remains the primary prevention strategy.

## IM09 — Delirium / Dementia

### 18. ⭐⭐ TOP 3 #1 — **Early Cognitive Intervention During Delirium Hospitalization Improves 4-Month Global Cognition: RCT**
PMID [41992853](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41992853/)
*Journal of the American Geriatrics Society* · 2026 | IM09

**Key outcomes:** RCT testing twice-daily in-hospital cognitive training + weekly outpatient cognitive rehabilitation (×12 weeks post-discharge) vs usual care in adults ≥65 with active delirium; primary outcome global cognition at 4 months (RBANS) significantly improved in intervention arm; delirium affects 17% of older ED patients and 25% of hospitalized older adults, and is associated with accelerated long-term cognitive decline.
**Methods:** RCT (NCT04740567); English-speaking inpatients ≥65 with active delirium at enrollment; excluded: end-stage dementia, nursing home residents, hospice; intervention: structured cognitive training (twice daily inpatient) + cognitive rehab (weekly ×12 weeks post-discharge); control: usual care.
**Rehab take:** IRF is ideally positioned to deliver the post-acute phase of this intervention — structured cognitive rehabilitation initiated during delirium hospitalization and continued through IRF stay and discharge may substantially reduce the cognitive sequelae of delirium; this is a strong rationale for formal cognitive rehab protocols for all admitted patients with delirium history.

### 19. LOW — **Delirium Often Presents Atypically in Older Adults: ED Triage Frequently Misses It**
PMID [41731381](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41731381/)
*BMC Geriatrics* · 2026 | IM09

**Key outcomes:** Retrospective cohort (Geneva); older adults hospitalized with delirium frequently presented with atypical chief complaints (falls, generalized weakness, functional decline) rather than confusion; emergency triage priority often underestimated; diagnosis delayed in significant proportion.
**Rehab take:** Patients transferred to IRF with recent hospitalization for "functional decline" or "fall" may have undetected or incompletely resolved delirium — a structured delirium screen (CAM or bCAM) on IRF admission is warranted for all patients ≥65 regardless of referring diagnosis.

## IM10 — Polypharmacy / Beers Criteria

### 20. MEDIUM — **Deprescribing in Older Adults with Polypharmacy: RCT Evidence Supports Benefit**
PMID [38719530](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38719530/)
*BMJ* · 2024 | IM10

**Key outcomes:** Narrative review of RCTs; >50% of deprescribing interventions showed benefit over usual care in primary outcomes; medication review with shared decision-making most effective approach; polypharmacy (≥5 meds) linked to falls, cognitive impairment, hospitalizations, and mortality in older adults; PPI, aspirin, and benzodiazepine/Z-drug (zolpidem) among most commonly deprescribed Beers criteria medications.
**Rehab take:** IRF admission is one of medicine's highest-yield deprescribing moments — the physiatry team should formally request pharmacy medication reconciliation targeting Beers-listed PIMs (especially benzodiazepines, anticholinergics, sedating antihistamines, and PPIs without indication) within 48h of admission, with a goal of reducing medication burden before discharge.
