## IM01 — Hypertension

### 1. ⭐ HIGH — AHA statement: treat the cause before escalating BP meds in hospitalized patients
PMID [38804130](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38804130/)
*Hypertension* · 2024 | IM01

**Key outcomes:** No existing guidelines define inpatient BP goals or recommend treating asymptomatic moderately elevated BP with pharmacological escalation. For hypertensive emergency (new or worsening target-organ damage), IV antihypertensives are appropriate. For asymptomatic elevation, best evidence supports addressing underlying causes first; IV agents in this context risk overcorrection and harm.  
**Methods:** AHA scientific statement; systematic literature review and expert consensus on inpatient BP management across acute care settings (non-emergency department). Writing group synthesis, published *Hypertension* 2024 (DOI 10.1161/HYP.0000000000000238).  
**Rehab take:** Before adding antihypertensives for an unexplained BP spike in an IRF patient, rule out the classic SCI/rehab triggers: autonomic dysreflexia (most urgently), pain, missed home medications, bladder or bowel distension, and anxiety. This statement provides institutional backing for a "treat the cause first" order set at MFB.

## IM02 — Diabetes Mellitus

### 2. ⭐⭐ TOP 3 #1 — ADA 2026 endorses inpatient CGM and AID; targets 100–180 mg/dL for non-ICU
PMID [41358892](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41358892/)
*Diabetes Care* · 2026 | IM02

**Key outcomes:** Non-ICU inpatient glycemic target: 100–180 mg/dL. ICU target: 140–180 mg/dL. Continuation of personal CGM during hospitalization explicitly recommended when clinically appropriate (confirmatory POC glucose required for insulin dosing). AID systems and insulin pumps should be continued under institutional protocol. Discharge diabetes self-management education established as a formal care standard.  
**Methods:** ADA Standards of Care annual update; Chapter 16 of Supplement 1. Diabetes Care 49(Suppl 1):S339–S355. Expert systematic evidence review by ADA writing committee.  
**Rehab take:** ADA 2026 provides the clinical authority MFB needs for a written inpatient CGM/AID protocol. Patients arriving on home CGM or insulin pump systems should continue them; IRF nursing staff need training and a confirmatory POC glucose workflow. Discharge glycemic education initiated on the IRF satisfies ADA 2026 and may reduce 30-day readmissions.

### 3. MEDIUM — Inpatient automated insulin delivery: feasibility confirmed, but barriers remain
PMID [41764080](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41764080/)
*Journal of Diabetes Science and Technology* · 2026 | IM02

**Key outcomes:** AIDING feasibility trial: hospitalized patients with diabetes on Omnipod 5 + Dexcom G7 AID system achieved higher time-in-range vs. conventional multiple daily insulin injections + CGM. Narrative review identifies persistent barriers: supply logistics, nursing competency training, formulary restrictions, and institutional liability concerns.  
**Rehab take:** Inpatient AID efficacy is now proven in feasibility data; MFB as an early-adopter IRF should begin developing a nursing AID protocol so complex rehab patients with labile glycemia can remain on their home AID systems safely.

### 4. MEDIUM — System-wide glucose dashboard measurably improves hypo- and hyperglycemia rates across 7 hospitals
PMID [41529840](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41529840/)
*Journal of Diabetes Science and Technology* · 2026 | IM02

**Key outcomes:** 455,303 hospital admissions analyzed (January 2018–March 2025) across a 7-hospital health system. **Rehab take:** A QI glucose dashboard at MFB could serve as an IRF-wide glycemic surveillance tool — surfacing unit-level hypo/hyperglycemia trends, benchmarking against prior months, and driving standardization of insulin protocols across nursing staff.

## IM04 — CHF / Post-MI

### 6. ⭐ HIGH — Telehealth cardiac rehab matches centre-based outcomes in heart failure (14 RCTs, N=7,371)
PMID [41597311](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41597311/)
*Medicina* · 2025 | IM04

**Key outcomes:** 14 RCTs, N=7,371 participants. Telehealth-based CR was comparable to centre-based CR on primary outcomes (six-minute walk distance and peak VO₂) and offered clinically meaningful improvements over usual care in quality of life and adherence. Access and participation significantly improved with telehealth delivery versus centre-based.  
**Methods:** PRISMA 2020 systematic review + meta-analysis of RCTs published 2020–2025. Databases searched: PubMed, Medline, CINAHL, EMBASE, Web of Science. Population: adults with heart failure. Comparator: usual care or centre-based CR. Primary outcomes: 6MWT, peak VO₂, QoL. Secondary: adherence, satisfaction, clinical events. Medicina 62(1):25, December 2025.  
**Rehab take:** Heart failure patients discharged from MFB IRF can be safely transitioned to a telehealth CR program with outcomes equivalent to traditional site-based programs. This should be incorporated into discharge planning discussions, especially when patient transport or geographic access is a barrier.

## IM05 — AFib & Anticoagulation

### 7. ⭐⭐ TOP 3 #2 — Meta-analysis of RCTs confirms early DOAC after AF-related stroke is safe
PMID [41697537](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41697537/)
*[Journal]* · February 2026 | IM05

**Key outcomes:** Meta-analysis of RCTs (search through June 2025): early anticoagulation after acute ischemic stroke in AF patients showed composite primary outcome (recurrent ischemic stroke, symptomatic ICH, or death) comparable to delayed initiation. No statistically significant increase in symptomatic ICH with early strategy. Results support early DOAC initiation in patients with predominantly mild-to-moderate ischemic stroke.  
**Methods:** Systematic review and meta-analysis of RCTs. Systematic search: PubMed, Embase, Cochrane CENTRAL, ScienceDirect (through June 2025). Population: adults with acute ischemic stroke + AF. Comparator: early vs. late anticoagulation. Outcomes: composite of recurrent ischemic stroke, symptomatic ICH, death; functional independence. Published February 2026.  
**Rehab take:** For stroke patients arriving to MFB IRF still in the acute-to-subacute anticoagulation decision window, early DOAC initiation appears safe in mild-to-moderate strokes. Confirm anticoagulation status on every AF + stroke admission; if not started, prompt neurology for a timing discussion before the window closes.

### 8. MEDIUM — Screening detects subclinical AFib but anticoagulation trials show no consistent benefit
PMID [42384436](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42384436/)
*[2026 Review]* · 2026 | IM05

**Key outcomes:** Device-detected AFib episodes ≥6 minutes elevate stroke risk by 2–2.5× versus no subclinical AF, but absolute risk remains substantially lower than clinically evident AF. Wearable screening increases detection rates without improving clinical outcomes (LOOP, NOAH-AFNET 6, ARTESiA trials). Routine oral anticoagulation for subclinical AFib showed no consistent clinical benefit across these randomized trials.  
**Rehab take:** Continuous cardiac monitoring in the IRF setting often incidentally detects brief subclinical AFib. Per this review, brief episodes in asymptomatic patients without prior clinical AF should prompt documentation and cardiology communication, but routine DOAC initiation is not yet evidence-based. Avoid reflexive anticoagulation without shared decision-making.

## IM06 — COPD / Asthma

### 9. MEDIUM — All pulmonary rehab delivery models improve COPD outcomes; mode matters less than participation
PMID [41461647](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41461647/)
*[2025]* · 2025 | IM06

**Key outcomes:** 33 studies (N=2,538); 27 studies (N=2,106) used for network meta-analysis. All delivery models (centre-based, home-based, telerehab) showed improvements in dyspnea, six-minute walk distance, and health-related QoL compared to no pulmonary rehab. Certainty of evidence rated LOW for all outcomes due to high risk of bias (22/33 studies) and imprecision.  
**Rehab take:** For COPD patients discharged from MFB IRF, any accessible form of pulmonary rehab is better than none. Connect patients to the nearest and most logistically feasible option — telerehab if distance or mobility limits centre-based attendance — rather than delaying enrollment.

## IM07 — Aspiration Pneumonia

### 10. MEDIUM — Ethnographic study reveals mealtime implementation gaps between SLP orders and bedside practice
PMID [42500843](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42500843/)
*International Journal of Language and Communication Disorders* · 2026 | IM07

**Key outcomes:** Participant observation of 10 acute stroke patients during mealtimes in the first 72 hours of admission. Barriers to implementing dysphagia management recommendations included competing nursing priorities, inadequate shift-change handoff of SLP-specific instructions, inconsistent oral hygiene practice, and improper head/trunk positioning. Facilitators included team communication and active patient/family engagement.  
**Rehab take:** Even at MFB with a strong SLP program, implementation of dysphagia precautions at mealtimes is a nursing-execution challenge. Explicit, visible nursing cues at the bedside (beyond just SLP documentation in the chart) — for positioning, diet consistency, cueing strategies, and oral care — reduce aspiration risk, particularly at shift transitions.

## IM08 — Urinary Tract Infection

### 11. MEDIUM — UA-to-culture reflex protocol drives high rates of inappropriate ASB treatment despite IDSA guidelines
PMID [40989674](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40989674/)
*Antimicrobial Stewardship & Healthcare Epidemiology* · 2025 | IM08

**Key outcomes:** Institutional UA-to-UC reflex protocol (UA positive for pyuria, leukocyte esterase, or nitrites → automatic reflex to urine culture): only 30.1% of ordered UAs triggered a UC reflex. Of UC reflex orders, >50% yielded negative results. System-wide, 45–83% of patients still received inappropriate antibiotics for asymptomatic bacteriuria (ASB) despite IDSA guideline recommendations against treatment.  
**Rehab take:** SCI patients on MFB IRF routinely have pyuria and bacteriuria without symptoms. Reflex UA→culture ordering patterns drive inappropriate antibiotic treatment and resistance. A symptom-based UTI ordering protocol (treat only symptomatic UTIs) is an actionable antimicrobial stewardship target — particularly important in an SCI population where antibiotic overuse accelerates resistant organism colonization.

## IM09 — Delirium / Dementia

### 12. ⭐ HIGH — ABCDEF bundle and family engagement independently reduce delirium incidence: meta-review of 32 SRs
PMID [41272698](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41272698/)
*Critical Care* · 2026 | IM09

**Key outcomes:** Systematic meta-review of 32 systematic reviews and meta-analyses encompassing 335 primary studies, evaluating 16 non-pharmacological interventions. ABCDEF bundle and multicomponent non-pharmacological interventions significantly reduced delirium incidence and duration. Early mobilization independently reduced delirium. Family participation in care independently reduced delirium incidence. Multicomponent interventions also associated with mortality benefits.  
**Methods:** Systematic meta-review of systematic reviews and meta-analyses. Eight databases searched from inception to August 2025. Interventions: non-pharmacological and multicomponent (Part 2 of 2-part series). Critical Care, DOI 10.1186/s13054-025-05726-8, 2026.  
**Rehab take:** IRF is ideally positioned to operationalize ABCDEF elements: structured sleep/wake cycles, daily spontaneous awakening/breathing trials (in vented patients), pain optimization, delirium screening, early mobilization (a core IRF function), and active family engagement. MFB should embed explicit delirium prevention bundles in admission protocols for high-risk populations (TBI, stroke, post-ICU, elderly).

## IM10 — Polypharmacy / Beers

### 13. ⭐⭐ TOP 3 #3 — Polypharmacy at IRF discharge independently predicts worse ADL recovery, especially in stroke and deconditioning
PMID [41408196](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41408196/)
*BMC Geriatrics* · 2025 | IM10

**Key outcomes:** N=1,903 patients aged ≥65 years in a Japanese convalescent rehabilitation hospital (April 2017–March 2024). 62.1% had polypharmacy (≥6 concurrent prescription medications) at discharge; 76.4% were aged ≥80 years. Polypharmacy at discharge was independently and negatively associated with functional recovery on FIM and Barthel Index, with the strongest effect in cerebrovascular disease and disuse syndrome groups.  
**Methods:** Retrospective observational study of older rehabilitation inpatients. Polypharmacy defined as ≥6 regular prescription medications at discharge. Functional outcomes: Functional Independence Measure (FIM) gain and Barthel Index gain. Subgroup analysis by rehabilitation diagnostic category. BMC Geriatrics, 2025.  
**Rehab take:** Every MFB IRF discharge is an opportunity to deprescribe. This study — the largest available specifically in rehabilitation inpatients — directly supports pharmacist-led medication review before discharge, targeting patients on ≥6 medications, especially those with stroke or general deconditioning where polypharmacy load most strongly correlates with worse ADL outcomes. A standing pharmacist consult on admission for all patients ≥80 years with ≥6 medications would be a high-yield quality initiative.
