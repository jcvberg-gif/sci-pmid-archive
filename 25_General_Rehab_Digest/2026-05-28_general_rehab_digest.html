⭐⭐ TOP 3 #1 ⭐ HIGH Telerehabilitation After Hip Fracture Surgery: Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis
PMID 41813424   JMIR mHealth and uHealth · 2026   GR02

Key outcomes: Harris Hip Score MD +7.42 (95% CI 5.61–9.23; p<0.001) at intervention endpoint (2 studies, n=156). Large short-term effect on basic ADL independence; small-to-moderate effect at long-term follow-up. TUG, SPPB, FIM, Barthel Index improved across pooled trials.

Methods: SR/MA. 8 studies, N=740 post-operative hip fracture patients. Databases searched through March 2025. Outcomes: Harris Hip Score, SPPB, TUG, FIM, Barthel Index, anxiety, walking ability, QoL, adherence.

Rehab take: Telerehabilitation is evidence-based for post-hip fracture recovery. At IRF discharge, assess technology readiness (smartphone/internet); document telerehab referral plan in transition note. Especially relevant for rural or mobility-limited patients.

Team take:
  • 🦿 PT — TUG and SPPB improved with telerehab; integrate remote monitoring platform into home exercise program; document baseline TUG at IRF admission and discharge.
  • ✋ OT — ADL independence effect strong short-term; incorporate telerehab platform demo into home ADL and adaptive equipment instruction before discharge.
  • 🗣️ SLP — Not primary focus; no swallowing/communication outcomes reported.
  • 🩺 Nursing — Flag technology barriers (no smartphone/internet) in discharge care plan; reinforce telerehab adherence and medication safety prior to discharge.
  • 🧠 Psychology — Remote format may improve engagement for patients with social anxiety or depression post-fracture; telerehab reduces post-discharge social isolation.

⭐⭐ TOP 3 #2 ⭐ HIGH Two-Week Elective Inpatient IRF Program Improves AM-PAC, Section GG, and Voice in Parkinson's Disease
PMC 12872888   Frontiers in Rehabilitation Sciences · 2026   GR07

Key outcomes: AM-PAC Basic Mobility improved (p<0.0001); AM-PAC Daily Activity improved (p<0.0001); Section GG self-care (p<0.001); Section GG mobility (p<0.001); Voice Handicap Index (p=0.028). Patients admitted directly from home. First US study of elective PD inpatient rehabilitation using IRF-standard metrics.

Methods: Retrospective cohort. N=37 PD patients. JFK Johnson Rehabilitation Institute, NJ (US academic IRF). 2-week interdisciplinary program: PT, OT, SLP, psychology, nursing, social work. Outcomes: AM-PAC, Section GG (IRF-PAI), VHI at admission and discharge.

Rehab take: Elective 2-week IRF admission for PD patients with functional decline is supported by gains across all standard IRF quality metrics. Consider developing a PD elective admission pathway; CMS Section GG documentation should start Day 1; VHI screening at admission warranted.

Team take:
  • 🦿 PT — Target Section GG Mobility subscales; use UPDRS-III, MiniBEST, dual-task gait speed; high-intensity gait training preferred over low-intensity.
  • ✋ OT — Section GG Self-Care and AM-PAC Daily Activity both improved; target fine motor, medication management, fall-proofing, dual-task ADLs.
  • 🗣️ SLP — VHI significant — LSVT LOUD consideration; clinical swallowing evaluation for aspiration risk; introduce AAC awareness early.
  • 🩺 Nursing — Schedule therapy during medication ON-state; fall prevention bundle essential; constipation management and sleep hygiene coaching.
  • 🧠 Psychology — Elective patients are highly motivated; reinforce goal-setting; screen for PD-related depression (GDS-15); address caregiver burnout.

⭐ HIGH TKA vs. THA: Gait Recovery Predictors in Post-Acute Inpatient Rehabilitation
PMC 11547026   Journal of Clinical Medicine · 2024 Oct 24;13(21):6381   GR01

Key outcomes: 76% THA and 72% TKA achieved ≥40% rehabilitation potential. TKA patients had significantly higher discharge pain scores vs. THA. Age + admission TUG predicted functional mobility gain in THA; non-operated knee extension strength + admission TUG predicted in TKA.

Methods: Prospective cohort. N=435 (THA n=251, TKA n=184). Kyushu University Hospital, Japan. Outcomes: FIM-motor, 10MWT, TUG, Berg Balance Scale, knee extension strength, NRS pain.

Rehab take: For TKA, prioritize non-operated quadriceps strengthening and multimodal pain management. For THA, early ambulation speed is the primary lever. Order admission TUG within 24 hours of IRF arrival for all TKA/THA patients.

Team take:
  • 🦿 PT — Standard assessment: TUG (admission/discharge), 10MWT, Berg Balance Scale, bilateral knee extension strength; non-operated quad strengthening highest-value target in TKA.
  • ✋ OT — TKA higher discharge pain; adaptive equipment (reacher, sock aid, raised toilet seat) critical for independence with lower body ADLs despite pain.
  • 🗣️ SLP — Not primary focus.
  • 🩺 Nursing — Multimodal analgesia timing with therapy sessions critical for TKA; NRS pain every shift; document analgesic timing in med-admin record.
  • 🧠 Psychology — Pain catastrophizing screening relevant for TKA patients with high discharge pain; brief CBT-pain intervention complements analgesic management.
MEDIUM Three Stages of Polytrauma Rehabilitation: SICOT Systematic Review and Recommendation
PMID 39680082   International Orthopaedics · 2025   GR03

Key outcomes: Evidence base is sparse — only 7 of 5,000+ screened studies met inclusion criteria. Three-stage framework endorsed: (1) Acute phase — ICU/acute care, early mobilization; (2) Post-acute inpatient rehabilitation (IRF) — functional recovery, multidisciplinary team; (3) Long-term community reintegration.

Methods: Systematic review + SICOT society recommendation. MEDLINE + Embase 2000–2023. 7 qualifying studies.

Rehab take: IRF represents Stage 2 in polytrauma rehabilitation. Use FIM motor + cognitive FIM to track gains; set realistic expectations for protracted recovery across all three stages.

MEDIUM Rehabilitation Recommendations for ALS: Comprehensive Clinical Practice Guidance
PMC 12693028   Journal of Clinical Medicine · 2025 Dec 4;14(23):8590   GR06

Key outcomes: Multidisciplinary ALS rehab improves functional outcomes and QoL; multidisciplinary teams associated with improved survival (4–7 months). Strongest evidence: moderate tailored exercise, early respiratory therapy, structured dysphagia/communication/spasticity management.

Methods: Narrative clinical review covering all ALS rehabilitation domains (PT, OT, respiratory, SLP, psychology, palliative care). Authors: Gratzer, Gdynia et al.

Rehab take: At ALS IRF admission order: respiratory FVC, SLP swallowing evaluation, OT adaptive equipment assessment, PT tailored exercise, psychology assessment. Palliative care consultation appropriate at any stage.

Team take:
  • 🦿 PT — Moderate tailored exercise (avoid overwork weakness); stretching for spasticity; respiratory muscle training if FVC >50%.
  • ✋ OT — Early adaptive equipment for UE function; home modification assessment; AAC device evaluation; energy conservation strategies.
  • 🗣️ SLP — Dysphagia staging (FEES/MBS preferred), dietary texture modification, AAC device introduction — essential and early.
  • 🩺 Nursing — FVC monitoring; oral secretion management; nutrition/PEG timing discussion; fall prevention.
  • 🧠 Psychology — Advance care planning facilitation; adjustment to diagnosis; existential distress — proactive early integration, not crisis referral.
MEDIUM Early Multidisciplinary ALS Rehabilitation Preserves Swallowing and Speech (Case Report)
PMC 12620117   Clinical Case Reports · 2025 Nov;13(11):e71486   GR06 GR13

Key outcomes: 49-year-old male with ALS (dysphagia, dysarthria, dyskinesia, anxiety, depression). After 45-day early multidisciplinary rehabilitation: significant improvement in swallowing and speech function, alleviation of non-motor symptoms, maintenance of motor function. Retained ability to consume soft foods at 2-year follow-up.

Methods: Case report. 45-day inpatient multidisciplinary rehab program. Shanghai Yangzhi Rehabilitation Hospital, China.

Rehab take: Early SLP swallowing evaluation at IRF admission in ALS — not only at dysphagia crisis. Non-motor symptoms (sleep, anxiety) are legitimate rehabilitation targets in ALS.

Team take:
  • 🦿 PT — Motor function maintained alongside swallowing/speech gains; avoid scheduling PT during SLP fatigue window; coordinate high-effort sessions.
  • ✋ OT — Non-motor symptom management (sleep, anxiety, ADL burden) as important as motor therapy; adaptive equipment reduces fatigue.
  • 🗣️ SLP — Early SLP involvement preserved oral feeding 2+ years; begin dysphagia treatment and communication strategy before crisis; introduce AAC options while speech is still functional.
  • 🩺 Nursing — Fatigue management and sleep hygiene education critical; schedule therapy during peak energy/medication ON-state windows.
  • 🧠 Psychology — Anxiety and depression directly addressed in multidisciplinary rehab alongside motor symptoms; psychological support is integral, not adjunctive.
MEDIUM PD-MCT Inpatient Rehab: Gait, Motor, and QoL Gains with Wearable Sensor Monitoring
PMC 12406477   Neurological Research and Practice · 2025 Sep   GR07

Key outcomes: N=43 PD patients. Gait speed, stride length, and lift height all improved (p<0.001). MiniBEST improved (p<0.001). 6-min walk distance improved (p<0.001). Dual-task gait speed improved (p=0.016). QoL improved. Novel: continuous wearable sensor gait monitoring throughout treatment.

Methods: Single-centre cohort. PD-MCT program, Philipps-University Marburg, Germany. Wearable sensors for continuous gait monitoring. Multidisciplinary: PT, OT, SLP, psychology, pharmacological optimization.

Rehab take: Dual-task gait training is a high-value, measurable PD target. MiniBEST and 6MWT should be standard PD inpatient outcomes. Wearable gait monitoring provides objective real-time data to adjust therapy intensity.

PMID 41013802   Journal of Medical Case Reports · 2025;19:450   GR08

Key outcomes: 40-year-old female with GBS, complete upper and lower limb paralysis. 4-month structured rehab (progressive resistance + functional training): significant improvement in physical function, ROM, trunk control, and ambulation independence.

MEDIUM Rheumatoid Arthritis: Rehabilitation Therapy Improves Function and Disease Activity
PMC 12535218   Cureus · 2025 Sep 18;17(9):e92627   GR09

Key outcomes: 8 RCTs, N=930. Rehabilitation + DMARDs: HAQ improved 0.3–0.6 points (clinically meaningful threshold ~0.22). DAS28-ESR/CRP decreased 0.4–0.7 points. QoL improved. Multidisciplinary programs and structured exercise show strongest effects.

Methods: Systematic review (PRISMA). Literature January 2013–July 2025 (PubMed, Cochrane, Embase). 8 RCTs, N=930. Predominantly women, mean age 50s–60s, disease duration 4–16 years.

Rehab take: Rehabilitation provides clinically meaningful functional improvement for RA patients on DMARDs. For RA IRF admissions, order joint protection education, active ROM exercises, and functional mobility goals. Note: most evidence is outpatient; IRF-specific RA evidence remains limited.

MEDIUM Early Mobilization Post-Orthopedic Surgery: Scoping Review of 5 Intervention Types
PMID 39866347   Journal of Multidisciplinary Healthcare · 2025 Jan 20   GR11

Key outcomes: 5 early mobilization types reduce LOS: (1) Motion/ROM interventions; (2) Progressive Muscle Relaxation (pain, kinesiophobia); (3) Weight-bearing (stiffness/pain reduction); (4) Positioning (reduce blood loss, increase ROM); (5) NMES — Neuromuscular Electrical Stimulation (muscle strength).

Methods: Scoping review. 8 articles, post-orthopedic surgery patients, 2019–2024.

Rehab take: Standardize early mobilization protocol (within 24–48 hours) for all orthopedic IRF admissions; integrate all 5 modalities per patient presentation.

Team take:
  • 🦿 PT — Integrate all 5 modalities per patient; prioritize weight-bearing progression and gait training from Day 1; NMES for quad activation post-TKA.
  • ✋ OT — ROM and positioning support early ADL engagement; bed mobility and transfer training from Day 1 coordinated with PT mobilization plan.
  • 🗣️ SLP — Not primary focus.
  • 🩺 Nursing — Positioning intervention (reduce postoperative blood loss) is nursing-led; coordinate early out-of-bed timing with therapy schedule; document mobility level per shift.
  • 🧠 Psychology — Kinesiophobia addressed by PMR; screen for movement-related fear in post-orthopedic patients; brief education: pain ≠ harm.
MEDIUM Weight-Bearing Protocols After Meniscal Repair: Evidence-Based Injury-Specific Guidance
PMID 40537782   Journal of Orthopaedic Surgery and Research · 2025 Jun   GR11

Key outcomes: No universal consensus on optimal weight-bearing after meniscal repair. Injury-specific protocols: stable/partial tears — PWB ~20 kg with ROM <90° × 4 weeks, then WBAT; circumferential fiber disruptions — NWB × 6 weeks with ROM <90°. Accelerated WB shows comparable failure rates with improved PROs for stable tears.

Methods: Systematic review with injury-type subgroup analysis. DOI: 10.1186/s13018-025-05988-6.

Rehab take: WB progression must be individualized by repair type. Always confirm WB orders with operating surgeon before PT progresses ambulation. NWB × 6 weeks for circumferential repairs — premature loading increases failure risk.

Team take:
  • 🦿 PT — Confirm WB status with surgeon on Day 1; do not assume WBAT; gait training with assistive device per restriction; progress quad sets/SLR before full WB; document WB compliance.
  • ✋ OT — WB restrictions affect transfers and stair navigation; train in safe transfer technique; adaptive equipment if stairs unavoidable at home.
  • 🗣️ SLP — Not applicable.
  • 🩺 Nursing — WB restriction education is nursing responsibility; document WB status prominently in care plan and whiteboard; alert therapy if patient non-compliant.
  • 🧠 Psychology — Kinesiophobia may increase non-compliance with NWB orders; address motivation and adherence barriers in patients with prolonged restrictions.
MEDIUM ERAS Rehabilitation Protocols Improve Pain and Recovery in Ankle Fracture Surgery
PMID 40497243   Therapeutics and Clinical Risk Management · 2025 Jun 5   GR14

Key outcomes: N=376 ankle fracture surgery patients. ERAS vs. conventional care: significantly reduced postoperative pain by Day 7, earlier mobilization, lower complication rates, higher patient satisfaction. ERAS components: pre-operative education, multimodal analgesia, early PT, standardized wound care.

Methods: Retrospective analysis. December 2022–December 2023.

Rehab take: ERAS protocols produce measurable improvements in pain control and mobilization timelines in fracture rehab. Coordinate analgesic timing with therapy sessions; multimodal pain management is an IRF physician order priority.

Team take:
  • 🦿 PT — ERAS supports earlier weight-bearing and gait training; use ERAS framework as basis for early mobilization protocol in ankle fracture IRF admissions.
  • ✋ OT — Standardized home safety and ADL education (ERAS component) is OT-led; ensure patient can manage wound dressing/splint at home before discharge.
  • 🗣️ SLP — Not applicable.
  • 🩺 Nursing — ERAS nursing role is central: pre-operative education, multimodal analgesia, wound care standardization, early mobility documentation. Nursing-led ERAS components demonstrated largest single-component benefit.
  • 🧠 Psychology — Pre-operative education reduces anxiety and improves post-operative cooperation; brief pre-admission education by social work/psychology valuable in ERAS model.
MEDIUM PTSD Amplifies Physical Skin Symptom Burden During and After Burn Rehabilitation
PMC 12372007   Evidence-Based Journal (MDPI) · 2025 Aug 8;6(3):43   GR15

Key outcomes: N=103 adult burn patients in inpatient rehabilitation (PTSD n=43, No PTSD n=60). PTSD patients reported significantly more physical symptoms at follow-up across all measured categories: skin dryness/xerosis (p<0.01), temperature sensitivity (p<0.01), numbness (p<0.01), skin tightness (p<0.01), sweating (p<0.05). PTSD associated with persistent physical skin symptom burden independent of burn severity.

Methods: Subgroup analysis of multicenter prospective cohort. 103 adult burn patients in IRF. Multiple German burn centers + Yale School of Medicine. IES-R ≥33 for PTSD classification.

Rehab take: PTSD amplifies physical symptom burden and likely interferes with functional recovery. Screen all burn IRF admissions for PTSD (IES-R or PCL-5) within 72 hours. Share screening results with PT/OT/Nursing at IDT rounds. Integrated psychological-somatic treatment is required, not optional.

Team take:
  • 🦿 PT — PTSD patients have higher physical symptom burden (skin tightness, temperature sensitivity) limiting ROM and exercise tolerance; adjust intensity and monitor pain response; coordinate with psychology.
  • ✋ OT — Skin tightness and numbness directly affect hand function and fine motor ADLs; expect more complex scar management in PTSD-positive burn patients; splinting compliance may be lower.
  • 🗣️ SLP — Xerostomia and sensory changes may affect oral feeding; coordinate dietary texture and oral care with nursing in PTSD-positive patients.
  • 🩺 Nursing — Document physical skin symptoms (xerosis, temperature sensitivity, tightness) per shift; escalate to psychology if IES-R ≥33; use trauma-informed care principles in all interactions.
  • 🧠 Psychology — PTSD screening within 72 hours of IRF admission is evidence-supported. Integrate trauma-focused CBT or EMDR into burn rehabilitation plan; early psychological intervention correlates with lower physical symptom chronicity. Brief check-ins at IDT rounds prevent escalation.
