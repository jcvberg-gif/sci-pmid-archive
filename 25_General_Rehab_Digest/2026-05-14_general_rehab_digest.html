MONTHLY HIGHLIGHTS — All 15 Papers by Diagnosis Bucket

GR01 — JOINT REPLACEMENT

MEDIUM 1. Baseline functional status predicts inpatient TKA rehab improvement

PMID 39849999

Journal of Rehabilitation Medicine · 2025  GR01

Key outcomes: Quartile analysis of 3-week inpatient TKA rehab: lowest-baseline patients (WOMAC, TUG, ROM, HAQ, EQ-5D-5L, NPRS) show greatest absolute functional gains; highest-baseline patients gain least — supports targeted intensity allocation.

Rehab take: Baseline-stratified therapy intensity for TKA admissions. Low-baseline patients warrant front-loaded, gait-focused programming; high-baseline patients may reach goals faster and be candidates for shorter stays.

🦿 PT — Use TUG and ROM at admission as early intensity-adjustment triggers. Program gait training volume per baseline quartile.
✋ OT — HAQ-based ADL assessment on day 1 guides adaptive equipment and discharge planning priorities.
🩺 Nursing — NPRS trajectory should inform PRN analgesic adjustments and PT/OT session timing per shift.
GR02 — HIP / FEMUR FRACTURE

⭐ HIGH 2. FEMuR III: Enhanced community multidisciplinary rehab after hip fracture shows no QoL gain vs. usual care (13-site RCT)

PMID 41424298

Health Technology Assessment · 2025  GR02

Key outcomes: No significant EQ-5D QoL improvement vs. usual NHS community rehab. Enhanced program cost £444/patient more.

Methods: Superiority multicenter RCT; 13 hospitals, England and Wales; 1:1 parallel allocation; concurrent economic and process evaluation.

Rehab take: Enhanced community rehab does not improve QoL over standard care post-hip fracture. IRF-level intensity during the acute window is where functional gains concentrate. Supports IRF admission for appropriate hip fracture patients.

🦿 PT — IRF is the high-value functional-gain window. Gait and balance goals must be well-established before community discharge.
✋ OT — Community follow-up maintains, not builds, ADL independence from IRF. Home environment assessment remains essential.
🧠 Psychology — Calibrate patient expectations about community rehab outcomes at IRF discharge. Motivation at discharge predicts community engagement.
GR04 — BURNS

⭐⭐ TOP 3 #3⭐ HIGH 3. Burns Model System: TBSA%, inhalation injury, and hand burns independently predict longer IRF stays (n=585)

PMID 40488410

Journal of Burn Care & Research · 2025  GR04GR11GR13

Key outcomes: Larger TBSA, inhalation injury, hand burn involvement, and younger age are independent predictors of longer IRF LOS. N=585 adults from Burns Model System National Database 1994–2022; mean TBSA 34.6%, 72% male, mean age 46.1 yr.

Methods: Retrospective cohort; Burns Model System National Database 1994–2022; n=585; negative binomial regression for IRF LOS predictors.

Rehab take: Use TBSA%, inhalation injury, and hand involvement as stay-length flags at IRF admission. These predictors inform extended-stay justification and early OT/SLP referrals. Patients with >30% TBSA + inhalation injury warrant full IDT engagement from day 1.

🦿 PT — TBSA and inhalation injury predict deconditioning severity; begin aerobic reconditioning immediately.
✋ OT — Hand burn involvement is an independent LOS predictor; prioritize splinting, edema control, and fine motor ADL from day 1.
🗣️ SLP — Inhalation injury flag: order dysphagia screen and voice assessment immediately (see PMID 39721235).
🩺 Nursing — Daily wound assessment documentation is foundational for stay-justification records.
🧠 Psychology — Early psychological screening recommended for TBSA >30%.
GR05 — MULTIPLE SCLEROSIS

⭐⭐ TOP 3 #2⭐ HIGH 4. ≥150 min/wk physical activity clinically reduces MS fatigue: systematic review and meta-analysis of RCTs

PMID 40167216

Physical Therapy (Oxford) · 2025  GR05GR11

Key outcomes: PA meeting guidelines (≥150 min/wk moderate aerobic OR 2×10–30 min aerobic + 2× resistance/wk) produces clinically meaningful fatigue reductions exceeding MCIDs. Fatigue in MS is associated with falls, activity limitations, pain, cognitive impairment, and mortality risk.

Methods: Systematic review + meta-analysis; 7 databases; search through Oct 2024; RCTs only meeting PA guidelines with fatigue as primary outcome.

Rehab take: Write PA orders for inpatient MS patients targeting ≥150 min/wk of moderate aerobic activity — a specific threshold-based IRF therapy order. Track cumulative weekly PA minutes as an outcome metric.

🦿 PT — Dose counts: track cumulative weekly minutes toward 150/wk threshold. Aerobic + resistance combination; monitor EDSS-adjusted tolerance.
✋ OT — Energy conservation must co-exist with PA prescription; pace ADL demands to preserve PA reserve.
🩺 Nursing — Document PA session minutes per shift; flag when weekly threshold is at risk.
🧠 Psychology — Low PA self-efficacy and fatigue catastrophizing are barriers; CBT-consistent fatigue reframing improves adherence.

⭐ HIGH 5. Multicenter pragmatic trial of standardized inpatient MS rehab documentation and mobility outcomes (Austria)

PMID 41774943

Multiple Sclerosis and Related Disorders · 2026  GR05GR11

Key outcomes: Standardized therapy catalogue enables reliable tracking of MS inpatient rehab mobility changes: MSWS-12, 2MWT, 5TSTS, T25FW. Usability confirmed by 47 physiotherapists.

Methods: Multicenter single-arm pragmatic clinical trial; Austria; therapeutic intervention catalogue from 12 video-rated sessions; System Usability Scale assessment.

Rehab take: Adopt the 4-test mobility battery (MSWS-12 + T25FW + 2MWT + 5TSTS) at MS IRF admission and discharge.

🦿 PT — Standardize 4-test mobility battery at MS admission/discharge. Enables benchmarking and therapy goal calibration.
✋ OT — Mobility ADL goals can be anchored to T25FW and 5TSTS benchmarks from this validated catalogue.
🩺 Nursing — Nursing mobility-assist documentation should align with PT mobility scale anchor language for IDT clarity.

MEDIUM 6. Hybrid telerehabilitation vs. clinic-based rehab for MS: 3-arm single-blind RCT

PMID 41106533

Archives of Physical Medicine and Rehabilitation · 2025  GR05GR11

Key outcomes: Hybrid telerehab and telerehab-only both improved gait speed (T25FW), fatigue, functional capacity, mobility, and QoL at 8 weeks; comparable to clinic-based. N=44 MS patients.

Rehab take: Hybrid telerehab is a viable step-down for MS patients transitioning from IRF. Introduce home exercise components during IRF stay to build telerehab readiness before discharge.

🦿 PT — Introduce home exercise component during IRF stay to prepare for post-discharge telerehab participation.
🧠 Psychology — Technology confidence and engagement predict telerehab success; screen during IRF stay and address digital literacy barriers.
GR07 — PARKINSON'S DISEASE

⭐⭐ TOP 3 #1⭐ HIGH 7. First US study: 2-week interdisciplinary inpatient rehab improves AM-PAC, Section GG, and voice function in Parkinson's disease

PMID 41660382

Frontiers in Rehabilitation Sciences · 2026  GR07GR11GR12GR13

Key outcomes: AM-PAC Basic Mobility improved (p<0.0001); AM-PAC Daily Activity improved (p<0.0001); Section GG self-care and mobility improved (p<0.001); Voice Handicap Index (VHI) improved significantly.

Methods: Prospective cohort; n=37 PD patients; elective 2-week acute inpatient IDT program admitted from home; first-of-kind US program; full IDT (PT/OT/SLP/Psychology/Nursing); AM-PAC and Section GG assessments at admission and discharge.

Rehab take: First US study using MDS-required instruments (AM-PAC + Section GG) demonstrating gains in an elective 2-week inpatient PD program. Supports IRF admission justification for PD patients with motor fluctuations, ADL decline, or fall risk. Order comprehensive SLP evaluation at admission.

🦿 PT — Admit with baseline UPDRS-III, gait speed, TUG. Implement PD-specific cueing strategies, dual-task training, falls-prevention PD protocol.
✋ OT — AM-PAC Daily Activity gains confirm OT benefit from day 1. Target dressing, bathing, fine motor tasks; align sessions with levodopa ON-phase.
🗣️ SLP — VHI improvement is a primary study finding; LSVT LOUD or equivalent voice-intensity protocol supported. Screen for dysphagia at admission.
🩺 Nursing — Falls prevention critical: on/off medication cycling increases fall risk during levodopa trough. Document levodopa timing; supervised transfer protocol during OFF phases.
🧠 Psychology — PD-related anxiety, depression, and MCI affect engagement. Mood and cognitive screen at admission. Patient and family psychoeducation on disease trajectory is IDT standard.

⭐ HIGH 8. Soft robotic exoskeleton (SER) gait training improves stride length and UPDRS in Parkinson's disease: RCT pilot

PMID 42048524

Journal of Medical Internet Research · 2026  GR07GR11

Key outcomes: SER group: left/right stride length each +0.15 m (p<0.001); ankle dorsiflexion +2.84° (p<0.001); UPDRS-III −2.80 pts; MoCA +1.23 pts (p<0.01).

Methods: RCT pilot; n=56 PD (experimental n=31 SER+conventional; control n=25 conventional only); 5×/wk × 20 min × 4 weeks.

Rehab take: SER-assisted gait adds measurable stride-length and ankle mobility gains above conventional PT for PD. Ankle dorsiflexion improvement addresses PD's reduced step clearance (fall risk). Consider for PD patients with significant gait impairment if exoskeleton technology is available.

🦿 PT — SER is an add-on to, not replacement for, conventional gait therapy. Ankle dorsiflexion gains may reduce freezing-of-gait frequency.
✋ OT — Improved stride and ankle mobility transfers to safer footwear and home floor navigation; include in discharge equipment checklist.
🧠 Psychology — Cognitive improvement (MoCA) in SER group; incorporate in patient motivation conversations.
GR08 — GBS / CIDP

⭐ HIGH 9. Psychological and behavioral therapy enhances neuromuscular recovery in GBS: propensity score matched study

PMID 40883445

[see PMID for full citation] · 2025  GR08GR15

Key outcomes: Psychological + behavioral intervention significantly enhanced functional independence and neuromuscular recovery vs. matched controls; greatest benefit in most severely affected patients.

Methods: Retrospective propensity score matched cohort; GBS patients (classic and variant forms); General Hospital of Western Theater Command, China; Jan 2014–Jan 2022; PI-Group vs. NI-Group.

Rehab take: Formal psychology/behavioral therapy consult should be routine at GBS IRF admission. Neurologic recovery is augmented with greatest benefit in most severely affected patients. Supports proactive psychology referral for all GBS admissions.

🦿 PT — Coordinate with psychology: kinesiophobia, pain catastrophizing, and motivational deficits affect progressive strengthening. Aligned sessions maximize recovery.
🗣️ SLP — GBS with bulbar involvement: psychological distress compounds communication and swallowing compliance. IDT huddle recommended.
🩺 Nursing — Flag high-disability-score GBS patients for early psychology referral; document engagement and motivational status.
🧠 Psychology — Formal behavioral therapy protocol is evidence-supported; set functional independence targets jointly with PT/OT.

LOW 10. Progressive resistance + functional training for GBS functional recovery: case report

[Case report · 2025]  GR08

Rehab take: Progressive resistance training is a safe, structured approach for GBS rehab — supports order-writing for progressive strengthening from IRF admission.

GR10 — SEVERE OA / DEBILITY

⭐ HIGH 11. OA year in review 2025: rehabilitation and outcomes including sex/gender reporting

PMID 41175919

Osteoarthritis and Cartilage · 2025  GR10

Key outcomes: 8 themes from Mar 2024–Mar 2025 literature: exercise effective for knee OA, variable for hip OA; digital rehab viable alternative; behavioral/mind-body add-ons show no independent benefit; electrotherapy inconsistent; orthoses help if individualized. Sex/gender disaggregated in only 5% of studies.

Methods: Systematic narrative review; 3 databases (Medline, Embase, CINAHL); Mar 2024–Mar 2025; moderate-to-high quality studies only.

Rehab take: Exercise remains the cornerstone OA intervention — especially knee OA. Digital rehab is viable for discharge continuity. Behavioral/mindfulness add-ons do not independently augment OA outcomes above exercise alone.

🦿 PT — Knee OA: exercise prescription is the evidence-based standard. Hip OA: strength + aerobic combination recommended despite weaker evidence base.
✋ OT — Orthoses should be personalized to patient preference and function — not prescribed by default.
🧠 Psychology — Focus psychological support on exercise adherence, pain reframing, and functional goal-setting.
GR11 — PT IDT BUCKET

MEDIUM 12. Early mobilization in post-orthopedic surgery patients: scoping review of effective interventions

PMID 39866347

Journal of Multidisciplinary Healthcare · 2025  GR11GR01GR02

Key outcomes: 5 effective mobilization modalities: active motion, PMR, weight-bearing, positioning, and NMES — all reduce LOS. Motion + PMR showed fastest ROM recovery (6-week benchmark). Based on 8 studies.

Rehab take: Post-orthopedic early mobilization order sets should specify all 5 modalities.

🦿 PT — Include NMES in early post-op toolkit for muscle activation when active contraction is too painful or limited.
🩺 Nursing — Limb positioning and PMR are nurse-implementable; include in shift care plans. Document positioning compliance for LOS justification.
GR13 — SLP IDT BUCKET

⭐ HIGH 13. Dysphonia after inhalation injury: 10-year retrospective audit of 167 burn patients from 2 Australian burn units

PMID 39721235

Burns · 2025  GR13GR04

Key outcomes: Dysphonia prevalence: 55% of all inhalation injury patients; 87% with severe inhalation injury. At 6 months: 98% non-severe resolved, but 27% severe cohort had persistent dysphonia.

Methods: Retrospective 10-year audit; 2 Australian burn units; n=167 with confirmed inhalation injury; 75% male, mean age 45 yr, mean TBSA 23%, 11% tracheostomy, mean intubation 7 days.

Rehab take: Order formal voice assessment and SLP evaluation at IRF admission for all patients with documented inhalation injury. Severe grade = 27% risk of persistent dysphonia at 6 months.

🗣️ SLP — Dual screen (voice + swallow) at admission for all inhalation injury burn patients. Severe inhalation injury: 27% persistent dysphonia at 6 months — set long-term voice rehab goals. Order VFSS if dysphagia suspected.
🩺 Nursing — Document inhalation injury severity grade at admission; tracheostomy history is an independent SLP-referral flag.
🧠 Psychology — Voice loss amplifies body-image and identity distress; co-time psychology engagement with SLP voice rehabilitation initiation.
GR14 — NURSING IDT BUCKET

MEDIUM 14. EHR-based education for non-pharmacological pain care: RCT mixed-method evaluation in inpatient nurses

PMID 40674717

JMIR Nursing · 2025  GR14GR01GR02

Key outcomes: EHR-integrated (Epic) NPPC education with CDS prompts was feasible and acceptable to inpatient nurses (n=29). Competing clinical demands were the primary barrier.

Rehab take: Formalize non-pharmacological pain management documentation in IRF nursing notes. EHR CDS prompts for NPPC techniques improve consistency and defensible documentation alongside pharmacological analgesia.

🩺 Nursing — Add NPPC technique documentation to pain assessment nursing notes; standardize which non-pharmacological options are attempted before/alongside PRN analgesics.
🧠 Psychology — Psychological NPPC strategies should be reinforced by nursing between formal psychology sessions.
GR15 — PSYCHOLOGY IDT BUCKET

MEDIUM 15. Mindfulness-based interventions for burn survivors: systematic review of psychosocial outcomes (12 studies)

PMID 40407681

Trauma (MDPI) · 2025  GR15GR04

Key outcomes: 12 studies (6 cross-sectional, 6 interventional): mindfulness associated with reduced PTSD, depression, and anxiety; improved emotional regulation and social reintegration in burn survivors. Mindfulness reduces perceived pain intensity.

Rehab take: Mindfulness-based interventions (MBI) are a validated adjunct for psychological care in burn patients. Integrate structured MBI components into psychology sessions during IRF burn admissions.

🧠 Psychology — Evidence-supported MBI protocol (mindfulness meditation, yoga, self-compassion training) addresses PTSD, depression, body-image adaptation, and pain in burn patients. Initiate early in IRF stay.
🩺 Nursing — Brief nurse-delivered mindfulness techniques (breathing exercises, grounding) reinforce psychology's MBI work between formal sessions.
