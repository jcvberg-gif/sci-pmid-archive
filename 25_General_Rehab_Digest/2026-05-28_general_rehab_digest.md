### 1. ⭐⭐ TOP 3 #1 · ⭐ HIGH — **Telerehabilitation After Hip Fracture Surgery: Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis**
PMID [41813424](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41813424/)  
*JMIR mHealth and uHealth* · 2026 | **GR02**

**Key outcomes:** Harris Hip Score MD +7.42 (95% CI 5.61–9.23; p<0.001) at intervention endpoint (2 studies, n=156). Large short-term effect on basic ADL independence; small-to-moderate effect at long-term follow-up. Functional mobility (TUG, SPPB) and FIM/Barthel improved across pooled trials.  
**Methods:** Systematic review and meta-analysis. 8 studies, N=740 post-operative hip fracture patients. Databases: PubMed, Cochrane, Embase, Web of Science (inception–March 2025). Outcomes: Harris Hip Score, SPPB, TUG, FIM, Barthel Index, anxiety, walking ability, QoL, adherence.  
**Rehab take:** Telerehabilitation is evidence-based for post-hip fracture recovery and a viable discharge option when outpatient access is limited. At IRF discharge, assess technology readiness (smartphone/internet access); document telerehab referral plan in transition note. Especially relevant for rural or mobility-limited post-fracture patients.  
**Team take:**
  - 🦿 **PT** — TUG and SPPB improved with telerehab; integrate remote monitoring platform into discharge home exercise program; document baseline TUG at IRF admission and discharge as outcome anchor.
  - ✋ **OT** — ADL independence effect strong short-term; incorporate telerehab platform demo and training into home ADL and adaptive equipment instruction before discharge.
  - 🗣️ **SLP** — Not primary focus; no swallowing/communication outcomes reported in this review.
  - 🩺 **Nursing** — Flag technology access barriers (no smartphone, no internet) in discharge care plan; reinforce telerehab adherence and medication safety with patient and caregiver prior to discharge.
  - 🧠 **Psychology** — Remote format may improve engagement for patients with social anxiety, limited mobility, or depression post-fracture; telerehab can reduce post-discharge social isolation.

### 2. ⭐⭐ TOP 3 #2 · ⭐ HIGH — **Two-Week Elective Inpatient IRF Program Improves AM-PAC, Section GG, and Voice in Parkinson's Disease**
PMC [12872888](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12872888/)  
*Frontiers in Rehabilitation Sciences* · 2026; DOI: 10.3389/fresc.2025.1672759 | **GR07**

**Key outcomes:** AM-PAC Basic Mobility improved (p<0.0001); AM-PAC Daily Activity improved (p<0.0001); Section GG self-care improved (p<0.001); Section GG mobility improved (p<0.001); Voice Handicap Index (VHI) improved (p=0.028). Patients admitted directly from home — not post-acute crisis. First US published study of elective PD inpatient rehabilitation using IRF-standard metrics.  
**Methods:** Retrospective cohort. N=37 PD patients admitted to suburban academic US IRF (JFK Johnson Rehabilitation Institute, NJ). 2-week interdisciplinary program: PT, OT, SLP, psychology, nursing, social work. Assessments: AM-PAC, Section GG (IRF-PAI), VHI at admission and discharge.  
**Rehab take:** Elective 2-week IRF admission for PD patients with functional decline is supported by measurable gains across all standard IRF quality metrics (Section GG, AM-PAC). Consider developing a PD-specific elective admission pathway to enable referral before crisis-level deterioration. CMS Section GG documentation should start Day 1. VHI screening at admission warranted.  
**Team take:**
  - 🦿 **PT** — Target Section GG Mobility subscales (sit-to-stand, ambulation distance, stairs); use UPDRS-III, MiniBEST, and dual-task gait speed as PD-specific assessment tools; high-intensity gait training preferred over low-intensity.
  - ✋ **OT** — Section GG Self-Care and AM-PAC Daily Activity both improved; target fine motor coordination, medication management, fall-proofing home environment, dual-task ADLs (walking + carrying).
  - 🗣️ **SLP** — VHI statistically significant — consider LSVT LOUD protocol for vocal intensity; also perform clinical swallowing evaluation (PD patients at high aspiration risk); introduce AAC awareness early.
  - 🩺 **Nursing** — PD-specific: schedule therapy during medication ON-state; fall prevention bundle essential; constipation management and sleep hygiene coaching in patient education.
  - 🧠 **Psychology** — Elective admission patients are highly motivated; reinforce goal-setting and quality-of-life framing; screen for PD-related depression (GDS-15); address caregiver burnout if applicable.

### 3. MEDIUM — **MS Inpatient Rehab Goal Documentation Validated in Austrian Multicenter Trial**
PMID [41774943](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41774943/)  
*Multiple Sclerosis and Related Disorders* · 2026 | **GR05**

**Key outcomes:** Therapeutic documentation catalogue and manual for MS inpatient mobility rehabilitation developed and validated. Moderate interrater reliability on video-reviewed PT sessions. Usability rated high by 47 physiotherapists (System Usability Scale). Mobility outcomes tracked: MSWS-12, 2-Minute Walk Test (2MWT), Five Times Sit-to-Stand (5-TSTS), Timed 25-Foot Walk (T25FW).  
**Methods:** Multicentre single-arm pragmatic clinical trial, Austria. Documentation tool development for PT intervention content, goals, and outcomes in MS inpatient rehab. Usability assessment by 47 PTs.  
**Rehab take:** Standardized documentation of MS PT intervention content and goals improves consistency and quality measurement across inpatient rehab. Consider adopting 2MWT, 5-TSTS, and T25FW as standard MS mobility outcome measures for PT at Mary Free Bed IRF.

### 4. ⭐ HIGH — **TKA vs. THA: Gait Recovery Predictors in Post-Acute Inpatient Rehabilitation**
PMC [11547026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11547026/)  
*Journal of Clinical Medicine* · 2024 Oct 24;13(21):6381 | **GR01**

**Key outcomes:** 76% THA and 72% TKA patients achieved ≥40% rehabilitation potential. TKA patients had significantly higher discharge pain scores than THA. Age and admission TUG predicted functional mobility gain in THA; non-operated knee extension strength and admission TUG predicted in TKA.  
**Methods:** Prospective cohort. N=435 (THA n=251, TKA n=184). Kyushu University Hospital, Japan. Outcomes: FIM-motor, 10MWT, TUG, Berg Balance Scale, knee extension strength, NRS pain.  
**Rehab take:** For TKA, prioritize non-operated quadriceps strengthening and multimodal pain management to optimize functional gain. For THA, early ambulation speed (TUG) is the primary lever. Order admission TUG within 24 hours of IRF arrival for all TKA/THA patients — it is the top independent predictor of functional gain.  
**Team take:**
  - 🦿 **PT** — Standard assessment: TUG (admission/discharge), 10MWT, Berg Balance Scale, bilateral knee extension strength; non-operated quad strengthening is highest-value PT target in TKA.
  - ✋ **OT** — TKA patients have higher discharge pain; adaptive equipment (reacher, sock aid, raised toilet seat) critical for maintaining independence with lower body ADLs despite pain.
  - 🗣️ **SLP** — Not primary focus of this study.
  - 🩺 **Nursing** — Multimodal analgesia coordination with therapy session timing particularly important for TKA; NRS pain every shift; document analgesic timing in med-admin record.
  - 🧠 **Psychology** — Pain catastrophizing screening relevant for TKA patients with high discharge pain; brief CBT-pain intervention can complement analgesic management.

### 5. MEDIUM — **Three Stages of Polytrauma Rehabilitation: SICOT Systematic Review and Recommendation**
PMID [39680082](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39680082/)  
*International Orthopaedics* · 2025 | **GR03**

**Key outcomes:** Evidence base for polytrauma rehabilitation is sparse — only 7 of 5,000+ screened studies met inclusion criteria. Three-stage framework endorsed: (1) Acute phase (ICU/acute care, early mobilization, pain/delirium management); (2) Post-acute/inpatient rehabilitation (IRF — functional recovery, multidisciplinary team); (3) Long-term community reintegration. SICOT recommends structured evidence-based approach across all stages.  
**Methods:** Systematic review + SICOT society recommendation. MEDLINE + Embase 2000–2023. N=7 studies met full inclusion criteria.  
**Rehab take:** IRF represents the critical Stage 2 in polytrauma rehabilitation. Despite sparse evidence, SICOT endorses systematic multidisciplinary IRF care. Use FIM motor + cognitive FIM to track gains; set realistic family/patient expectations for protracted recovery across all three stages.

### 6. MEDIUM — **Rehabilitation Recommendations for ALS: Comprehensive Clinical Practice Guidance**
PMC [12693028](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12693028/)  
*Journal of Clinical Medicine* · 2025 Dec 4;14(23):8590 | **GR06**

**Key outcomes:** Multidisciplinary ALS rehabilitation (PT, OT, respiratory, SLP, psychology, palliative care) improves functional outcomes and QoL. Multidisciplinary teams associated with improved survival (4–7 months). Strongest evidence: moderate tailored exercise, early respiratory therapy, structured dysphagia/communication/spasticity management.  
**Methods:** Narrative clinical review covering all ALS rehabilitation domains. Authors: Gratzer, Gdynia et al.  
**Rehab take:** ALS IRF admissions require a staged multidisciplinary approach with explicit goals at each disease stage. At IRF admission order: respiratory FVC, SLP swallowing evaluation, OT adaptive equipment assessment, PT tailored exercise prescription, psychology assessment. Palliative care consultation appropriate at any stage.  
**Team take:**
  - 🦿 **PT** — Moderate tailored exercise (avoid overwork weakness); stretching for spasticity management; respiratory muscle training if FVC >50%; aquatic therapy consideration.
  - ✋ **OT** — Early adaptive equipment for UE function; home modification assessment; AAC device evaluation; energy conservation strategies.
  - 🗣️ **SLP** — Dysphagia staging (FEES/MBS preferred), dietary texture modification, AAC device introduction — essential and early; coordinate with OT for communication-augmentation devices.
  - 🩺 **Nursing** — FVC monitoring for respiratory decline trending; oral secretion management; nutrition/PEG timing discussion with care team; fall prevention.
  - 🧠 **Psychology** — Advance care planning facilitation; adjustment to diagnosis; existential distress common — proactive early integration, not crisis referral.

### 7. MEDIUM — **Early Multidisciplinary ALS Rehabilitation Preserves Swallowing and Speech (Case Report)**
PMC [12620117](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12620117/)  
*Clinical Case Reports* · 2025 Nov;13(11):e71486; DOI: 10.1002/ccr3.71486 | **GR06 · GR13**

**Key outcomes:** 49-year-old male with ALS presenting with dysphagia, dysarthria, dyskinesia, anxiety, and depression. After 45-day early multidisciplinary rehabilitation: significant improvement in swallowing and speech function, alleviation of non-motor symptoms (sleep, anxiety, depression), maintenance of motor function. Retained ability to consume soft foods at 2-year follow-up.  
**Methods:** Case report. 45-day inpatient multidisciplinary rehab program. Shanghai Yangzhi Rehabilitation Hospital, China.  
**Rehab take:** Early multidisciplinary rehab in ALS preserves functional communication and oral feeding longer than late-stage-only intervention. Initiate SLP swallowing evaluation and speech therapy at IRF admission, not only at dysphagia crisis. Non-motor symptoms (sleep, anxiety) are legitimate rehabilitation targets.  
**Team take:**
  - 🦿 **PT** — Motor function maintained alongside swallowing/speech gains; avoid scheduling PT during SLP fatigue window; coordinate high-effort sessions.
  - ✋ **OT** — Non-motor symptom management (sleep, anxiety, ADL burden) as important as motor therapy in ALS; adaptive equipment reduces fatigue.
  - 🗣️ **SLP** — Early SLP involvement in ALS preserved oral feeding 2+ years post-intervention; begin dysphagia treatment and communication strategy introduction before crisis; introduce AAC options early, while speech is still functional.
  - 🩺 **Nursing** — Fatigue management and sleep hygiene education critical; schedule therapy during peak energy/medication ON-state windows.
  - 🧠 **Psychology** — Anxiety and depression directly addressed in multidisciplinary rehab alongside motor symptoms; psychological support is integral, not adjunctive, to ALS rehabilitation.

### 8. MEDIUM — **Multidisciplinary PD Inpatient Care: Gait, Motor, and QoL Gains with Wearable Sensor Monitoring**
PMC [12406477](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12406477/)  
*Neurological Research and Practice* · 2025 Sep | **GR07**

**Key outcomes:** N=43 PD patients in German PD-MCT inpatient program. Gait speed, stride length, and lift height improved (p<0.001). MiniBEST improved (p<0.001). 6-min walk distance improved (p<0.001). Dual-task gait speed improved (p=0.016). QoL improved. Novel: continuous wearable sensor gait monitoring throughout treatment.  
**Methods:** Single-centre cohort study. PD-MCT program, Philipps-University Marburg, Germany. Wearable sensors for continuous gait monitoring. Multidisciplinary: PT, OT, SLP, psychology, pharmacological optimization.  
**Rehab take:** PD-MCT framework with wearable gait sensors provides objective real-time data to adjust therapy intensity. Dual-task gait training (walking + cognitive/manual task) is a high-value, measurable PD target. MiniBEST and 6MWT should be standard PD inpatient outcomes.

### 9. MEDIUM — **GBS: Structured Inpatient Rehabilitation with Progressive Resistance Training**
PMID [41013802](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41013802/)  
*Journal of Medical Case Reports* · 2025;19:450 | **GR08**

**Key outcomes:** 40-year-old female with GBS, complete upper and lower limb paralysis with spasticity. 4-month structured inpatient rehab (progressive resistance training + functional training): significant improvement in physical function, ROM, trunk control, and ambulation independence.  
**Methods:** Case report. Program sequence: (1) inhibit tightness/spasticity, (2) activate weak muscles, (3) improve ROM and proprioception, (4) develop trunk control, (5) advance to functional training. Gulf Medical University, UAE.  
**Rehab take:** GBS recovery is protracted (4+ months in moderate-severe cases); progressive resistance training is an evidence-compatible approach. Set realistic expectations for long recovery trajectory with patient/family from IRF admission. Use MRC Scale and ONLS to track motor recovery and disability over time.

### 10. MEDIUM — **Rheumatoid Arthritis: Rehabilitation Therapy Improves Function and Disease Activity**
PMC [12535218](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12535218/)  
*Cureus* · 2025 Sep 18;17(9):e92627; DOI: 10.7759/cureus.92627 | **GR09**

**Key outcomes:** 8 RCTs, N=930. Rehabilitation (exercise/PT) added to DMARDs vs. DMARDs alone: HAQ improved by 0.3–0.6 points (clinically meaningful threshold ~0.22). DAS28-ESR/CRP decreased 0.4–0.7 points. QoL improved. Multidisciplinary programs and structured exercise show strongest effects.  
**Methods:** Systematic review, PRISMA. Literature January 2013–July 2025 (PubMed, Cochrane, Embase). 8 RCTs included. N=930 predominantly female participants, mean age 50s–60s, disease duration 4–16 years.  
**Rehab take:** Rehabilitation provides clinically meaningful functional and disease activity improvement for RA patients on DMARDs. For RA IRF admissions, order joint protection education, active ROM exercises, and functional mobility goals. Note: most evidence is outpatient RA; IRF-specific RA evidence remains limited — clinical judgment essential.

### 11. MEDIUM — **OA Year in Review 2025: Rehabilitation Evidence and Sex/Gender Reporting Gaps**
PMID [41175919](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41175919/)  
*Osteoarthritis and Cartilage* · 2025 | **GR10**

**Key outcomes:** 8 rehabilitation themes for OA: (1) exercise effective for knee OA; (2) diet+exercise superior for weight loss; (3) digital rehab viable alternative; (4) mind/behavioral treatments alone show minimal benefit; (5) electrotherapy effects inconsistent; (6) individualized orthoses effective; (7) acupuncture and blood flow restriction (BFR) training showing effectiveness; (8) treatment packages variable. Individualized orthoses are supported. Document sex/gender in outcomes per emerging reporting standards; consider sex-stratified outcome analysis in departmental QI.

### 12. MEDIUM — **Early Mobilization Post-Orthopedic Surgery: Scoping Review of 5 Intervention Types**
PMID [39866347](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39866347/)  
*Journal of Multidisciplinary Healthcare* · 2025 Jan 20 | **GR11**

**Key outcomes:** 5 early mobilization intervention types identified across 8 studies: (1) Motion/ROM interventions (increase ROM); (2) Progressive Muscle Relaxation (reduce pain, short-term kinesiophobia); (3) Weight-bearing interventions (reduce stiffness and pain); (4) Positioning interventions (reduce postoperative blood loss, increase ROM); (5) NMES — Neuromuscular Electrical Stimulation (increase muscle strength). All 5 types associated with reduced hospital LOS.  
**Methods:** Scoping review. Databases: EBSCOhost, PubMed, Sage, ScienceDirect. 8 articles included. Post-orthopedic surgery patients, 2019–2024.  
**Rehab take:** Early weight-bearing, ROM, and NMES are all evidence-supported and complementary post-orthopedic mobilization strategies that shorten LOS. Standardize early mobilization protocol (within 24–48 hours) for all orthopedic IRF admissions.  
**Team take:**
  - 🦿 **PT** — Integrate all 5 modalities per patient presentation; prioritize weight-bearing progression and gait training from Day 1 post-orthopedic admission; NMES for quad activation post-TKA.
  - ✋ **OT** — ROM and positioning interventions support early ADL engagement; bed mobility and transfer training from Day 1 coordinated with PT mobilization plan.
  - 🗣️ **SLP** — Not primary focus.
  - 🩺 **Nursing** — Positioning intervention (reduce postoperative blood loss) is a nursing-led component; coordinate early out-of-bed timing with therapy schedule; document mobility level per shift.
  - 🧠 **Psychology** — Kinesiophobia (fear of movement) addressed by Progressive Muscle Relaxation; screen for movement-related fear in post-orthopedic patients; brief education on pain ≠ harm.

### 13. MEDIUM — **Weight-Bearing Protocols After Meniscal Repair: Evidence-Based Injury-Specific Guidance**
PMID [40537782](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40537782/)  
*Journal of Orthopaedic Surgery and Research* · 2025 Jun | **GR11**

**Key outcomes:** No universal consensus on optimal weight-bearing after meniscal repair. Injury-specific protocols recommended: (1) Stable/partial tears: partial weight-bearing (PWB ~20 kg) with ROM <90° × 4 weeks, then WBAT; (2) Circumferential fiber disruptions: NWB × 6 weeks with ROM <90°. Accelerated weight-bearing protocols show comparable failure rates with improved patient-reported outcomes for stable tears in some series.  
**Methods:** Systematic review with injury-type subgroup analysis. J Orthop Surg Res. DOI: 10.1186/s13018-025-05988-6.  
**Rehab take:** Weight-bearing progression for meniscal repair must be individualized by repair type and tear pattern. Always confirm weight-bearing orders with the operating surgeon before PT progresses ambulation. NWB × 6 weeks for circumferential repairs is standard; premature weight-bearing increases failure risk.  
**Team take:**
  - 🦿 **PT** — Confirm WB status with surgeon on Day 1; do not assume WBAT; gait training with assistive device per WB restriction; progress quad sets/SLR before full WB; document WB compliance.
  - ✋ **OT** — WB restrictions affect transfers and stair navigation; train patient in safe transfer technique per restriction; adaptive equipment if stairs unavoidable at home.
  - 🗣️ **SLP** — Not applicable to this orthopedic topic.
  - 🩺 **Nursing** — Post-operative WB restriction patient education is a nursing responsibility; document WB status prominently in care plan and whiteboard; alert therapy team if patient non-compliant.
  - 🧠 **Psychology** — Kinesiophobia may increase non-compliance with weight-bearing restrictions; address motivation and adherence barriers in patients with prolonged NWB orders.

### 14. MEDIUM — **ERAS Rehabilitation Protocols Improve Pain and Recovery in Ankle Fracture Surgery**
PMID [40497243](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40497243/)  
*Therapeutics and Clinical Risk Management* · 2025 Jun 5 | **GR14**

**Key outcomes:** N=376 ankle fracture surgery patients (December 2022–December 2023). ERAS group vs. conventional care: significantly reduced postoperative pain by Day 7, earlier mobilization, lower complication rates, higher patient satisfaction. ERAS included pre-operative education, multimodal analgesia, early PT, and standardized wound care protocols.  
**Methods:** Retrospective analysis. 376 patients, ankle fracture surgery. ERAS vs. conventional care comparison.  
**Rehab take:** ERAS protocols applied to ankle fracture patients produce measurable improvements in pain control and mobilization timelines — directly applicable to fracture rehabilitation admissions. Coordinate analgesic timing with therapy sessions; multimodal pain management is an IRF physician order priority.  
**Team take:**
  - 🦿 **PT** — ERAS supports earlier weight-bearing and gait training; use ERAS framework as basis for early mobilization protocol in ankle fracture IRF admissions.
  - ✋ **OT** — Standardized home safety and ADL education (part of ERAS protocol) is OT-led; ensure patient can manage wound dressing/splint at home before discharge.
  - 🗣️ **SLP** — Not applicable.

### 15. MEDIUM — **PTSD Amplifies Physical Skin Symptom Burden During and After Burn Rehabilitation**
PMC [12372007](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12372007/)  
*Evidence-Based Journal (MDPI)* · 2025 Aug 8;6(3):43; DOI: 10.3390/ebj6030043 | **GR15**

**Key outcomes:** N=103 adult burn patients in inpatient rehabilitation (PTSD n=43, No PTSD n=60; classified by IES-R and clinical evaluation). Patients with PTSD reported significantly more physical symptoms at follow-up across ALL measured categories: skin dryness/xerosis (p<0.01), temperature sensitivity — cold and heat (p<0.01), numbness (p<0.01), skin tightness (p<0.01), increased sweating (p<0.05). PTSD associated with persistent physical skin symptom burden even after controlling for burn severity.  
**Methods:** Subgroup analysis of multicenter prospective cohort. 103 adult burn patients in IRF. Institutions: multiple German burn centers + Yale School of Medicine. IES-R ≥33 used for PTSD classification. Skin symptom assessment via standardized instrument.  
**Rehab take:** PTSD is not only a psychological comorbidity in burn rehabilitation — it amplifies physical symptom burden and likely interferes with functional recovery. Screen all burn IRF admissions for PTSD (IES-R or PCL-5) within the first 72 hours. Integrated psychological-somatic treatment planning is required, not optional. Share PTSD screening results with PT/OT/Nursing at IDT rounds.  
**Team take:**
  - 🦿 **PT** — PTSD patients have higher physical symptom burden (skin tightness, temperature sensitivity) that may limit ROM and exercise tolerance; adjust intensity and monitor for heightened pain response; coordinate with psychology.
  - ✋ **OT** — Skin tightness and numbness directly affect hand function and fine motor ADLs; expect more complex scar management and functional limitation in PTSD-positive burn patients; daily splinting compliance may be lower.
  - 🗣️ **SLP** — Xerostomia and sensory changes may affect oral feeding and communication; coordinate dietary texture and oral care with nursing in PTSD-positive patients.
  - 🩺 **Nursing** — Document physical skin symptoms (xerosis, temperature sensitivity, tightness) per shift; escalate to psychology if IES-R ≥33; use trauma-informed care principles in all interactions.
  - 🧠 **Psychology** — PTSD screening within 72 hours of IRF admission is supported by this evidence. Integrate trauma-focused CBT or EMDR into burn rehabilitation plan; early psychological intervention correlates with lower physical symptom chronicity. Brief psychological check-ins at IDT rounds prevent escalation.
