1. ⭐⭐ TOP 3 #1 ⭐ HIGH Two-Week Interdisciplinary Inpatient Rehab Produces Significant Functional and Speech Gains in Parkinson's Disease

PMC PMC12872888  |  Frontiers in Rehabilitation Sciences · 2026  GR07

Key outcomes: AM-PAC Basic Mobility and Daily Activity improved significantly (p<0.0001) admission to discharge; Section GG self-care and mobility improved (p<0.001); Voice Handicap Index improved (p=0.028). No adverse events.

Methods: Prospective study. N=37 PD patients, 2-week elective acute inpatient multidisciplinary rehabilitation at JFK Johnson Rehabilitation Institute (New Jersey). Assessments: AM-PAC, Section GG IRF-PAI, VHI.

Rehab take: Two-week elective IRF admission for PD is safe and produces meaningful functional and voice outcomes on standard IRF tools (AM-PAC, Section GG, VHI). Directly supports admission criteria justification and goal-setting.

🦿 PT · Daily PT with cueing strategies and gait training drove AM-PAC Basic Mobility gains; Parkinson's-specific cueing and fall prevention are PT core responsibilities.

✋ OT · AM-PAC Daily Activity gains confirm ADL training effectiveness; focus on fine motor tasks, instrumental ADLs, and home modification planning.

🗣️ SLP · Statistically significant VHI improvement is a standout; LSVT LOUD or phonatory intensity exercises within a 2-week IRF window produce measurable voice outcomes.

🩺 Nursing · Dopaminergic medication timing optimization before therapy and Parkinson's-specific fall prevention protocols are key nursing contributions.

🧠 Psychology · Depression and apathy are common in PD and affect therapy participation; brief psychiatric screening on admission is warranted.

2. ⭐ HIGH Standardized Early Weight-Bearing Starting 2–3 Days Post-Op Improves Harris Hip Score, FIM, and QOL in Intertrochanteric Fractures — RCT

PMC PMC13104271  |  BMC Geriatrics · 2026  GR02 GR11

Key outcomes: Early WB group: significantly better Harris Hip Score, lower VAS pain, higher FIM, better QOL at discharge and 1-month follow-up vs. conventional 4-week delayed WB. Exercise adherence higher in early group.

Methods: RCT. N=54 older adults (27/27). Intervention: standardized early WB starting day 2–3 post-op. Control: WB initiation at 4 weeks. Outcomes: Harris Hip Score, VAS, FIM, QOL, adherence.

Rehab take: WB precautions for intertrochanteric fractures should be reviewed with orthopedics at each admission. Early WB (day 2–3) following intramedullary fixation appears safe and superior to 4-week delay in older adults. Consider protocol revision for WBAT orders.

🦿 PT · Early WB protocol driver; progressive ambulation from day 2–3 post-op; gait training frequency correlated with FIM improvement.

✋ OT · Earlier mobilization enables earlier ADL engagement; bed-to-chair, toilet transfers, LE dressing achievable sooner with early WB.

🩺 Nursing · Fall prevention must accompany early WB orders; pre-therapy pain management timing matters for participation.

🧠 Psychology · Fear of falling and pain catastrophizing are barriers to early WB adherence; brief motivational support improves compliance.

3. ⭐ HIGH Brain-Computer Interface With FES and Virtual Reality Targets Gait in Multiple Sclerosis — Pilot Trial Shows Sustained Motor Gains to Week 37

PMC PMC12812722  |  Frontiers in Medicine · 2026  GR05

Key outcomes: 24/26 completed 30 BCI sessions. Gait and motor outcomes improved at week 13, partially maintained at weeks 17 and 37. All MS subtypes included (RRMS 37.5%, PPMS 33.3%, SPMS 16.7%).

Methods: Pilot single-arm trial. N=26 persons with MS (24 completers), mean age 54. Motor imagery BCI + FES + VR avatar targeting gait. 30 sessions over 13 weeks.

Rehab take: BCI-FES-VR is an emerging neuroplasticity-targeting technology for MS motor rehabilitation. Not yet standard-of-care, but progressive MS subtypes showed engagement and durable improvement. Relevant for program development planning.

🦿 PT · BCI-driven gait rehab adds neuroplasticity mechanisms to standard PT; motor imagery and FES augmentation are growing evidence for MS PT practice.

✋ OT · BCI interfaces require upper extremity control and cognition; OT assessment of hand function and processing speed may be a prerequisite for BCI candidacy.

🧠 Psychology · Technology acceptance and belief in motor imagery effectiveness influence BCI engagement; psychoeducation may improve adherence.

GR01 — Joint Replacement

4. MEDIUM Worse Baseline Function Predicts Greater IRF Gains After TKA — Ceiling Effects Limit Benefits for Best-Functioning Patients

PMID 39849999  |  Journal of Rehabilitation Medicine · 2025  GR01

Key outcomes: Lowest baseline quartile showed greatest absolute improvements in WOMAC, HAQ, EQ-5D-5L, ROM, and TUG during 3-week IRF. Highest-functioning quartile showed ceiling effects with minimal change.

Methods: Retrospective observational. N=329 (63.8% female, mean age 68.25) undergoing 3-week IRF after TKA in Austria. Assessed with NPRS, HAQ, EQ-5D-5L, WOMAC, ROM, TUG at admission and discharge. Quartile group comparison.

Rehab take: IRF for TKA is most impactful for patients with poor baseline function. Frame IRF goals and LOS justification around baseline quartile — patients with low preoperative function benefit most from IRF intensity.

🦿 PT · ROM and TUG improvement greatest in lowest baseline quartile; tailor goal-setting to starting ROM and gait status rather than fixed protocols.

✋ OT · HAQ improvements significant in lowest baseline group; prioritize most functionally limited patients for most intensive ADL intervention.

GR03 — Polytrauma

5. MEDIUM Acute Polytrauma Rehabilitation Integrated Into Trauma Systems Reduces Immobilization Complications and Improves Return-to-Work — UK Major Trauma Network Review

PMC PMC12716797  |  Cureus · 2025  GR03

Key outcomes: APR reduces immobilization complications, improves functional independence, enhances return-to-work, and may shorten LOS with projected long-term cost savings.

Methods: Literature review. Synthesized systematic reviews, clinical audits, European and UK trauma guidelines, and observational studies. Reference to London's Major Trauma System. Published December 2025.

Rehab take: Early rehabilitation integration into trauma admission pathways is supported. Coordination with acute trauma services for early IRF referral is a system-level priority that reduces downstream complications.

🦿 PT · Early mobilization post-polytrauma reduces immobilization complications; PT involvement from acute trauma phase optimizes functional trajectory at IRF.

✋ OT · ADL retraining and adaptive equipment needs emerge early; early OT consultation in trauma settings is evidenced to improve IRF readiness.

🩺 Nursing · Nursing-driven mobilization protocols in trauma settings are a key implementation lever for early rehabilitation integration.

6. MEDIUM SICOT 3-Stage Polytrauma Rehabilitation Framework: Acute Stabilization → Functional Restoration → Community Reintegration — Systematic Review

PMID 39680082  |  International Orthopaedics · 2025  GR03

Key outcomes: 5,212 studies screened; 7 included. Three-stage framework: (1) acute stabilization/early mobilization; (2) intensive functional restoration (IRF scope); (3) community reintegration. Structured SICOT recommendations for each stage.

Methods: Systematic literature review with clinical recommendations on behalf of SICOT. Covers acute, post-acute, and long-term rehabilitation stages.

Rehab take: Use this 3-stage framework to structure IRF polytrauma admission goals. Stage 2 maps directly to IRF scope. Community reintegration goals should be embedded in discharge planning from admission day 1.

🦿 PT · Stages 2–3 feature gait restoration, progressive strengthening, and RTW capacity; community ambulation goals set at IRF admission.

🩺 Nursing · Stage 1 nursing priorities (PU prevention, DVT prophylaxis, nutrition) directly inform IRF admission nursing care plans for polytrauma.

GR04 — Burns

7. MEDIUM Burns Model System Database Identifies TBSA, Lower Extremity Burns, and Pre-Injury Disability as Key Predictors of IRF Length of Stay

PMID 40488410  |  Journal of Burn Care & Research · 2025  GR04

Key outcomes: Larger TBSA, lower extremity burn involvement, and pre-injury disability associated with longer IRF stay. Multi-center Burn Model System National Database (1994–2022) provides benchmarks for IRF LOS expectations after burn hospitalization.

Methods: Retrospective cohort study. Adult burn survivors discharged to IRF following acute hospitalization. Multi-center Burn Model System National Database. Patient and clinical factors analyzed as predictors of IRF LOS.

Rehab take: Use TBSA and LE burn involvement as predictors when estimating projected IRF LOS at admission. Supports case management planning and payer communication for burn IRF LOS justification.

🦿 PT · LE burns with ROM limitations and contracture risk drive longer PT-intensive stays; early ambulation and contracture prevention are primary PT goals.

✋ OT · UE and hand burns requiring splinting, adaptive equipment, and ADL retraining are OT-primary domains in burn IRF.

🩺 Nursing · Wound care complexity (dressings, graft monitoring, infection surveillance) drives nursing intensity in burn IRF; documentation supports LOS justification.

8. MEDIUM Microsurgical Flap Reconstruction Resolves Post-Burn Joint Contracture in ~99% of Pedicled Cases — Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of 830 Joints

PMC PMC12357132  |  Microsurgery · 2025  GR04

Key outcomes: 27 studies; 830 joint contractures reconstructed. Contracture resolution: 98.9% pedicled flaps, 90.1% free flaps. Recurrence: 1.8%. Non-viability: 1.5% pedicled, 2.9% free.

Methods: Systematic review and meta-analysis (PRISMA). PubMed, EMBASE, Scopus, Web of Science. Included flap-based reconstruction studies for post-burn joint contractures.

Rehab take: Understanding surgical reconstruction options for burn contractures informs IRF discharge planning. Patients with significant contractures may require staged surgical intervention post-IRF; surgical timelines affect rehabilitation goal-setting.

🦿 PT · Conservative stretching should be maximized before surgical candidacy assessment; post-reconstructive ROM protocols require careful progression.

✋ OT · Functional splinting and adaptive ADL strategies are OT priorities when contracture impairs hand/joint function pending or following reconstruction.

🩺 Nursing · Signs of contracture progression should be documented and communicated to the burn surgical team during IRF.

GR05 — Multiple Sclerosis

9. MEDIUM Austrian Multicenter Trial: 76% of Ambulatory MS Inpatients Achieve 100% Mobility Goals; Only 20% of Wheelchair Users Do

PMID 41774943  |  Journal of Rehabilitation Medicine · 2026  GR05

Key outcomes: 76% of ambulatory MS patients achieved 100% mobility goals during IRF; only 20% of wheelchair-bound patients achieved 100% mobility goals. Goal attainment significantly varied by baseline disability level.

Methods: Multicenter single-arm pragmatic clinical trial. MS patients in Austrian inpatient rehabilitation centers. Validated standardized goal documentation for rehabilitation goals, therapy content, and mobility outcomes.

Rehab take: Baseline mobility status must drive IRF goal-setting in MS. Ambulatory MS patients show high goal attainment; goal complexity should be calibrated to EDSS/disability level for realistic planning and accurate payer documentation.

🦿 PT · EDSS-calibrated mobility goals improve documentation and payer justification; mobility goal attainment tracking is the PT accountability metric.

✋ OT · Wheelchair-bound MS patients need OT-focused goals: ADL function, power wheelchair management, energy conservation, environmental modification.

🧠 Psychology · Lower goal attainment in severely disabled MS patients raises adjustment and coping challenges; psychological support for realistic expectation-setting is relevant at admission.

GR06 — Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis (ALS)

10. ⭐⭐ TOP 3 #3 ⭐ HIGH Comprehensive ALS Rehabilitation Framework: Clinical Practice Recommendations Across PT, OT, Respiratory, SLP, Psychology, and Palliative Care — 2025

PMC PMC12693028  |  Journal of Clinical Medicine · 2025  GR06

Key outcomes: Evidence supports: moderate tailored exercise (slows ALSFRS-R decline), early respiratory therapy (FVC monitoring, NIV timing), SLP intervention for dysphagia/dysarthria/AAC, psychological/palliative care integration at every disease stage.

Methods: Comprehensive narrative review with clinical recommendations. German ALS centers. Published December 4, 2025. Covers PT, OT, respiratory, SLP, psychology, and palliative care across all ALS disease stages.

Rehab take: ALS IRF admissions should be stage-appropriate: early-stage → exercise, equipment prescription, caregiver training; mid-to-late stage → respiratory monitoring, AAC evaluation, palliative care coordination. IDT goals should include explicit stage-appropriate functional preservation targets.

🦿 PT · Moderate non-exhausting exercise slows ALSFRS-R motor decline; aquatic and resistance training with fatigue monitoring evidenced; fall risk screening critical at every admission.

✋ OT · Equipment progression (AFO → rollator → manual → power wheelchair) and AAC device timing are OT-central across ALS stages.

🗣️ SLP · Dysarthria and dysphagia management at every stage; AAC introduction before speech is lost; PEG timing coordination with SLP assessment is critical.

🩺 Nursing · Respiratory monitoring (FVC, SpO2, secretion management), nutrition, and caregiver education are nursing priorities; NIV initiation education begins during IRF.

🧠 Psychology · Adjustment disorders, anticipatory grief, and existential distress are prevalent and addressable in IRF; caregiver psychological support is explicitly evidenced.

GR08 — Guillain-Barré Syndrome / CIDP

12. LOW Structured 4-Month Progressive Resistance and Functional Training Achieves Full Independence in GBS Following Campylobacter Infection: Case Report

PMID 41013802  |  Journal of Medical Case Reports · 2025  GR08

Key outcomes: 40-year-old female with GBS (Campylobacter jejuni etiology) with quadriplegia achieved full functional independence over 4 months of structured rehabilitation.

Methods: Case report. N=1. Structured rehab: progressive resistance training, functional training, ROM/proprioception, trunk control. Published 2025.

Rehab take: GBS recovery is highly variable; intensive programs with progressive resistance training drive functional recovery. IRF LOS justification should reference recovery trajectory and functional potential.

🦿 PT · Progressive resistance training (not passive) is the evidence-based cornerstone of GBS rehabilitation; functional training drives ADL transfer efficiency.

✋ OT · UE strengthening and functional ADL retraining are OT priorities; splinting for footdrop and wrist drop is an early OT intervention.

🧠 Psychology · Uncertainty of GBS recovery timing is psychologically distressing; brief psychological support improves therapy participation and reduces anxiety.

GR09 — Rheumatologic Conditions

13. ⭐ HIGH Rehabilitation Added to DMARDs Reduces HAQ by 0.3–0.6 Points and DAS28 by 0.4–0.7 Points in RA — Systematic Review of 8 RCTs, N=930

PMC PMC12535218  |  Cureus · 2025  GR09

Key outcomes: 8 RCTs, N=930. Rehabilitation + DMARDs vs. DMARDs alone: HAQ reduction 0.3–0.6 points; DAS28-ESR/CRP reduction 0.4–0.7 points. Multimodal rehabilitation (exercise + OT + PT) produced greatest effects.

Methods: Systematic review (PRISMA). PubMed, Cochrane, EMBASE, January 2013–July 2025. RCTs examining rehabilitation + DMARDs with disease activity, function, or QOL outcomes. Published September 2025.

Rehab take: Rehabilitation is well-evidenced as an adjunct to pharmacological RA management. IRF admissions should explicitly track HAQ and DAS28 as measurable outcomes to support payer justification.

🦿 PT · Exercise and aerobic conditioning reduce DAS28 scores; land-based and aquatic exercise both supported; joint-protective movement during active flare.

✋ OT · Joint protection education, adaptive equipment, hand splinting, and energy conservation are OT-central with documented HAQ functional gains.

🧠 Psychology · Depression worsens HAQ functional outcomes in RA; psychological support integration may amplify rehabilitation gains.

14. MEDIUM Fewer Than 3% of RA and SLE Patients Receive Rehabilitation Referrals from US Rheumatology Practices — RISE Registry Analysis

PMC PMC12813549  |  ACR Open Rheumatology · 2026  GR09

Key outcomes: Only 2.7% of RA patients and 2.6% of SLE patients received a rehabilitation referral in 2022; 4.4% for axSpA. 52–61% of practices referred <1% of their patients.

Methods: Registry analysis. RISE (~30% of US clinical rheumatology workforce). Patients ≥18 with ≥2 qualifying ICD codes and ≥1 visit in 2022. Published 2026.

Rehab take: IRF physicians and the rehab team can actively close the rehabilitation referral gap for rheumatologic conditions. Standardize post-IRF outpatient rehabilitation referral pathways in coordination with rheumatology.

✋ OT · OT is uniquely positioned to address this gap; joint protection, energy conservation, and adaptive equipment referrals are immediately actionable targets post-IRF.

🧠 Psychology · The rehabilitation gap likely extends to unaddressed psychological needs in RA/SLE populations; psychological referrals should accompany rehab referrals.

GR10 — Severe OA / Debility

15. MEDIUM Bedside Ultrasound-Guided Genicular Nerve Block Improves Pain and Therapy Participation in Knee OA During Inpatient Rehabilitation — First Published IRF Series

PMID 40432824  |  Frontiers in Pain Research · 2025  GR10

Key outcomes: Nearly all patients demonstrated clinical improvement in pain and therapy participation post-procedure. Safe in IRF setting (no safety signals). First published IRF GNB series.

Methods: Retrospective case series. IRF inpatients with disabling knee OA limiting therapy participation, Singapore General Hospital, July 2022–August 2023. Bedside US-guided GNB with corticosteroids and lignocaine.

Rehab take: Bedside US-guided GNB is a feasible IRF procedure for knee OA patients where conventional analgesia is insufficient or contraindicated. Consider as an analgesic adjunct to enable therapy participation.

🦿 PT · Monitor ambulation distance and therapy tolerance pre/post-GNB to objectively track procedural benefit; adjust PT intensity accordingly.

🩺 Nursing · Post-procedure neurological monitoring, local reaction check, and pain re-assessment are nursing roles; reconcile analgesic medications to avoid redundancy.

GR11 — PT (Interdisciplinary)

16. MEDIUM Partial Weight-Bearing After Lower Extremity Surgery: Patient Compliance Remains the Critical Weakness Despite Biofeedback Innovations — 2026 Review

PMC PMC13084172  |  Review · 2026  GR11

Key outcomes: Patient compliance with PWB restrictions is consistently poor. Traditional scales have poor accuracy vs. biofeedback devices. Multiple trials since 2010 show biofeedback improves compliance but no tool has been widely adopted.

Methods: Narrative review of current PWB standards, innovations, and compliance data for lower extremity surgery. Published 2026.

Rehab take: PWB prescriptions are inherently difficult to enforce in IRF. Patient education frequency and documentation practices matter more than device availability. Biofeedback devices may be considered for high-risk non-compliant patients.

🦿 PT · Reinforce WB restrictions at every session; smart insoles may be requested for non-compliant patients; assistive device selection affects actual WB loading.

🩺 Nursing · Nursing enforcement of WB status during off-hours (bathroom visits, overnight) is critical; nursing team education on WB rationale reduces off-therapy violations.

GR13 — SLP (Interdisciplinary)

17. ⭐⭐ TOP 3 #2 ⭐ HIGH Pharyngeal Electrical Stimulation Significantly Accelerates Dysphagia Recovery After Stroke Extubation — RCT Pilot, N=60

PMC PMC12418440  |  Neurotherapeutics · 2025  GR13

Key outcomes: By day 3: PES group — absent spontaneous swallows 8% vs 41% (sham); pharyngeal sensory loss 4% vs 55%. FOIS at day 3: 4.1 vs 2.1. FEDSS improvement sustained at days 5–7 and 8–10. Shorter ICU and hospital LOS in PES group.

Methods: RCT pilot. N=60 acute stroke patients with severe post-extubation dysphagia (FEDSS >4). Daily PES 10 min/day × 3 days early post-extubation (real vs. sham). Outcomes: spontaneous swallows, pharyngeal sensation, FOIS, FEDSS, ICU/hospital LOS.

Rehab take: PES administered early post-extubation significantly accelerates dysphagia recovery in stroke. Informs IRF transfer timing — PES-treated patients may arrive with faster oral intake recovery trajectories and earlier FOIS advancement expectations.

🗣️ SLP · PES is an active neuromodulation tool for post-extubation dysphagia; FOIS and FEDSS scoring familiarity is essential; oral feeding progression can be accelerated post-PES; ongoing IRF SLP assessment monitors trajectory durability.

🩺 Nursing · Safe oral feeding protocols should acknowledge PES treatment history in acute records; diet upgrade monitoring and aspiration precautions remain nursing responsibilities in IRF.

🧠 Psychology · Dysphagia is highly distressing; inability to eat orally affects mood and engagement; early psychological support during NG tube or modified diet periods improves IRF participation.

GR14 — Nursing (Interdisciplinary)

18. MEDIUM ERAS Nursing Interventions in Minimally Invasive Spine Surgery: Opioid Use Drops from 45% to 24%, LOS Shortens Significantly

PMID 40046500  |  Frontiers in Surgery · 2025  GR14

Key outcomes: ERAS group: shorter LOS (p<0.001); opioid requirement 24% vs 45.3% (standard care); lower postoperative pain scores. Nursing ERAS bundle included pain education, early mobility support, and patient counseling.

Methods: Retrospective analysis. ERAS vs. conventional care in minimally invasive spine surgery. Published February 2025.

Rehab take: ERAS-educated nursing significantly reduces opioid burden and LOS in spine surgical patients transitioning to IRF. ERAS-protocol patients may present with better pain control and earlier functional readiness at IRF admission.

🩺 Nursing · ERAS nursing bundles (multimodal pain education, early ambulation, nutritional optimization) are reproducible in IRF; adapting ERAS-consistent nursing protocols for post-spine admissions is evidence-supported.

🦿 PT · ERAS-treated patients arrive with less opioid burden; early ambulation protocols can begin more aggressively; coordinate pain medication timing with nursing for optimal therapy window.

19. MEDIUM ERAS Protocols Significantly Reduce Postoperative Pain and Hospital LOS in Ankle Fracture Surgery — N=376 Retrospective Study

PMID 40497243  |  Therapeutics and Clinical Risk Management · 2025  GR14

Key outcomes: N=376. ERAS vs. standard — VAS at 24h (4.2 vs 5.6), 48h (3.1 vs 4.4), 7 days (2.0 vs 3.1). Hospital LOS: 5.1 vs 6.7 days. Satisfaction: 92.1% vs 78.4%.

Methods: Retrospective study. 376 ankle fracture surgical patients, December 2022–December 2023. ERAS vs. standard care. Components: multimodal pain control, prompt mobilization, personalized PT.

Rehab take: Ankle fracture ERAS patients arrive to IRF with significantly less pain and shorter acute-care LOS. Early WB and mobilization are integral ERAS components to continue in IRF from day 1.

🩺 Nursing · Pain protocol continuity (multimodal, non-opioid-first) is the key ERAS transition element; nursing-driven VAS reassessment schedules support ERAS goals in IRF.

🦿 PT · Early WB and mobilization begin day 1 for ERAS ankle fracture patients; resistance training and proprioception within WB limits.

GR15 — Psychology (Interdisciplinary)

20. MEDIUM Mindfulness-Based Interventions Reduce Pain, PTSD, and Depression in Burn Survivors — Systematic Review of 12 Studies

PMC PMC12101291  |  Trauma Care (MDPI) · 2025  GR15

Key outcomes: 12 studies (6 cross-sectional, 6 interventions). Mindfulness interventions (meditation, yoga, self-compassion) improved pain management, PTSD symptoms, depression, and social adaptation in burn survivors.

Methods: Systematic review. MEDLINE and Web of Science through February 2025. 91 studies screened; 12 included. Published May 2025.

Rehab take: Mindfulness-based interventions are a viable adjunct to standard burn rehabilitation for chronic pain, PTSD, and psychosocial adjustment. Consider psychology referral for MBI evaluation in patients with significant pain catastrophizing or PTSD during burn IRF admission.

🧠 Psychology · MBIs are directly implementable by rehabilitation psychologists; brief mindfulness, yoga-adapted breathing, and self-compassion interventions are evidence-based in IRF settings.

🩺 Nursing · Nursing recognition of PTSD symptoms and pain catastrophizing cues enables timely psychology referral; routine screening with PC-PTSD-5 is feasible at burn IRF admission.

🗣️ SLP · Mindfulness-based approaches may complement dysphagia therapy by addressing anxiety and hypervigilance around swallowing in burn patients with airway injury history.
