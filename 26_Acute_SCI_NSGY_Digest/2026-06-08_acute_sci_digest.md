## 🔬 ISSUE THEME: THE 12-HOUR MANDATE AND THE ANTICOAGULANT RECKONING

A definitive 22-study meta-analysis closes the operative timing debate: ultra-early decompression (≤12h) doubles neurological recovery odds vs later surgery (OR 2.30, P<0.00001). A Feb 2026 meta-analysis demolishes the UFH default in spine trauma: LMWH cuts VTE mortality by 57% with no major bleeding penalty. RISCIS riluzole was COVID-truncated to 55% enrollment but a global statistical test re-analysis trends positive. Phenylephrine drives 57% of real-world SCI vasopressor use (N=3,636, Critical Care Medicine, Oct 2025) despite its reflex-bradycardia liability in high cervical injury.

## ⭐⭐ WEEKLY SPOTLIGHT — TOP 5 THIS CYCLE

### 1. ⭐⭐ TOP 3 #1 — **Ultra-Early Decompression (≤12h) Doubles Recovery Odds**
PMID [41036037](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41036037/)
*Surgical Neurology International* · 2025 | A01

**Key outcomes:** ≤12h vs >12h: OR 2.30 (95%CI 1.69–3.14, P<0.00001). ≤24h vs >24h: OR 1.49. No increase in mortality or complications.

**Methods:** Systematic review and meta-analysis; 22 comparative studies; N=2,395 (346 ultra-early ≤12h · 1,140 early ≤24h · 908 delayed >24h).

**Surgical/ICU take:** OR 2.30 is operationally decisive. The 2024 AANS/CNS 24h threshold is the floor, not the ceiling. Mobilize OR within 6–8h for cervical injuries. Notify spine surgery from the trauma bay. MAP, airway, and operative timing are a parallel process, not serial.

### 2. ⭐⭐ TOP 3 #2 — **LMWH Beats UFH in Spine Trauma VTE — 57% Mortality Reduction, No Bleeding Penalty**
PMID [40071770](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40071770/)
2026 | A07

**Key outcomes:** Anticoagulation vs none: DVT OR 0.40 (P=0.0013). LMWH vs UFH: DVT OR 0.78 (P=0.0050); PE OR 0.66 (P=0.0013); mortality OR 0.43 (P<0.0001). Major bleeding OR 0.52 (P=0.14, NS).

**Methods:** Systematic review and meta-analysis; 16 included studies (10 retrospective + 6 prospective); spine trauma patients.

**Surgical/ICU take:** Switch default spine trauma VTE prophylaxis to LMWH (enoxaparin 30mg BID or 40mg daily per CrCl). Initiate within 72h per Consortium for SCI Medicine 3rd Ed. once hemostasis confirmed. LMWH reduces VTE mortality 57% with no excess bleeding. UFH default has no evidence basis.

### 3. ⭐⭐ TOP 3 #3 — **RISCIS Riluzole: COVID-Truncated, Underpowered, Global Stat Positive**
PMID [40712181](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40712181/)
PMC12309938 · 2025 | A04

**Key outcomes:** UEMS gain +1.76 at 180d (95%CI −2.54 to +6.06, NS). Global statistical test (UEMS + SCIM III + SF-36 PCS): positive trend. No drug-related SAEs.

**Methods:** Phase III RCT; cervical AIS A–C, C4–C8, ≤12h; riluzole 100mg BID×24h → 50mg BID×13d; N=193 (54.9% of 351 planned); halted May 2020 (COVID-19).

**Surgical/ICU take:** 45% underpowered — interpret NS as inconclusive, not negative. Global stat positive. Compassionate use: riluzole within 12h for AIS A–C cervical injury, no contraindications. Dose: 100mg NG q12h×24h → 50mg q12h×13d.

### 4. ⭐ HIGH — **Phenylephrine Dominates SCI Vasopressor Use (57%) — Wrong Agent for High Cervical SCI**
PMID [40693862](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40693862/)
*Critical Care Medicine* · October 2025 | A06

**Key outcomes:** N=3,636 SCI T8+ requiring vasopressors ≤48h (Premier Database 2016–2020): phenylephrine 57%, NE 15%, multiple 25%, other 3%.

**Methods:** Propensity-score weighted retrospective cohort; mean age 56y, 75% male.

**Surgical/ICU take:** Default to norepinephrine for neurogenic hypotension in cervical SCI. Phenylephrine causes reflex bradycardia, compounding unopposed vagal tone. Avoid dopamine. Target MAP 75–90 mmHg.

### 5. ⭐ HIGH — **Multimodal Respiratory Weaning Bundle (MRWI) Improves Vent Liberation in Cervical SCI**
PMID [41062804](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41062804/)
*Spinal Cord* · 2025;63(11):579–587 | A13, A14

**Key outcomes:** MRWI improved vent liberation at ICU discharge. QI project Jan 2022–Jan 2025.

**Methods:** Retrospective baseline + prospective QI, Birmingham NHS. Bundle: (1) readiness-to-wean proforma, (2) RISCI guideline adaptation, (3) weekly MDT SCI ward round.

**Surgical/ICU take:** C1–C4: trach ≤7d. C5–C8: daily diaphragm U/S, daily SBT, NIF goal ≥−20 cmH₂O. Weekly SCI MDT round = lowest-barrier structural intervention. PM&R consult ICU day 1.

### 6. ⭐ HIGH — **Precision MAP: ≥85 Fails AIS Conversion — Sweet Spot 76–104 mmHg (First 48h)**
PMID [40922801](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40922801/) · PMC12414179
2025 | A02

**Key outcomes:** EAST N=164: MAP ≥85 for 96h failed to increase AIS conversion (>80% adherence). 200,000 intraoperative datapoints: sweet spot 76–104 mmHg first 48h. SCPP 60–90 mmHg in 92 catheter-monitored patients.

**Surgical/ICU take:** Treat MAP floor ≥75–80 aggressively; ceiling individualized. SCPP monitoring at specialized centers for incomplete injuries. [Week 23 TOP 3 #1.]

### 8. MEDIUM — **Autonomic Dysfunction Post-SCI: Cardiovascular Instability Independent of AIS Level**
[PMC12815724](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12815724/)
*Frontiers in Neurology* · January 6, 2026 | A02, A06

**Key outcomes:** Autonomic dysregulation shows weak association with AIS grade/level. MAP 75–95 recommended; NE first-line.

**Surgical/ICU take:** Do not use AIS as proxy for autonomic risk. Continuous cardiac monitoring for ALL SCI patients regardless of completeness. AIS D cervical can have profound hemodynamic instability.

*Deutsches Ärzteblatt International* · October 2025 | A03, A15

**Key outcomes:** MPSS: within 8h only if no TBI, no contraindications; not beyond 8h. Evidence quality mostly moderate/low.

**Surgical/ICU take:** MPSS decision must happen in the trauma bay, not after CT review. Contraindications preclude it in most polytrauma patients.

### 10. MEDIUM — **AS Cervical Fractures: 858-Patient Systematic Review — Posterior vs Combined Approaches**
[PMC12831493](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12831493/)
*Cureus* · December 24, 2025 | A08

**Key outcomes:** 17 studies, N=858; mean age 59.9y; C6–C7 most common; minor trauma 66%.

**Surgical/ICU take:** AS = ground-level fall = potential 3-column fracture. Alert anesthesia at trauma bay: awake fiberoptic intubation. Non-operative management of unstable AS cervical fractures is inappropriate.

### 11. MEDIUM — **C-Spine Clearance Updated Meta-Analysis: CT Clears 99.3%, MRI Changes 4.9% Management**
PMID [38266326](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38266326/)
2024 | A09

**Key outcomes:** CS-MRI after normal MDCT: abnormalities 16.6%, unstable injury 0.7%, management change 4.9%. Zero neurological changes after CT-based clearance in 1,017 subjects.

**Surgical/ICU take:** CT-based clearance safe for 99.3%. MRI still indicated for deficit inconsistent with CT, SCIWORA-risk mechanism, equivocal CT. Coordinate with neurosurgery in ambiguous cases.

### 12. MEDIUM — **Spinal GSW Systematic Review: Surgery Doesn't Improve Complete SCI — No Orthosis**
PMID [40756246](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40756246/)
*NASJ* · June 21, 2025 | A10

**Key outcomes:** Surgery for complete GSW-SCI: longer LOS, no functional benefit. GSWs are stable spine injuries; no orthosis required.

**Surgical/ICU take:** No collar, no TLSO. Operate only for progressive deficit, intradural bullet, CSF leak, vascular injury, or incomplete compression. Steroids absolutely contraindicated in penetrating SCI.

### 13. MEDIUM — **Pediatric SCIWORA Redefined: 60 Studies, 848 Patients — MRI Classification Urgently Needed**
PMID [40944097](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40944097/)
*Journal of Clinical Medicine* · September 2025 | A11

**Key outcomes:** Systematic review (PRISMA); 60 studies; 848 pediatric patients. Most recover with conservative treatment; prognosis driven by initial status and MRI.

**Surgical/ICU take:** Peds trauma + neurological deficit + normal CT = MRI mandatory. Immobilize until MRI complete. PM&R consult ICU day 1.

### 14. MEDIUM — **AIS Grade E Conversion: Median 171 Days — Full FI Only 14.3% If Age >70**
PMID [40873959](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40873959/)
2025 | A12

**Key outcomes:** EMSCI N=4,286; 12,221 ISNCSCI datasets. AIS E median conversion 171 days. Full FI: <40y 76.9%; 40–70y 42.9%; >70y 14.3%.

**Surgical/ICU take:** AIS E ≠ full recovery in older patients. SCIM III at discharge is the operational benchmark. Outpatient PM&R at 3 and 6 months mandatory for elderly AIS E patients.
