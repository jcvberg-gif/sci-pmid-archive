## MONTHLY HIGHLIGHTS

### 1. ⭐⭐ TOP 3 #1 — IRF vs SNF: +6.5% and +5.8% 90-day mortality with SNF assignment for stroke and hip fracture

PMID [41129694](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41129694/)
*Journal of the American Geriatrics Society* · 2026 | PC05, PC10

**Key outcomes:** SNF discharge associated with +6.5% 90-day mortality for stroke (95% CI 1.5%–11.4%) and +5.8% 90-day mortality for hip fracture (95% CI 2.5%–9.0%) relative to IRF. IRF community discharge rate ~70%, approximately 37% higher than SNF. IRF average LOS approximately half SNF ALOS. No significant difference in 90-day readmissions.

**Methods:** Quasi-experimental study using natural experiment of IRF unit closure; 55 US hospitals, 10,761 stroke and 13,963 hip fracture Medicare hospitalizations, 2009–2017. IRF closure used as instrument for causal inference of post-acute setting effect.

**Physician take:** This is the strongest causal-design evidence to date that IRF admission reduces 90-day mortality for stroke and hip fracture. When completing PAS, frame IRF medical necessity with explicit reference to mortality risk of SNF downgrade. ICD-10 anchors: I63.x (ischemic stroke), I61.x (hemorrhagic stroke), S72.0x (hip fracture). MS-DRG triggers: 061–063 (stroke without procedure), 536–538 (hip fracture with/without MCC).

**Liaison take:** Use these mortality differentials (+6.5% stroke, +5.8% hip fracture) directly in payer conversations and denial appeals. Script: *"A 2026 JAGS comparative effectiveness study (N=24,724) found IRF reduces 90-day mortality by 5–7% relative to SNF for this diagnosis — we are including this evidence in our appeal packet."* Flag hip fracture + mild cognitive impairment presentations: highest SNF-vs-IRF mortality gap in the literature.

### 2. ⭐⭐ TOP 3 #2 — 2026 AHA/ASA Acute Ischemic Stroke Guideline: PM&R consultation strongly recommended during acute hospitalization

PMID [41582814](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41582814/)
*Stroke* · 2026 · DOI: 10.1161/STR.0000000000000513 | PC09

**Key outcomes:** Replaces 2018 AHA/ASA stroke guidelines and 2019 update. Strongly recommends PM&R/physiatry consultation, ideally beginning during the acute stroke hospitalization, for all post-stroke patients with ongoing rehabilitation needs. Introduces new evidence-based recommendations for post-stroke dysphagia, pediatric stroke, and expands thrombolysis/thrombectomy eligibility criteria.

**Methods:** AHA/ASA multidisciplinary writing group; comprehensive graded evidence review. Expert authorship includes vascular neurology, emergency medicine, neuroradiology, neurosurgery, and rehabilitation medicine.

**Physician take:** This guideline provides Class-of-Recommendation backing for early PM&R consult orders on any acute stroke admission. Use it when building institutional stroke PM&R consultation protocols. Dysphagia recommendations clarify speech pathology coordination with rehabilitation physician. ICD-10: I63.x, I61.x, G46.x (vascular syndromes in cerebrovascular disease). Secure PM&R consult within 48 hours of admission as standard.

**Liaison take:** The 2026 AHA/ASA guideline = society-level endorsement for early physiatry involvement. When neurology or case management delays PM&R consult, cite guideline directly: *"The 2026 AHA/ASA Stroke Guideline specifically recommends PM&R consultation during acute hospitalization for post-stroke rehabilitation planning."* Earlier consult enables earlier PAS completion → reduced days to IRF. Particularly relevant for hemorrhagic stroke (I61.x) where IRF admission delays are historically greater.

### 3. ⭐⭐ TOP 3 #3 — GG admission scores predict IRF community discharge after stroke (OR 1.10/point)

PMID [39319640](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39319640/)
*PM&R* · 2025 | PC08, PC09

**Key outcomes:** IRF admission GG self-care score was an independent predictor of community discharge (OR 1.10, 95% CI 1.03–1.17). Admission GG mobility score equally significant (OR 1.10, 95% CI 1.03–1.18). Both remained significant in multivariable models adjusting for sociodemographic and clinical variables.

**Methods:** Retrospective cohort of stroke patients admitted to IRF; analyzed GG Section 0130 (self-care) and 0170 (mobility) admission scores as predictors of community discharge outcome.

**Physician take:** Document admission GG scores precisely at IRF intake — validated predictors of community discharge and the basis for CMS QRP Discharge Self-Care and Discharge Mobility Score measures. Low admission GG scores do not contraindicate IRF admission; they identify patients with the greatest gain potential. ICD-10: I63.x, I69.3x (stroke sequelae with neurological deficits).

**Liaison take:** Use this OR at screening: each GG point at admission carries a 10% incremental odds of community discharge. Counters payer arguments that "too low function" = SNF-appropriate. Script: *"Our PAS documents admission GG self-care [score] and mobility [score] — published PM&R literature shows each point corresponds to OR 1.10 for community discharge, supporting IRF intensity over SNF."*

### 5. ⭐ HIGH — PM&R consultation during acute stroke: reduced days to IRF, higher community discharge rate

PMID [35262316](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35262316/)
*PM&R / Archives of Physical Medicine and Rehabilitation* · 2022 | PC09, PC15

**Key outcomes:** PM&R consultation during acute hospitalization associated with higher rate of community discharge following IRF stay. Physiatry-led interdisciplinary stroke consult service reduced onset-to-IRF time by 0.6 days (ischemic) and 2.1 days (hemorrhagic). Increased IRF referral rates observed after consult service implementation. No difference in baseline functional scores or LOS.

**Methods:** Single-center before/after implementation study; physiatry-led stroke consult service with huddle rounds and virtual rounding tool; outcomes: IRF referral rates, days to IRF, community discharge post-IRF.

**Physician take:** Earlier PM&R consult → earlier IRF admission → higher community discharge. Target physiatry involvement by day 2 of acute stroke admission. Physician concurrence note should document rehabilitation potential, current GG-equivalent functional status, and anticipated IRF therapy tolerance specific to the patient's trajectory.

**Liaison take:** When IRF referral is delayed due to late physiatry consult, advocate for earlier involvement through case management. Track days-from-onset-to-PAS as a quality metric — the published target supports IRF admission within ~4–5 days of ischemic stroke onset. For hemorrhagic stroke where 2.1-day delay improvement was demonstrated, liaison involvement at ICU step-down should be proactive, not reactive.

### 6. MEDIUM — Non-traumatic SCI: 22% volume growth at IRFs (2013–2018), stable 12-day LOS and 74% home discharge

PMID [38588027](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38588027/)
*Journal of Spinal Cord Medicine* · 2024 | PC11

**Key outcomes:** Medicare patients with NTSCI treated at IRFs increased 22.1% from 2013 to 2018 (14,149 → 17,275). Home discharge rate: 73.7%–75.2% across years. Median LOS: stable at 12 days. Trend toward higher-acuity CMGs and comorbidity tiers over study period; patient complexity increasing even as LOS held stable.

**Methods:** Retrospective longitudinal analysis of IRF-PAI data, Medicare beneficiaries ≥65 with non-traumatic SCI, 2013–2018.

**Physician take:** NTSCI (cervical myelopathy, vascular SCI, infectious SCI) is a growing IRF population with increasing acuity. PAS documentation: AIS classification where applicable, bowel/bladder status, upper extremity motor function, and ambulation potential. ICD-10: G82.2x (paraplegia), G82.5x (quadriplegia), M47.11–M47.13 (cervical spondylotic myelopathy with myelopathy).

**Liaison take:** Post-surgical cervical myelopathy patients are high-volume, high-acuity IRF referrals. Home discharge 73–75% supports IRF over SNF for medically stable NTSCI patients. Denial-risk flag: ensure non-traumatic etiology is clearly documented and distinguished from degenerative-change-only findings — reviewers will challenge NTSCI IRF necessity if surgical stabilization is not documented or SCI is not established.

### 7. MEDIUM — MedPAC March 2026: IRF median community discharge 67.5%, $11B Medicare spend, payment adequacy positive

**Source:** MedPAC March 2026 Report to Congress: Medicare Payment Policy, Chapter 6 — Post-Acute Care: Trends and Key Issues
Published: March 13, 2026
URL: https://www.medpac.gov/document/march-2026-report-to-the-congress-medicare-payment-policy/ | PC05, PC06

**Key outcomes:** Median facility risk-adjusted IRF community discharge rate: 67.5% (FY2023–2024, stable). Total Medicare PAC spending: $57.7 billion (SNF $31.0B, HHA $15.7B, IRF $11.0B). MedPAC evaluated FY2027 IRF payment adequacy and access indicators.

**Physician take:** 67.5% median community discharge is the national benchmark — individual facility performance below this may trigger scrutiny or benchmark comparisons. MedPAC's payment adequacy findings influence Congressional and CMS rate-setting; awareness helps anticipate future rule directions.

**Liaison take:** IRF delivers $11B in Medicare spend versus SNF's $31B — but with superior community discharge and mortality outcomes. Use this in C-suite and payer contract negotiations as the IRF value-per-dollar argument. Facilities above 67.5% community discharge have a quality story to tell in network negotiations.

### 8. MEDIUM — Hospital frailty interventions: exercise, nutritional supplementation, and CGA improve outcomes (systematic review, 2025)

PMC [12186596](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12186596/)
*Systematic review — 2025* | PC14

**Key outcomes:** All included hospital-based frailty intervention studies demonstrated improvement in frailty screening and diagnosis rates. Exercise, nutritional supplementation, and comprehensive geriatric assessment (CGA) effectively improved frailty outcomes in hospitalized patients. Multidimensional interventions (physical + psychological + social) more effective than single-domain approaches. Implementation in routine clinical practice remains challenging.

**Methods:** Systematic review of strategies and outcomes for implementing frailty interventions in hospital settings.

**Physician take:** Pre-IRF frailty optimization in acute care improves IRF rehabilitation potential. Consider CGA-based geriatric consultation for frail elderly patients (CFS ≥5) prior to PAS completion. Document frailty-mitigating interventions performed during acute stay in PAS to strengthen medical necessity narrative. ICD-10: R54 (age-related weakness/debility), M62.84 (sarcopenia), Z87.39 (personal history of nutritional deficiency).

**Liaison take:** Frail elderly patients (CFS 4–6) are IRF-eligible if they can tolerate intensive therapy. Pre-IRF nutrition repletion, early mobilization, and geriatric consult can push borderline patients to IRF-ready status. Denial-risk flag: document that frailty is the reason intensive services are needed, not a contraindication — payers may conflate frailty with SNF-only level of care, which the literature does not support.

### 9. MEDIUM — Cognitive FIM at IRF admission predicts LOS efficiency and readmission in burn patients

PMC [9968425](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9968425/)
*PMC — Burn Rehabilitation Research* | PC13

**Key outcomes:** In 5,347 adults admitted to IRF with burn injury (2002–2011), cognitive FIM total at admission was a significant predictor of FIM total gain, LOS efficiency, and 3-day acute readmission. At IRF admission, 79% presented with cognitive-communication impairments; 27% had persistent deficits at discharge. Discharge FIM ≥110 associated with sufficient independence for community discharge.

**Methods:** Retrospective analysis of burn patients in national IRF dataset, 2002–2011.

**Physician take:** For burn patients referred to IRF, cognitive assessment is as essential as motor function for prognosis and PAS documentation. Cognitive FIM drives FIM gain prediction and LOS efficiency — document cognitive-communication status explicitly. Burn injury is one of CMS's 13 qualifying conditions for the 60% rule. ICD-10: T20.x–T32.x (burns), T71.x (asphyxia sequelae), residual functional deficits.

**Liaison take:** Burn patients with ADL limitations from hand burns, lower extremity burns, or inhalation injury with cognitive sequelae are strong IRF candidates. Screen cognitive status early in acute-burn hospitalization using validated tools. Patients with significant cognitive deficits may require longer LOS authorization — pre-authorize with detail on cognitive-communication goals. Community discharge remains achievable in the majority.

### 11. MEDIUM — Standardized GG functional data at IRFs: coding distribution, outcome correlations, QRP implications

PMC [9464264](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9464264/)
*PMC — Rehabilitation Outcomes Research* | PC07, PC08

**Key outcomes:** Characterized distribution of GG0130 (self-care) and GG0170 (mobility) scores at IRF admission and discharge; demonstrated correlations between GG scores and clinical outcomes including community discharge. Section GG provides cross-setting comparability unavailable with legacy FIM. GG data forms foundation for three CMS QRP measures: Discharge Self-Care Score, Discharge Mobility Score, and Discharge Function Score.

**Methods:** Analysis of IRF-PAI GG data characterizing coding practices and score distributions in the IRF patient population.

**Physician take:** GG coding accuracy at both admission and discharge is the foundation of CMS quality measurement. Discrepant admission-to-discharge GG scoring is an audit and quality-reporting target. Ensure GG items are coded per IRF-PAI v4.4 specifications effective October 2026.

**Liaison take:** Educate referring teams and families: IRF QRP measures evaluate IMPROVEMENT from admission to discharge, not just discharge score. A patient with low GG at admission who achieves meaningful gains meets CMS quality standards. This counters the misperception that low-function patients "belong in SNF because they won't score well."

### 12. LOW — Surgical stabilization of rib fractures: predictors of discharge to IRF, SNF, or home (2026)

PMID [40608005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40608005/)
*Annals of Surgery* · 2026 | PC08

**Key outcomes:** Identified predictors of discharge disposition (IRF, SNF, home) following surgical stabilization of rib fractures (SSRF). Discharge functional status was a significant predictor of all-cause outcomes. Age, comorbidities, and functional independence at discharge influenced post-SSRF disposition.

**Methods:** Retrospective analysis; patients undergoing SSRF; evaluated functional status at discharge and predictors of disposition.

**Physician take:** Post-SSRF patients with chest wall pain, respiratory compromise, and functional deconditioning are IRF-eligible candidates. Document ADL independence, respiratory therapy needs, and progressive mobilization goals. ICD-10: S22.3x–S22.4x (rib fractures). Major trauma is an IRF qualifying condition under 42 CFR §412.29.

**Liaison take:** Screen post-SSRF referrals for functional ADL limitations and pain-limited mobility. Denial-risk flag: document that respiratory therapy, pain management, and progressive mobilization require IRF intensity — reviewers may question IRF necessity vs. step-down unit management.

### 13. LOW — Traumatic vs. non-traumatic SCI: comparative epidemiology, complications, and rehab neurological outcomes (2025)

PMC [12347098](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12347098/)
*PMC — Spinal Cord Medicine* · 2025 | PC11

**Key outcomes:** Traumatic SCI (TSCI) and non-traumatic SCI (NTSCI) demonstrate distinct rehabilitation trajectories, complication profiles, and neurological recovery patterns. NTSCI patients differ in acuity, comorbidity burden, and neurological recovery from TSCI populations. Both populations are IRF-qualifying under the 13 conditions.

**Methods:** Comparative analysis of TSCI vs. NTSCI populations across epidemiological, complication, and rehabilitation outcome dimensions.

**Physician take:** Clearly distinguish TSCI from NTSCI on PAS — different ICD-10 coding, different MS-DRG impact, different rehab potential framing. NTSCI (M47.1x, G82.x) often has slower but meaningful recovery — document realistic and measurable functional goals over 2–3 week horizon. ICD-10: S14.x (traumatic cervical), G82.x (NTSCI sequelae).

**Liaison take:** Both TSCI and NTSCI count toward 60% rule compliance. NTSCI etiology must be confirmed and clearly coded — mixing etiology language in documentation creates audit flags. Both populations typically achieve >70% home discharge from IRF.

### 14. LOW — Cancer rehabilitation: not among 13 qualifying conditions, but individual patients remain fully IRF-eligible

**Source:** CMS 42 CFR §412.29(b)(2); AAPM&R Cancer Rehabilitation FAQ
AAPM&R resource: https://www.aapmr.org/career-support/practicing-physician-resources/clinical-toolkits/cancer-rehabilitation-faq
| PC12

**Key outcomes:** Cancer is not among the 13 CMS qualifying conditions that count toward the 60% rule compliance threshold; advocacy for inclusion ongoing for >30 years. Individual cancer patients meeting IRF medical necessity criteria remain fully IRF-eligible. ~18.6 million US cancer survivors; ~27.9% report mobility disability, ~10% self-care disability.

**Physician take:** Document cancer rehabilitation medical necessity independently of 60% rule language. Strategy: when comorbid diagnoses allow (brain metastasis = TBI-equivalent; spinal cord compression = SCI), code the qualifying condition to strengthen 60% rule contribution. ECOG PS ≥2 correlates with impaired function warranting IRF-intensity services.

**Liaison take:** The 60% rule challenge is facility-level, NOT patient-level. Any cancer patient meeting individual medical necessity criteria is IRF-eligible regardless. Denial-risk flag: payers may incorrectly conflate the facility 60% rule with individual patient eligibility — these are entirely distinct regulatory standards. Script: *"The 60% rule is a facility classification criterion. This patient's individual IRF eligibility is determined by medical necessity criteria under 42 CFR §412.622, which they meet."*

### 15. LOW — Clinical Frailty Scale predicts 90-day nursing home admission (35% severe vs. 5.7% mild frailty)

**Source:** RESORT Cohort Study data — published literature
| PC14

**Key outcomes:** Higher CFS score at admission associated with in-hospital mortality and 3-month and 1-year post-discharge mortality. Poor outcomes at 3 months: 14.0% in mild/moderate frailty vs. 27.5% in severe frailty. Permanent nursing home placement: 5.7% mild/moderate frailty vs. 35.0% severe frailty. CFS increase at discharge independently associated with 3-month and 1-year mortality.

**Physician take:** CFS ≥6 at hospital admission predicts 35% permanent nursing home placement — precisely the outcome intensive IRF rehabilitation can interrupt. Document frailty as the reason for IRF-level rehabilitation, not a contraindication. Target CFS 4–6 range for maximum IRF impact.

**Liaison take:** Use CFS to triage frail elderly patients at referral screening. CFS 4–5 patients who can participate in 3-hour therapy are high-value IRF candidates with demonstrable community-discharge potential. CFS 7–9 may need modified-intensity protocol or geriatric consultation during acute stay before IRF.

## 📋 POLICY WATCH

### [PROPOSED — Comment Deadline June 1, 2026] FY2027 IRF PPS Proposed Rule (CMS-1845-P)

**Title:** Medicare Program; Inpatient Rehabilitation Facility Prospective Payment System for Federal Fiscal Year 2027 and Updates to the IRF Quality Reporting Program
**Federal Register:** Vol. 91, April 6, 2026, Document No. 2026-06642
**CMS Fact Sheet:** https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/fiscal-year-2027-inpatient-rehabilitation-facility-prospective-payment-system-proposed-rule-cms-1845
**Proposed effective date:** October 1, 2026
**Comment deadline: June 1, 2026**

Three proposed coverage rule changes under §412.622:

**1. All therapies within 36 hours (§412.622(a)(3)(ii)):** All therapies must begin within 36 hours from midnight on the day of admission. Action: Audit therapy initiation workflow; PT, OT, and SLP must each have initial evaluation documented within 36 hours.

**2. Current functional status in PAS (§412.622(a)(4)(i)(B)):** Preadmission screening must document the patient's current functional status. Action: Update PAS templates to include GG-equivalent functional assessment at time of screening.

**3. IDT meeting on or before day 4 (§412.622(a)(5)(ii)):** Initial IDT meeting must occur on or before the fourth day of admission; subsequent IDT meetings confirmed as weekly. Action: Audit current IDT meeting practices — day 5 or later requires process correction before October 1, 2026.

**Payment:** 2.8% proposed update (3.5% market basket − 0.7% productivity adjustment).
**Comment period:** Submit by June 1, 2026 — https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/04/06/2026-06642
