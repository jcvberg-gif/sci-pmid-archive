MONTHLY HIGHLIGHTS

⭐⭐ TOP 3 #1 HIGH IRF vs SNF: +6.5% and +5.8% 90-day mortality with SNF assignment for stroke and hip fracture

PMID 41129694 · Journal of the American Geriatrics Society · 2026 | PC05, PC10

Key outcomes: SNF discharge associated with +6.5% 90-day mortality for stroke (95% CI 1.5%–11.4%) and +5.8% 90-day mortality for hip fracture (95% CI 2.5%–9.0%) relative to IRF. IRF community discharge rate ~70%, approximately 37% higher than SNF. IRF average LOS approximately half SNF ALOS. No significant difference in 90-day readmissions.

Methods: Quasi-experimental study using natural experiment of IRF unit closure; 55 US hospitals, 10,761 stroke and 13,963 hip fracture Medicare hospitalizations, 2009–2017.

Physician Take
Strongest causal-design evidence to date that IRF admission reduces 90-day mortality. Frame IRF medical necessity with explicit reference to mortality risk of SNF downgrade. ICD-10: I63.x, I61.x, S72.0x. MS-DRG: 061–063 (stroke), 536–538 (hip fracture).
Liaison Take
Use +6.5%/+5.8% mortality differentials in payer conversations and denial appeals. Script: "A 2026 JAGS comparative effectiveness study (N=24,724) found IRF reduces 90-day mortality by 5–7% relative to SNF — we are including this in our appeal packet." Flag hip fracture + mild cognitive impairment: highest SNF-vs-IRF mortality gap.

⭐⭐ TOP 3 #2 HIGH 2026 AHA/ASA Acute Ischemic Stroke Guideline: PM&R consultation strongly recommended during acute hospitalization

PMID 41582814 · Stroke · 2026 · DOI: 10.1161/STR.0000000000000513 | PC09

Key outcomes: Replaces 2018 AHA/ASA stroke guidelines. Strongly recommends PM&R/physiatry consultation ideally beginning during acute stroke hospitalization. New evidence-based recommendations for post-stroke dysphagia, pediatric stroke; expanded thrombolysis/thrombectomy eligibility.

Methods: AHA/ASA multidisciplinary writing group; comprehensive graded evidence review including vascular neurology, emergency medicine, neuroradiology, neurosurgery, and rehabilitation medicine.

Physician Take
Guideline provides Class-of-Recommendation backing for early PM&R consult orders. Use for institutional stroke PM&R consultation protocol development. ICD-10: I63.x, I61.x, G46.x. Target physiatry consult within 48 hours of admission.
Liaison Take
Society-level endorsement for early physiatry involvement. When neurology or case management delays consult: "The 2026 AHA/ASA Stroke Guideline specifically recommends PM&R consultation during acute hospitalization." Earlier consult → earlier PAS → reduced days to IRF.

⭐⭐ TOP 3 #3 HIGH GG admission scores predict IRF community discharge after stroke (OR 1.10/point)

PMID 39319640 · PM&R · 2025 | PC08, PC09

Key outcomes: IRF admission GG self-care score: independent predictor of community discharge (OR 1.10, 95% CI 1.03–1.17). Admission GG mobility score equally significant (OR 1.10, 95% CI 1.03–1.18). Both significant in multivariable models.

Methods: Retrospective cohort of stroke patients admitted to IRF; GG Section 0130 (self-care) and 0170 (mobility) scores analyzed as predictors of community discharge.

Physician Take
Document admission GG scores precisely — validated predictors of community discharge AND basis for CMS QRP measures. Low admission GG ≠ SNF-only; it identifies patients with greatest gain potential. ICD-10: I63.x, I69.3x.
Liaison Take
Each GG point = OR 1.10 for community discharge. Counter payer arguments at screening: "Our PAS documents GG self-care [score] — published PM&R literature shows each point carries OR 1.10 for home discharge, supporting IRF over SNF."

HIGH RCD Expansion to Texas (March 2, 2026) and California (May 1, 2026): pre-claim review now in 4 states

Policy Item — CMS Review Choice Demonstration for IRF Services | PC04

AHA News — December 1, 2025 | AAPM&R Opposition — December 17, 2025

Key outcomes: RCD active in Alabama, Pennsylvania, Texas (eff. March 2, 2026), and California (eff. May 1, 2026). All Original Medicare IRF claims subject to pre-claim or post-payment review. AAPM&R formally opposed expansion.

Physician Take
TX and CA IRFs: PAS must be audit-ready at admission. Address qualifying diagnosis, current functional status, rehab potential, 3-hour therapy tolerance, physician supervision plan, and IDT composition explicitly. ICD-10/MS-DRG accuracy is critical.
Liaison Take
Confirm RCD option with compliance. RCD expansion signals national CMS audit intent — maintain documentation rigor in all states. Denial-risk flag: ensure PAS is completed by qualified clinician within 48 hours with physiatrist concurrence documented.

HIGH PM&R consultation during acute stroke: reduced days to IRF, higher community discharge rate

PMID 35262316 · PM&R / Archives of Physical Medicine and Rehabilitation · 2022 | PC09, PC15

Key outcomes: PM&R consultation associated with higher community discharge from IRF. Physiatry-led stroke consult service reduced onset-to-IRF time by 0.6 days (ischemic) and 2.1 days (hemorrhagic). Increased IRF referral rates post-implementation.

Methods: Single-center before/after study; physiatry-led stroke consult service with huddle rounds and virtual rounding tool.

Physician Take
Target physiatry involvement by day 2 of acute stroke admission. Physician concurrence note should document rehabilitation potential, GG-equivalent functional status, and anticipated IRF therapy tolerance.
Liaison Take
Track days-from-onset-to-PAS as a quality metric. For hemorrhagic stroke, liaison involvement at ICU step-down should be proactive. Script: "Early PM&R consult (within 48 hrs) is associated with better IRF community discharge outcomes per published literature and the 2026 AHA/ASA stroke guidelines."

MEDIUM Non-traumatic SCI: 22% volume growth at IRFs (2013–2018), stable 12-day LOS and 74% home discharge

PMID 38588027 · Journal of Spinal Cord Medicine · 2024 | PC11

Key outcomes: NTSCI patients at IRFs increased 22.1% (14,149 → 17,275) from 2013–2018. Home discharge: 73.7%–75.2%. Median LOS: stable at 12 days. Trend to higher-acuity CMGs and comorbidity tiers.

Physician Take
Document AIS classification, bowel/bladder status, UE motor function, and ambulation potential. ICD-10: G82.2x, G82.5x, M47.11–M47.13.
Liaison Take
Post-surgical cervical myelopathy = high-volume, high-acuity IRF referral. Denial-risk flag: distinguish non-traumatic etiology clearly — reviewers challenge NTSCI IRF necessity when surgical stabilization is not documented.

MEDIUM MedPAC March 2026: IRF median community discharge 67.5%, $11B Medicare spend, payment adequacy positive

MedPAC March 2026 Report to Congress, Chapter 6 — Post-Acute Care: Trends and Key Issues. Published March 13, 2026. | PC05, PC06

MedPAC Report Link

Key outcomes: Median risk-adjusted IRF community discharge rate: 67.5% (FY2023–2024, stable). Total Medicare PAC spending: $57.7B (SNF $31.0B, HHA $15.7B, IRF $11.0B).

Physician Take
67.5% median community discharge = national benchmark. Facility performance below this may trigger scrutiny.
Liaison Take
IRF $11B vs SNF $31B with superior outcomes = IRF value-per-dollar argument for C-suite and payer negotiations. Facilities above 67.5% community discharge have a quality story for network contracting.

MEDIUM Hospital frailty interventions: exercise, nutritional supplementation, and CGA improve outcomes (systematic review, 2025)

PMC 12186596 · Systematic review · 2025 | PC14

Key outcomes: All studies showed improvement in frailty screening/diagnosis. Exercise, nutrition, and CGA effectively improved frailty outcomes. Multidimensional interventions more effective than single-domain.

Physician Take
CGA-based consult for CFS ≥5 prior to PAS. Document frailty-mitigating interventions to strengthen medical necessity narrative. ICD-10: R54, M62.84, Z87.39.
Liaison Take
CFS 4–6 patients who tolerate 3-hour therapy are IRF-eligible. Denial-risk flag: frailty is the reason for IRF-level services, not a contraindication — document this explicitly.

MEDIUM Cognitive FIM at IRF admission predicts LOS efficiency and readmission in burn patients

PMC 9968425 · Burn Rehabilitation Research | PC13

Key outcomes: In 5,347 burn IRF patients, cognitive FIM predicted FIM total gain, LOS efficiency, and 3-day readmission. 79% had cognitive-communication impairments at admission; 27% had persistent deficits at discharge. FIM ≥110 at discharge associated with community independence.

Physician Take
Cognitive assessment is essential for burn IRF prognosis. Burn injury is a 13 qualifying conditions for 60% rule. ICD-10: T20.x–T32.x.
Liaison Take
Screen cognitive status early. Significant cognitive deficits may require longer LOS authorization — pre-authorize with cognitive-communication goals specified.

MEDIUM Standardized GG functional data at IRFs: coding distribution, outcome correlations, QRP implications

PMC 9464264 · Rehabilitation Outcomes Research | PC07, PC08

Key outcomes: GG0130 (self-care) and GG0170 (mobility) score distributions characterized at IRF admission/discharge. GG correlates with community discharge. Basis for three CMS QRP measures: Discharge Self-Care Score, Discharge Mobility Score, and Discharge Function Score.

Physician Take
GG coding accuracy at admission and discharge is the foundation of CMS quality measurement. Discrepant scoring is an audit target. Update per IRF-PAI v4.4 (October 2026).
Liaison Take
IRF QRP measures evaluate IMPROVEMENT from admission to discharge, not just discharge score. Educate referring teams: low GG at admission + meaningful gain = successful IRF case by CMS standards.

Key outcomes: Discharge functional status, age, and comorbidities predicted disposition following SSRF.

LOW Traumatic vs. non-traumatic SCI: comparative epidemiology, complications, rehab outcomes (2025)

PMC 12347098 · Spinal Cord Medicine · 2025 | PC11

Key outcomes: TSCI and NTSCI have distinct trajectories, complication profiles, and neurological recovery. Both IRF-qualifying under 13 conditions.

Physician Take
Distinguish TSCI from NTSCI on PAS. ICD-10: S14.x (traumatic), G82.x (NTSCI sequelae). Document realistic but measurable goals.
Liaison Take
Both TSCI and NTSCI count toward 60% rule. NTSCI etiology must be clearly confirmed — mixing etiology coding creates audit flags.

LOW Cancer rehabilitation: not among 13 qualifying conditions, but individual patients remain fully IRF-eligible

Source: CMS 42 CFR §412.29(b)(2); AAPM&R Cancer Rehabilitation FAQ | PC12

Key outcomes: Cancer NOT in 13 qualifying conditions for 60% rule (advocacy ongoing >30 years). Individual cancer patients remain IRF-eligible under medical necessity. ~18.6M US cancer survivors; ~27.9% mobility disability.

Physician Take
Document medical necessity independently of 60% rule language. Code comorbid qualifying conditions when present (brain metastasis = TBI-equivalent, spinal cord compression = SCI).
Liaison Take
Denial-risk flag: 60% rule is facility-level; individual eligibility is separate. Script: "The 60% rule is a facility classification criterion. This patient's individual eligibility is determined by 42 CFR §412.622, which they meet."

LOW Clinical Frailty Scale predicts 90-day nursing home admission (35% severe vs. 5.7% mild frailty)

Source: RESORT Cohort Study published literature | PC14

Key outcomes: CFS ≥6 predicts in-hospital and post-discharge mortality. Permanent nursing home placement at 3 months: 5.7% mild/moderate vs. 35.0% severe frailty. CFS increase at discharge independently associated with 1-year mortality.

Physician Take
CFS ≥6 predicts 35% nursing home placement — intensive IRF rehabilitation can interrupt this trajectory. Frailty = indication for IRF, not contraindication. Target CFS 4–6 range for maximum impact.
Liaison Take
CFS 4–5 patients tolerating 3-hour therapy are high-value IRF candidates. CFS 7–9 may need geriatric consultation during acute stay before IRF. Document pre-admission CFS in referral paperwork.

📋 POLICY WATCH

[PROPOSED — Comment Deadline June 1, 2026] FY2027 IRF PPS Proposed Rule (CMS-1845-P)

Title: Medicare Program; IRF PPS for FY2027 and Updates to IRF Quality Reporting Program
Federal Register: Vol. 91, April 6, 2026, Document No. 2026-06642
CMS Fact Sheet: cms.gov/…/fy-2027-irf-pps-proposed-rule
Proposed effective date: October 1, 2026  |  Comment deadline: June 1, 2026

Three proposed coverage rule changes:

1. All therapies within 36 hours (§412.622(a)(3)(ii)): All therapies must begin within 36 hours from midnight on admission day.
Action: Audit therapy initiation — PT, OT, and SLP each need initial evaluation within 36 hours.
2. Current functional status in PAS (§412.622(a)(4)(i)(B)): PAS must document patient's current functional status.
Action: Update PAS templates to include GG-equivalent functional assessment at time of screening.
3. IDT meeting on or before day 4 (§412.622(a)(5)(ii)): Initial IDT meeting on or before fourth day of admission; subsequent meetings weekly.
Action: Audit IDT meeting timing — day 5 or later requires process correction before October 2026.

Payment: 2.8% proposed update (3.5% market basket − 0.7% productivity adjustment).
Submit comments at Federal Register document 2026-06642
