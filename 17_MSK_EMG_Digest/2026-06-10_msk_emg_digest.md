## KATIRJI CASE BLOCK — Case 11: Suprascapular Neuropathy

> *Reference: Katirji B. Electromyography in Clinical Practice: A Case Study Approach, 2nd ed. — Shoulder girdle neuropathies chapter. No copyrighted text reproduced.*

### Clinical Vignette

**MK** is a 34-year-old right-handed recreational volleyball player who presents to the EMG clinic with a 4-month history of insidious right posterior shoulder pain and progressive weakness with overhead serving and spiking. She denies neck pain, arm paresthesias, or hand symptoms. She has no history of trauma, prior shoulder surgery, or systemic illness. On examination:

- **Inspection/palpation**: Prominent wasting of the right supraspinatus and infraspinatus fossae; visible asymmetry compared with the left side.
- **Motor**: Shoulder external rotation 3/5 right (2/5 with resistance at end range), shoulder abduction 4-/5 right. Elbow flexion, wrist/finger extension and flexion: 5/5 bilaterally.
- **Reflexes**: Biceps 2+, brachioradialis 2+, triceps 2+ bilaterally. No asymmetry.
- **Sensation**: Intact to light touch and pin throughout.
- **Special**: No scapular winging on wall push-up. No Tinel's sign at the supraclavicular fossa. No tenderness along the cervical spine.

---

### Localization Questions

1. **Where along the suprascapular nerve course is the lesion most likely?** How do you distinguish suprascapular notch from spinoglenoid notch entrapment on clinical grounds?
2. **What additional physical finding** would shift you toward spinoglenoid notch involvement (isolated infraspinatus) vs. suprascapular notch (supraspinatus + infraspinatus)?
3. **Which muscles must you sample** to confidently exclude C5–C6 radiculopathy, brachial neuritis (Parsonage-Turner), and axillary neuropathy?

---

### Anatomy Primer

The **suprascapular nerve** arises from the upper trunk (C5 > C6). It passes posterolaterally beneath the transverse scapular ligament through the **suprascapular notch**, innervating the **supraspinatus** first, then continues around the lateral scapular spine through the **spinoglenoid notch** to innervate the **infraspinatus**.

| Compression site | Muscles denervated | Typical etiology |
|---|---|---|
| **Suprascapular notch** | Supraspinatus **+** Infraspinatus | Ossified/tight transverse scapular ligament; ganglion cyst; traction (overhead throwing) |
| **Spinoglenoid notch** | **Infraspinatus only** | Paralabral cyst (labral tear); ganglion; repetitive overhead: volleyball "server's palsy," baseball pitchers |

---

### Expected Nerve Conduction Studies

| Study | Finding in Suprascapular Neuropathy |
|---|---|
| Median motor/sensory (bilateral) | Normal |
| Ulnar motor/sensory | Normal |
| Radial sensory | Normal |
| **Suprascapular motor NCS** | ↑ distal latency (>3.7 ms to SS; >4.5 ms to IS) ± ↓ CMAP amplitude recording supraspinatus and/or infraspinatus; stimulate at Erb's point |

> **Technical Pearl**: The suprascapular motor NCS is technically demanding. Erb's point stimulation artifacts are common; electrode placement in the supraspinatus vs. infraspinatus fossa must be confirmed. Neuromuscular ultrasound guidance improves precision (see Saddler 2026, this issue).

---

### Expected Needle EMG

| Muscle | C5/C6 Rad | Parsonage-Turner | SupraN (notch) | SupraN (spinoglenoid) | Axillary N |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cervical paraspinals | **Abn** | Normal | Normal | Normal | Normal |
| Deltoid (C5, axillary) | Abn | Possible | Normal | Normal | **Abn** |
| Biceps (C5–C6, musc.) | Abn | Possible | Normal | Normal | Normal |
| Supraspinatus (suprascapular) | Abn (C5) | Possible | **Abn** | **Normal** | Normal |
| Infraspinatus (suprascapular) | Abn (C5–C6) | Possible | **Abn** | **Abn** | Normal |
| Teres minor (axillary) | Normal | Possible | Normal | Normal | **Abn** |
| Brachioradialis (radial, C5–C6) | Abn | Normal | Normal | Normal | Normal |

**Key EDx localizing rule:**
- **Both** SS + IS denervated → **suprascapular notch** (proximal to branching)
- **Only IS** denervated → **spinoglenoid notch** (distal to supraspinatus branch)
- Abnormal paraspinals = root level (radiculopathy)
- Multifocal denervation across multiple nerve territories + normal paraspinals → Parsonage-Turner

---

### Differential Diagnosis

1. **Suprascapular neuropathy — suprascapular notch** *(most consistent with this vignette: bilateral SS + IS atrophy, overhead athlete)*
2. **Suprascapular neuropathy — spinoglenoid notch** *(would expect isolated IS; abduction would be preserved)*
3. **C5–C6 radiculopathy** *(cervical paraspinals would be abnormal; deltoid, biceps involved)*
4. **Parsonage-Turner syndrome** *(neuralgic amyotrophy: acute severe pain onset preceding weakness; EMG may show multifocal, patchy denervation)*
5. **Rotator cuff tear** *(structural/disuse atrophy without EMG fibrillations)*
6. **Quadrilateral space syndrome** *(axillary neuropathy: deltoid + teres minor + deltoid patch sensory loss)*

---

### EMG Pearls (Katirji-style)

- Never skip the **cervical paraspinals** in a shoulder case — this single step separates radiculopathy from mononeuropathy.
- In an overhead athlete with isolated infraspinatus atrophy and a clean supraspinatus: get shoulder MRI to look for **paralabral cyst at the spinoglenoid notch** before attributing to idiopathic or suprascapular notch compression.
- Follow-up EMG at 6 months post-decompression or post-rotator cuff repair is indicated to document **suprascapular nerve recovery** — clinical pain relief may precede or lag behind electrodiagnostic evidence of reinnervation.
- Suprascapular NCS has modest sensitivity; a **normal latency does not exclude** suprascapular neuropathy. If clinical picture and needle EMG are convincing, proceed with imaging.

#### 1. Combined Use of EMG and Muscle Ultrasound for the Evaluation of Scapular Winging
**Rardin BP, Meiling JB, Cartwright MS, Caress JB.** *Muscle Nerve.* 2026;73(3):495–499. DOI: [10.1002/mus.70134](https://doi.org/10.1002/mus.70134) · Bucket: E08/Shoulder · Pub: Feb 2026

**Study**: Retrospective analysis; 13 patients referred with "scapular winging" or "scapular dyskinesis." All underwent muscle ultrasound (MUS) protocol of scapular stabilizers followed by EMG of relevant muscles. Neurogenic cause confirmed in 7/13 patients.

**Results**: MUS demonstrated **sensitivity 87.5%**, **specificity 95.7%**, and individual-muscle concordance with EMG of **90.6%**. In all 7 patients with neurogenic scapular winging, both EMG and MUS were abnormal. No false negatives from MUS in the neurogenic group.

**EMG/NCS Pearl**: Needle EMG of the serratus anterior (long thoracic nerve), trapezius (spinal accessory nerve), and rhomboids can be technically uncomfortable and procedurally complex in patients with scapular pathology. MUS provides rapid side-to-side comparison of muscle echogenicity and thickness, identifying atrophy/increased echogenicity that mirrors EMG denervation findings — without needle placement in the intrathoracic risk zone.

**Clinical Takeaway**: High MUS–EMG concordance supports adding MUS to the shoulder/scapular winging workup as a complementary tool, particularly in patients unable to tolerate needle EMG, pediatric patients, or as a rapid pre-needle screen. This aligns directly with this week's suprascapular case: if the patient has early infraspinatus atrophy, MUS can quantify it and guide EMG electrode placement.

**Action tag**: 🔧 **Technique upgrade** — consider adding muscle US to scapular muscle assessment protocol; especially valuable for serratus anterior (long thoracic nerve) evaluation.

#### 2. Ultrasound Guidance to Augment Needle EMG Precision in the Complex Nerve Injury Setting
**Saddler N, Ro H, Bristol S, Khayambashi S, Berger MJ.** *Muscle Nerve.* 2026;73(2):133–148. DOI: [10.1002/mus.70100](https://doi.org/10.1002/mus.70100) · PMC: PMC12803594 · Bucket: E08 · Pub: Dec 2025/Jan 2026

**Study**: Reference guide/protocol paper for the EDx medicine specialist; describes ultrasound (US) anatomy and approaches for 10 upper extremity and 2 lower extremity muscles commonly used as donor or recipient muscles in nerve transfer surgery. Discusses short-axis vs. long-axis views, in-plane vs. out-of-plane needle approaches.

**Results**: Conceptual/technique paper rather than a diagnostic accuracy study. Documents that traditional anatomical landmarks for needle placement become unreliable in the setting of peripheral nerve injury, spinal cord injury, or prior surgery. US confirmation of needle placement in target muscle reduces misidentification and improves electrodiagnostic yield.

**EMG/NCS Pearl**: Surgeons now depend on preoperative needle EMG to determine donor viability for nerve transfers (e.g., nerve transfer to suprascapular nerve using intercostal or phrenic donors). Misidentification of target muscle by 1–2 cm in a scarred or wasted shoulder girdle can lead to a false-normal or false-abnormal result with surgical consequences. US-guided needle placement provides real-time confirmation.

**Clinical Takeaway**: As nerve transfer surgery for peripheral nerve injury becomes more common in the MFB population (spinal cord injury, brachial plexus trauma), the physiatrist/EDx specialist becomes a critical pre-surgical partner. US-guided needle EMG is the standard of care in this setting.

**Action tag**: 🔧 **Technique standard** — for pre-nerve-transfer EMG, US guidance for needle placement in supraspinatus, infraspinatus, serratus anterior, and pectoralis is now best practice.

#### 3. Suprascapular Nerve Entrapment Syndrome Caused by a Spinoglenoid Notch Cyst with a Concomitant Giant Lipoma
**Ma Z, Guan M, Abudukadier M, Han X, Huang T, Yang Z, Li B, Cui Y.** *Front Surg.* 2025;12:1737191. PMID: [41602926](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41602926/) · PMC: PMC12833322 · Bucket: Suprascapular/E11 · Pub: Jan 2026

**Study**: Case report (rare double-lesion) of a 27-year-old male with 1-month rapidly progressive right shoulder weakness and pain. Isolated infraspinatus atrophy on exam. MRI revealed a paralabral cyst occupying the spinoglenoid notch with a concomitant giant lipoma causing double-crush compression of the suprascapular nerve. EMG confirmed isolated infraspinatus denervation with normal supraspinatus.

**Results**: EDx showed prolonged suprascapular motor latency to infraspinatus only. Supraspinatus EMG: normal. Infraspinatus EMG: fibrillations, PSWs, and reduced recruitment. Surgical decompression with cyst excision + lipoma resection; full neurological recovery at 6-month follow-up with repeat EDx showing normalized latency.

**EMG/NCS Pearl**: The key distinguishing feature: **isolated infraspinatus denervation with a normal supraspinatus on needle EMG = spinoglenoid notch localization** (distal to the supraspinatus branch). Confirm with MRI when this pattern is found — space-occupying lesions (paralabral cyst, ganglion, lipoma) are surgically correctable and carry an excellent prognosis.

**Clinical Takeaway**: Any overhead athlete or young adult with isolated infraspinatus weakness/atrophy and a localizing EDx pattern at the spinoglenoid notch warrants shoulder MRI. A negative MRI for cyst doesn't end the workup — unusual masses (lipoma in this case) can be missed on standard sequences. Re-image with dedicated sequences or ultrasound if clinical suspicion remains high.

**Action tag**: 🖼️ **Image correlation** — spinoglenoid notch localization on EDx → MRI shoulder mandatory; consider ultrasound if MRI negative and high suspicion.

#### 4. Pediatric Neuromuscular Ultrasound to Augment the Diagnostic Capabilities for Scapular Winging
**White et al.** *J Clin Ultrasound.* 2026. DOI: [10.1002/jcu.70147](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcu.70147) · Bucket: E08/Shoulder

**Study**: Case report of pediatric patient with post-surgical scapular winging (after anterior neck lymphadenopathy resection) in whom needle EMG was limited due to patient cooperation. NMUS revealed asymmetric trapezius thinning and hyperechogenicity showing more extensive involvement than clinically evident.

**EMG/NCS Pearl**: NMUS identified bilateral trapezius involvement in a case where unilateral injury was suspected clinically — directly affecting rehabilitation planning and prognosis counseling.

**Clinical Takeaway**: In patients (especially pediatric) where needle EMG is incomplete or not tolerated, NMUS provides objective neuromuscular data for scapular stabilizer assessment. Supports MUS as a validated complement to EMG, not merely a curiosity.

#### 5. Ultrasound Measurements of the Ulnar Nerve at the Medial Epicondyle Correlate With Electrodiagnostic Studies
**Aloi NF, Hyzny R, Fowler JR.** *HAND.* 2025. PMC: PMC11571372. Bucket: E02

**Study**: Prospective cohort; patients with cubital tunnel syndrome presenting for EDx and US. Pearson correlation between US cross-sectional area (CSA) at medial epicondyle and nerve conduction velocity across the elbow.

**Results**: US CSA at medial epicondyle inversely correlated with ulnar motor nerve conduction velocity across the elbow (r = −0.35, *P* = .02). Patients with positive NCS had significantly larger CSA than those with negative NCS.

**EMG/NCS Pearl**: A larger CSA (enlarged, entrapped ulnar nerve) at the medial epicondyle corresponds to slower conduction velocity — confirming that US and NCS measure the same pathophysiology from different angles. Useful when NCS is borderline: an enlarged CSA supports a true cubital tunnel diagnosis.

**Clinical Takeaway**: When ulnar NCS is equivocal, US CSA at the medial epicondyle adds diagnostic confidence. Conversely, a small CSA in a patient with mildly prolonged velocity should prompt search for an alternative diagnosis.

#### 6. Subacute High-Grade Ulnar Neuropathy Without Trauma: A Case of Cubital Tunnel Syndrome
**Authors (Henry Ford/Cureus).** *Cureus.* 2026. PMC: PMC12880857. Bucket: E02

**Study**: Case report. 46-year-old female pickleball player who awoke with right-hand claw deformity and sensory loss without trauma. EDx within days showed subacute-on-chronic axonal and demyelinating changes at the elbow consistent with moderate-to-severe cubital tunnel syndrome.

**EMG/NCS Pearl**: Acute overnight onset of ulnar neuropathy without trauma — "woke up with a claw" — is rare but represents a severe acute compression event (e.g., prolonged elbow flexion during sleep). EDx characteristically shows mixed axonal-demyelinating pattern indicating a chronic substrate with acute superimposed injury.

**Clinical Takeaway**: In the MSK/sports medicine clinic, recreational pickleball players are an emerging patient demographic with repetitive elbow flexion mechanics. Acute severity on a chronic background portends prolonged motor recovery — counsel patients accordingly even if sensory recovery is rapid.

#### 7. Noninvasive Assessment of the Ulnar Nerve Using Machine Learning
**Authors.** *J Hand Surg Glob Online.* 2026. DOI: S2589-5141(26)00008-3. PMC: PMC12927053. Bucket: E02

**Study**: 34 subjects (11 patients, 23 controls); YOLOv8 convolutional neural network trained on 2,011 US images to auto-segment ulnar nerve CSA at the cubital tunnel.

**Results**: Average Dice score **0.90**; 99% of test images scored >0.75. Mean difference vs. manual ground truth: 1.78 mm² (13.8%). Mean absolute difference: 2.08 mm² (16.1%).

**EMG/NCS Pearl**: Automated US nerve segmentation removes inter-rater variability from CSA measurement — a known limitation of manual US. Near-ground-truth accuracy at 99% of images suggests this may be deployable clinically, not just as a research tool.

**Clinical Takeaway**: AI-assisted nerve US segmentation is moving toward clinical readiness. In EMG labs with US capability, this technology would standardize cubital tunnel nerve sizing — particularly valuable for comparing serial studies over time.

#### 8. Parsonage-Turner Syndrome Due to SEPTIN9 Mutation: Report of an Italian Family with Childhood Onset
**Authors.** *Front Pediatr.* 2025. PMID: [40852410](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40852410/) · PMC: PMC12367472. Bucket: E05

**Study**: Case report + 109-patient literature review of hereditary neuralgic amyotrophy (HNA) linked to SEPTIN9 mutations. 9-year-old girl with episodic brachial plexus neuritis from age 5 years; father affected. EMG: chronic denervation of deltoid (polyphasic MUPs, increased duration); asymmetric F-wave persistence. Genetic sequencing: SEPTIN9 c.262C>T (p.Arg88Trp).

**Results (literature review)**: Mean HNA onset age: 13 years; mean time to diagnosis: 22 years. Classic presentation: recurrent attacks of severe arm pain followed by weakness and atrophy.

**EMG/NCS Pearl**: HNA caused by SEPTIN9 mutations can be clinically indistinguishable from idiopathic Parsonage-Turner by EDx alone. Clues: recurrent attacks, pediatric onset, positive family history, patchy multifocal denervation. EMG pattern in SEPTIN9-HNA: chronic denervation in brachial plexus territories, asymmetric F-wave abnormalities.

**Clinical Takeaway**: In a young patient with recurrent brachial neuritis — especially family history — genetic testing for SEPTIN9 mutation changes prognostication (recurrent, lifelong) and opens a family cascade screening discussion.

#### 9. MR Neurography Findings in Clinically Suspected Posterior Interosseous Neuropathy
**Javvaji A et al.** *JSES Open Access.* 2025. PMC: PMC12326117. Bucket: E03

**Study**: Retrospective, n=47 PIN neuropathy vs. 47 controls. MRN evaluated for T2 hyperintensity of PIN-innervated extensor muscles and nerve enlargement.

**Results**: Statistically significant ↑ T2 hyperintensity of PIN-innervated extensors and enlargement of both the PIN and radial nerve proper in the study cohort vs. controls (p<0.05).

**EMG/NCS Pearl**: MRN adds a spatial/structural layer to EDx in PIN. When EDx is equivocal (absence of conduction block; patchy denervation), MRN can confirm nerve-level pathology and help localize to the radial tunnel/supinator vs. spiral groove vs. more proximal radial nerve.

**Clinical Takeaway**: For diagnostically challenging PIN cases — particularly when imaging is requested to guide surgical decompression decisions — MRN is the preferred advanced imaging modality. Can identify fascicular abnormalities not visible on routine MRI.

#### 10. Radial Motor Nerve Conduction Study in PIN Syndrome and Multifocal Motor Neuropathy
**Baima JPS, Heise CO.** *Arq Neuropsiquiatr.* 2025;83(4). PMC: PMC12034393. Bucket: E03

**Study**: Retrospective chart review; Brazilian reference center; 2014–2022; 44 radial nerves in patients with PIN syndrome or multifocal motor neuropathy (MMN). Analysis of motor CV, conduction block (CB), CMAP amplitude, and distal motor latency.

**Results**: **Axonal loss** (↓ CMAP amplitude, normal or mild ↓ CV) associated with **PIN syndrome** (p<0.05). **Conduction block** associated with **MMN** (p<0.05).

**EMG/NCS Pearl**: If you see radial motor conduction block across the forearm/elbow without axonal loss in a patient with "PIN syndrome" — think MMN. The absence of pain (MMN is painless), bilateral motor-only involvement, and CB are the key distinguishing features. MMN requires IV immunoglobulin, not surgical decompression.

**Clinical Takeaway**: PIN + conduction block = workup for MMN (GM1 antibodies, multifocal motor NCS). Don't miss this: the treatment is diametrically opposite to PIN decompression.

#### 11. A Review of Electromyography Techniques of the Cervical Paraspinal Muscles
**McGuire TA.** *PM&R.* 2024. PMID: [37528546](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37528546/) · Bucket: E04

**Summary**: Comprehensive review of cervical paraspinal EMG technique, anatomy-based electrode placement, normal values, and pitfalls. Key point: the absence of a standardized reproducible cervical paraspinal EMG protocol has hampered the utility of cervical radiculopathy diagnosis by EDx. Cervical paraspinal fibrillations/PSW occur in up to 12% of asymptomatic adults under 60 and up to 90% over 60 — false-positive rate is high in the elderly.

**Pearl**: For cervical radiculopathy, paraspinal findings must be interpreted with the limb muscles; isolated paraspinal abnormalities in an older patient carry low specificity.

#### 12. Regression Equation Analysis Enhances Detection of Conduction Slowing Beyond Axonal Loss in Diabetic Neuropathy
**Authors.** PMC: PMC11577174. 2024. Bucket: E07

**Summary**: Proposes regression-based correction for conduction velocity values that accounts for axonal loss (amplitude), revealing "hidden" demyelination in diabetic sensorimotor polyneuropathy. May improve detection of superimposed CIDP in diabetic patients.

**Pearl**: When a diabetic patient's NCS looks "only axonal" but clinically CIDP is suspected, regression-corrected CV analysis may unmask a demyelinating component — informing the immunotherapy decision.
