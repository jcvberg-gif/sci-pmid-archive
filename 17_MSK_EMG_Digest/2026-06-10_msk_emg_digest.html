KATIRJI CASE BLOCK — Case 11: Suprascapular Neuropathy

Reference: Katirji B. Electromyography in Clinical Practice: A Case Study Approach, 2nd ed. — Shoulder girdle neuropathies chapter. No copyrighted text reproduced.

Clinical Vignette

MK is a 34-year-old right-handed recreational volleyball player presenting with 4-month insidious right posterior shoulder pain and progressive weakness with overhead serving and spiking. No neck pain, arm paresthesias, or hand symptoms. No trauma, prior surgery, or systemic illness.

  • Inspection: Prominent wasting of right supraspinatus and infraspinatus fossae; visible vs. left side.
  • Motor: Shoulder external rotation 3/5 right; shoulder abduction 4−/5 right. Elbow flexion, wrist/finger: 5/5.
  • Reflexes: Biceps 2+, brachioradialis 2+, triceps 2+, symmetric.
  • Sensation: Intact throughout.
  • Special: No scapular winging. No Tinel's at supraclavicular fossa. Non-tender cervical spine.

Localization Questions

  1. Where along the suprascapular nerve course is the lesion most likely? How do you distinguish suprascapular notch from spinoglenoid notch entrapment clinically?
  2. What additional finding would shift you toward spinoglenoid notch (isolated infraspinatus) vs. suprascapular notch (supraspinatus + infraspinatus)?
  3. Which muscles must you sample to exclude C5–C6 radiculopathy, brachial neuritis (Parsonage-Turner), and axillary neuropathy?

Anatomy Primer

The suprascapular nerve arises from the upper trunk (C5 > C6). It passes posterolaterally beneath the transverse scapular ligament through the suprascapular notch → innervates supraspinatus → continues around the lateral scapular spine through the spinoglenoid notch → innervates infraspinatus.

Key EDx Localizing Rule:
• Both SS + IS denervated → suprascapular notch (proximal to branching)
• IS only denervated → spinoglenoid notch (distal to supraspinatus branch)
• Abnormal paraspinals = root level (radiculopathy)
• Multifocal denervation across territories + normal paraspinals → Parsonage-Turner

Differential Diagnosis

  1. Suprascapular neuropathy — suprascapular notch (most consistent with this vignette)
  2. Suprascapular neuropathy — spinoglenoid notch (isolated IS; abduction preserved)
  3. C5–C6 radiculopathy (paraspinals abnormal; deltoid, biceps involved)
  4. Parsonage-Turner syndrome (acute severe pain onset; multifocal patchy denervation)
  5. Rotator cuff tear (structural atrophy without EMG fibrillations)
  6. Quadrilateral space syndrome (axillary neuropathy: deltoid + teres minor)

EMG Pearls

  • Never skip the cervical paraspinals in a shoulder case — this single step separates radiculopathy from mononeuropathy.
  • Isolated infraspinatus atrophy in an overhead athlete + normal supraspinatus on EMG: MRI for paralabral cyst at the spinoglenoid notch.
  • Follow-up EMG at 6 months post-decompression or post-rotator cuff repair is indicated to document suprascapular nerve recovery.
  • Normal suprascapular motor latency does not exclude suprascapular neuropathy — needle EMG may be positive when NCS is normal.

⭐ HIGH PRIORITY

1. Combined Use of EMG and Muscle Ultrasound for the Evaluation of Scapular Winging

⭐ HIGH — Directly relevant to this week's case

Rardin BP, Meiling JB, Cartwright MS, Caress JB. Muscle Nerve. 2026;73(3):495–499. DOI: 10.1002/mus.70134

Study: Retrospective analysis; 13 patients with "scapular winging" or "scapular dyskinesis." All underwent muscle ultrasound (MUS) protocol of scapular stabilizers followed by EMG. Neurogenic winging confirmed in 7/13.

Results:

  • MUS sensitivity: 87.5%
  • MUS specificity: 95.7%
  • Individual-muscle concordance with EMG: 90.6%
  • All 7 neurogenic patients: both EMG and MUS abnormal
EMG/NCS Pearl: Needle EMG of the serratus anterior (long thoracic nerve) and trapezius (spinal accessory nerve) can be technically uncomfortable and procedurally complex. MUS provides rapid side-to-side comparison of muscle echogenicity/thickness that mirrors EMG denervation — without intrathoracic risk. In suprascapular neuropathy with infraspinatus wasting, MUS quantifies the atrophy and can guide electrode placement.

Clinical Takeaway: High MUS–EMG concordance supports MUS as a complementary tool in scapular muscle assessment — particularly when needle EMG is incomplete, patient comfort is a concern, or rapid side-to-side comparison is needed.

🔧 Technique upgrade — consider MUS in scapular/shoulder muscle protocol

2. Ultrasound Guidance to Augment Needle EMG Precision in the Complex Nerve Injury Setting

⭐ HIGH — Practice-changing for EMG technique

Saddler N, Ro H, Bristol S, Khayambashi S, Berger MJ. Muscle Nerve. 2026;73(2):133–148. DOI: 10.1002/mus.70100 · PMC12803594

Study: Reference/protocol paper describing US anatomy and needle approaches for 10 upper extremity and 2 lower extremity muscles used in nerve transfer surgery. Discusses short-axis/long-axis views, in-plane/out-of-plane approaches, and dynamic US confirmation of target muscle.

EMG/NCS Pearl: As nerve transfer surgery becomes more common for brachial plexus injury and SCI, the physiatrist/EDx specialist becomes the critical preoperative partner. Traditional anatomical landmarks for needle placement become unreliable in scarred or wasted shoulder girdle muscles. US confirmation of needle placement in supraspinatus, infraspinatus, serratus anterior, and pectoralis reduces misidentification with direct surgical consequences.

Clinical Takeaway: For pre-nerve-transfer EMG, US-guided needle placement is now best practice. MFB's PMR-surgery partnership in peripheral nerve injury/brachial plexus cases should adopt this standard.

🔧 Technique standard — US-guided needle EMG for pre-nerve-transfer workup

3. Suprascapular Nerve Entrapment at Spinoglenoid Notch Cyst + Concomitant Giant Lipoma

⭐ HIGH — Directly relevant: spinoglenoid notch localization

Ma Z, Guan M, Abudukadier M, et al. Front Surg. 2025;12:1737191. PMID: 41602926 · PMC12833322

Study: Case report. 27-year-old male, 1-month progressive right shoulder weakness/pain. Isolated infraspinatus atrophy. MRI: paralabral cyst at spinoglenoid notch + concomitant giant lipoma creating double-crush on suprascapular nerve.

Results:

  • EDx: prolonged suprascapular motor latency to infraspinatus only
  • Needle EMG: fibrillations/PSWs in infraspinatus; supraspinatus normal
  • Surgical decompression: cyst excision + lipoma resection → full neurological recovery at 6 months
  • 6-month follow-up EDx: normalized latency
EMG/NCS Pearl: Isolated infraspinatus denervation + normal supraspinatus = spinoglenoid notch localization. Any overhead athlete or young adult with this EDx pattern needs shoulder MRI. Unusual compressive masses (lipoma in this case) can be missed on routine MRI — consider dedicated sequences or ultrasound if suspicion remains.

Clinical Takeaway: Spinoglenoid notch pathology is surgically correctable with excellent prognosis. The EDx finding of isolated infraspinatus denervation is the key to triggering the MRI request.

🖼️ Image correlation — spinoglenoid localization on EDx → MRI shoulder mandatory

4. Pediatric NMUS to Augment Diagnostic Capabilities for Scapular Winging

🔵 MEDIUM

White et al. J Clin Ultrasound. 2026. DOI: 10.1002/jcu.70147

Case report: post-surgical scapular winging in a child where needle EMG was limited. NMUS revealed asymmetric trapezius thinning and hyperechogenicity showing more extensive involvement than clinically apparent — directly affecting rehab planning and prognosis. Confirms NMUS as a validated complement to EMG in the pediatric setting.

5. Ulnar Nerve US at Medial Epicondyle Correlates With Electrodiagnostic Studies

🔵 MEDIUM

Aloi NF, Hyzny R, Fowler JR. HAND. 2025. PMC11571372

Prospective; US CSA at medial epicondyle inversely correlates with ulnar motor conduction velocity across the elbow (r = −0.35, P = .02). Larger CSA = slower conduction. When ulnar NCS is equivocal, a proportionally enlarged CSA adds diagnostic confidence for cubital tunnel syndrome.

6. Subacute High-Grade Ulnar Neuropathy Without Trauma: Cubital Tunnel Syndrome

🔵 MEDIUM

Authors. Cureus. 2026. PMC12880857

Case: 46-year-old pickleball player awoke with right-hand claw deformity and ulnar sensory loss without trauma. EDx: mixed axonal-demyelinating pattern (subacute-on-chronic) at the elbow. Rapid sensory recovery post-decompression; delayed motor recovery — counseling point for patients. Emerging pickleball-related ulnar neuropathy presentation to recognize.

7. Noninvasive Ulnar Nerve Assessment Using Machine Learning

🔵 MEDIUM

Authors. J Hand Surg Glob Online. 2026. PMC12927053

YOLOv8 CNN auto-segments ulnar nerve CSA at cubital tunnel from US images. Dice score 0.90; 99% of images >0.75. Mean difference from manual ground truth: 1.78 mm² (13.8%). AI-assisted nerve sizing removes inter-rater variability — moving toward clinical deployment for serial cubital tunnel monitoring.

8. Parsonage-Turner Syndrome Due to SEPTIN9 Mutation (Italian Family, Childhood Onset)

🔵 MEDIUM

Authors. Front Pediatr. 2025. PMID: 40852410 · PMC12367472

Case + 109-patient literature review. 9-year-old girl, episodic brachial plexus neuritis from age 5; SEPTIN9 c.262C>T mutation. EMG: chronic denervation in deltoid, asymmetric F-wave persistence. HNA mean onset age: 13 yr; mean time to diagnosis: 22 yr. In young patients with recurrent brachial neuritis + family history, consider SEPTIN9 genetic testing — changes prognosis (lifelong recurrent) and opens cascade screening.

9. MR Neurography Findings in Clinically Suspected PIN Neuropathy

🔵 MEDIUM

Javvaji A et al. JSES Open Access. 2025. PMC12326117

Retrospective; 47 PIN vs. 47 controls. MRN showed significant ↑ T2 hyperintensity of PIN-innervated extensor muscles + enlargement of posterior interosseous nerve and radial nerve proper (p<0.05). MRN adds structural/spatial detail when EDx is equivocal — particularly valuable when guiding surgical decompression decisions at the radial tunnel vs. spiral groove.

10. Radial Motor NCS in PIN Syndrome vs. Multifocal Motor Neuropathy

🔵 MEDIUM

Baima JPS, Heise CO. Arq Neuropsiquiatr. 2025;83(4). PMC12034393

Retrospective, 44 radial nerves. Axonal loss → PIN syndrome; conduction block → MMN (p<0.05). Critical pearl: if you see radial motor conduction block without axonal loss in a "PIN syndrome" referral, work up for MMN (GM1 antibodies, IV immunoglobulin). Missing MMN means surgical decompression instead of immunotherapy.

11. Review of EMG Techniques for Cervical Paraspinal Muscles

⬜ LOW

McGuire TA. PM&R. 2024. PMID: 37528546

Comprehensive review of cervical paraspinal EMG technique. Key caveat: cervical paraspinal fibrillations/PSW occur in up to 12% of asymptomatic adults under 60 and up to 90% over age 60 — false-positive rate is high in the elderly. Isolated paraspinal abnormalities in older patients carry low specificity for cervical radiculopathy.

12. Regression Equation Analysis Enhances Detection of Conduction Slowing in Diabetic Neuropathy

⬜ LOW

Authors. PMC11577174. 2024.

Regression-based correction for conduction velocity accounting for axonal loss reveals "hidden" demyelination in diabetic sensorimotor polyneuropathy. May improve detection of superimposed CIDP in diabetic patients when NCS appears axonal-only — potentially informing the immunotherapy decision.
