🌶️🌶️🌶️ HOT  ·  PL07 — Prior Authorization

🔥 Prior Auth Is Literally Killing People — And Insurers Are Lying About Fixing It

AMA 2025 Prior Authorization Physician Survey · Released May 13, 2026  |  AMA Press Release · GlobeNewsWire · AJMC

Key outcomes:

  • 26% of physicians report PA led to a serious adverse event (hospitalization, permanent impairment, or death)
  • 92% say PA negatively affects clinical outcomes
  • 95% report PA delays access to necessary care
  • 79% say patients abandon treatment due to PA challenges
  • Only 33% believe the 2025 voluntary insurer reform pledge will make a meaningful difference

Methods: Annual AMA Physician Survey on Prior Authorization, conducted December 2025; released May 13, 2026

Hot take: Sixty health insurers signed a "voluntary pledge" in June 2025 to streamline prior authorization. Two-thirds of physicians called it vapor. When 1 in 4 physicians has personally watched a patient suffer a serious adverse event because a non-physician said no, "voluntary pledges" are liability management — not reform. The CMS Prior Authorization Final Rule is the floor. It is not enough.

🌶️🌶️🌶️ HOT  ·  PL03 — Burnout / Moral Injury

💔 You're Not Burned Out. You're Morally Injured. Here's the Peer-Reviewed Proof.

Tutty MA et al. JAMA Network Open. 2026;9(3):e263161. DOI: 10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.3161  |  JAMA Network Open · Healio Apr 2026

  • 39% of US physicians report high moral distress (Moral Distress Thermometer ≥4/10)
  • Physicians have >4× the odds of high moral distress vs. other US workers
  • Burnout prevalence: 75.1% (high distress) vs. 30.7% (low)
  • Intent to leave within 24 months: 34.5% (high distress) vs. 18.2% (low)

Methods: Cross-sectional survey; 5,741 physicians + 3,501 US workers; Oct 2023–Mar 2024; Moral Distress Thermometer 0–10

Hot take: 4 in 10 physicians are living in chronic ethical violation. Physicians are 4× more morally distressed than everyone else in the workforce. The institutional response? A resilience app. The fix is systemic authority restoration — not another mindfulness module.

🌶️🌶️🌶️ HOT  ·  PL09 — Salary Transparency / RVU

💸 CMS Gave Your Procedural Work a 2.5% Stealth Pay Cut — And Plans to Repeat It Every 3 Years

CMS CY2026 Physician Fee Schedule Final Rule, November 2025  |  Nixon Peabody · MGMA · PYA

  • CMS finalized a –2.5% efficiency adjustment to work RVUs for all non-time-based codes, effective January 1, 2026
  • Rationale: productivity gains mean lower payment per unit — you got better, so they pay less
  • Time-based codes (E/M, psychiatry, behavioral health) are exempt
  • Procedural, imaging, surgical, diagnostic specialists: full impact
  • CMS plans to repeat this adjustment on a 3-year recurring cycle

Methods: CMS CY2026 PFS Final Rule, November 2025; MEI productivity factor, 5-year lookback

Hot take: CMS built a mechanism that automatically penalizes procedural physicians for becoming more efficient. For physiatrists with procedural volume, this is a direct comp cut if your hospital hasn't renegotiated comp-per-wRVU upward. If your contract hasn't been revisited since October 2025, your employer may be pocketing the difference. Review your contract language now.

🌶️🌶️🌶️ HOT  ·  PL02 — Admin Bloat / Exec Comp

🤑 California Voters Are About to Tell Hospital CEOs That $450K Is Enough

California Limit Compensation of Healthcare Executives Initiative (2026) · SEIU-UHW; SullivanCotter 2025; Form 990 filings  |  Ballotpedia · SEIU-UHW · SullivanCotter via BusinessWire

  • California ballot measure (Nov 2026): cap total annual exec comp at $450,000/year
  • HCA CEO Sam Hazen: $23,799,137 in 2024 — 391:1 ratio vs. median employee ($60,820)
  • Hartford HealthCare CEO: $5.1M in 2025, up from $4.1M (+24%)
  • Vermont, Massachusetts: similar exec pay cap bills filed in 2025
  • SullivanCotter 2025: executive total direct compensation up 8.4% in one year

Methods: SullivanCotter 33rd Annual Survey, Oct 2025 (3,320 orgs, ~48,000 incumbents); Form 990 public filings; SEIU-UHW state ballot filings

Hot take: Three states, one ballot initiative, all in the same legislative session. Built on years of Form 990 data becoming publicly searchable. Pull your hospital's Form 990 Schedule B and Part VII. The numbers will tell you what "we can't afford to expand clinical services" really means.

🌶️🌶️🌶️ HOT  ·  PL08 — Physician Unions

✊ The ACP Told Doctors to Organize — In a Peer-Reviewed Journal

Doherty RB et al. Annals of Internal Medicine. 2025;178(6). PMID: 40294420. DOI: 10.7326/ANNALS-24-03973  |  PubMed PMID 40294420 · ACP Online

  • ACP formally endorses physician right to collective bargaining, including union membership, without employer retaliation
  • Calls for practicing physicians to hold executive positions and voting board seats at hospitals and health systems
  • Supports "protected concerted activity" for patient safety and working condition concerns
  • Published in Annals of Internal Medicine, June 2025; ePub April 29, 2025

Methods: ACP policy position paper; expert committee; peer-reviewed; PMID 40294420; DOI: 10.7326/ANNALS-24-03973

Hot take: The oldest, most academically conservative physician society in the US just put "physicians should unionize and sit on hospital boards" into a peer-reviewed journal. Hand your CMO PMID 40294420. The burden of proof has shifted.

🌶️🌶️ MEDIUM  ·  PL01 — Physician-Led Outcomes

🏥 Physician CEOs = Lower Pneumonia Deaths. The Citation Is Right Here.

Peltier D, Becker H. Social Science & Medicine. 2020;250:112896. PMID: 32087485  |  PubMed PMID 32087485 · ScienceDirect

  • Physician-CEO hospitals: lower in-hospital pneumonia mortality (p<0.05) and higher patient satisfaction (p<0.01)
  • Business-trained CEOs: better financial performance (p<0.10) and hip/knee surgical volume
  • n=370 German hospitals, 2016 data; OLS regression with hospital-level controls

Hot take: Physician CEO = fewer people die of pneumonia. MBA CEO = better hip replacement margin. These are not equivalent outcomes. The next time administration argues for business leadership, cite Peltier & Becker: your patients' lungs disagree. A 2020 paper still holds because nobody has funded a large US replication. That absence of evidence is itself evidence of a funding priority choice.

🌶️🌶️ MEDIUM  ·  PL06 — CPOM Doctrine

⚖️ Texas Drew a CPOM Line. Find Out If Your State Is Next.

Texas SB 951 / HB 3410 (2025). Effective June 9, 2025 (noncompetes); Phase 1 Jan 1, 2026 (new MSOs); Phase 2 Jan 1, 2029 (all MSOs)  |  Source on Healthcare · US of Care one-pager · TrueEval analysis

  • First-in-the-nation legislation targeting MSO structures that enable corporate control without technically employing physicians
  • Phase 1 (Jan 1, 2026): new MSOs must preserve true physician clinical control
  • Phase 2 (Jan 1, 2029): all pre-existing MSOs must comply
  • Physician noncompete provisions: effective June 9, 2025

Hot take: Texas called out MSO structures explicitly in statute. If you're in an MSO arrangement and cannot clearly articulate who controls your clinical autonomy in writing, your state may be next — or you're already noncompliant and haven't been told.

🌶️ MILD  ·  PL04 — Medical Staff Governance

⚖️ Courts Rule: Hospital Boards Cannot Unilaterally Amend Medical Staff Bylaws

AMA Litigation Center. Avera Marshall Medical Staff v. Avera Marshall Regional Medical Center (Minnesota Supreme Court); Florida physician self-governance case  |  AMA — autonomy upheld · AMA — Florida case

  • Minnesota Supreme Court: hospital governing board unilaterally amended bylaws, stripping physicians of "nearly all rights and responsibilities" in medical direction
  • Court upheld medical staff self-governance; reversed lower court ruling that medical staff lacked standing to sue
  • Established that medical staff bylaws are an enforceable contract
  • AMA Litigation Center filed amicus briefs in both Minnesota and Florida cases

Hot take: Your bylaws are a legally enforceable contract. If your governing body has quietly amended them without a medical staff vote, you may have a breach of contract on your hands. Read your current bylaws. Compare to the version from when you were credentialed. Avera Marshall is your precedent.

📈 Numbers don't lie

🌶️🌶️ MEDIUM  ·  PL05 — Private Equity

💰 PE Buys Your Primary Care Practice. Then Watches the Good Doctors Leave.

Health Affairs. 2026;45(3). DOI: 10.1377/hlthaff.2025.01159  |  Health Affairs · Commonwealth Fund

  • 451 US primary care practices acquired by PE, 2018–2022
  • Post-acquisition: +0.39 clinicians/practice (12.2% growth) — primarily APPs
  • Also post-acquisition: +0.22 clinician exits/practice — largely APPs plus original physician departures
  • Net workforce stability: worse than pre-acquisition baselines

Methods: Difference-in-differences; 451 PE-acquired practices vs. matched controls; 2018–2022; Health Affairs peer-reviewed, March 2026

Hot take: They added 0.39 new hires and then 0.22 of them left. "Modest growth offset by exits" is clinical staff churn. The doctors who knew patients longest are statistically the ones walking out. The revolving door is the financial model. Point 0.22 at anyone who tells you PE acquisition stabilizes a practice.

🌶️🌶️ MEDIUM  ·  PL08 — Physician Unions

💪 Doctors Are Striking. Actual Attending Physicians. On a Picket Line.

Becker's Physician Leadership, 2026; PMC 11775727; HFMA 2025  |  Becker's Physician Leadership · PMC 11775727 · HFMA

  • Physician union membership: 17,000 → 40,000+ (2021–2025)
  • SEIU Doctors United launched May 2025: national attending-physician organizing strategy
  • November 5, 2025, Allina Health, MN: first physician-inclusive strike in Minnesota history — 600 clinicians
  • At least 38 healthcare strikes in 2025; 8+ included physician-specific issues

Hot take: "Attending physicians don't strike" was the conventional wisdom until November 5, 2025 in Minnesota. SEIU Doctors United launched in 2025 with an explicit pipeline strategy: resident union wins are the proof of concept being exported to attendings. If your CMO thinks this can't reach community hospitals in the Midwest, they haven't looked at the NLRB docket.

🌶️ MILD  ·  PL01 — Physician-Led Outcomes

🩺 More Primary Care Doctors = Fewer Deaths. Simple. Consistently Ignored.

PMID 39455301. "Primary care physician density and mortality in the United States." Published October 2024  |  PubMed PMID 39455301

  • Higher primary care physician density: associated with lower all-cause and cardiovascular mortality
  • Statistically significant across multiple multivariable model specifications; county-level US data

Hot take: Every primary care physician converted to an administrative role correlates with measurable population mortality increases. The next time a system proposes another administrative expansion without adding a clinician, name the trade-off they're making.
