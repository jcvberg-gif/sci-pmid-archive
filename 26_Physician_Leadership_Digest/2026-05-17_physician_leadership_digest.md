### 🔥 1. Prior Auth Is Literally Killing People — And Insurers Are Lying About Fixing It
🌶️🌶️🌶️ **HOT** | **Prior Authorization Kills. Full stop.**

**Source:** AMA 2025 Prior Authorization Physician Survey. American Medical Association. Released May 13, 2026. | PL07
[AMA Press Release](https://www.ama-assn.org/press-center/ama-press-releases/ama-survey-prior-authorization-reform-pledge-falls-short-physicians) · [GlobeNewsWire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/05/13/3294164/0/en/AMA-Survey-Prior-Authorization-Reform-Pledge-Falls-Short-with-Physicians.html) · [AJMC](https://www.ajmc.com/view/ama-survey-highlights-growing-burden-of-prior-authorization-on-physicians-patients)

**Key outcomes:**
- **26%** of physicians report prior auth led to a **serious adverse event** (hospitalization, permanent impairment, or death)
- **92%** say PA negatively affects clinical outcomes
- **95%** report PA delays access to necessary care
- **79%** report patients abandon treatment due to PA challenges
- Only **33%** believe the 2025 voluntary insurer reform pledge will make a meaningful difference

**Methods (HOT):** Annual AMA Physician Survey on Prior Authorization, conducted December 2025; annual survey methodology; released May 13, 2026

**Hot take:** Sixty health insurers signed a "voluntary pledge" in June 2025 to streamline prior authorization. Two-thirds of physicians called it what it is: vapor. When 1 in 4 of your colleagues has personally watched a patient suffer a serious adverse event — hospitalization, permanent disability, or death — because a non-physician at a spreadsheet said no, "voluntary pledges" aren't reform. They are liability management. The CMS Prior Authorization Final Rule is the floor. It is not enough. Stop politely requesting change and start demanding it with citations in hand.

### 💔 2. You're Not Burned Out. You're Morally Injured. Here's the Peer-Reviewed Proof.
🌶️🌶️🌶️ **HOT** | **The burnout-as-weakness narrative is dead**

**Source:** Tutty MA et al. "Moral Distress and Occupational Burnout in US Physicians." *JAMA Network Open.* 2026;9(3):e263161. DOI: 10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.3161 | PL03
[JAMA Network Open](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2846921) · [Healio coverage, Apr 2026](https://www.healio.com/news/hematology-oncology/20260420/nearly-40-of-physicians-report-high-moral-distress-which-significantly-increases-burnout) · [HealthExec](https://healthexec.com/topics/healthcare-management/healthcare-staffing/moral-distress-physicians-new-study-measures-prevalence-offers-interventions)

**Key outcomes:**
- **39%** of US physicians report high moral distress (Moral Distress Thermometer score ≥4/10)
- Physicians have **>4× the odds** of high moral distress compared to other US workers
- Burnout prevalence: **75.1%** in high-moral-distress physicians vs. **30.7%** in low
- Intent to leave within 24 months: **34.5%** (high distress) vs. **18.2%** (low)

**Methods (HOT):** Cross-sectional survey; 5,741 physicians + 3,501 US workers; October 2023–March 2024; Moral Distress Thermometer (0–10 scale, ≥4 = high); multivariable logistic regression

**Hot take:** Nearly 4 in 10 physicians are living in a state of chronic ethical violation. Your colleagues are 4× more morally distressed than the general workforce they serve. The institutional response? A resilience app. A gratitude journal. A wellness subsidy. Tutty et al. draw a bright statistical line: this is not a character defect, a personal bandwidth problem, or a generational attitude. It is what happens when you are systematically prevented from practicing medicine the way you were trained and sworn to practice. The fix is systemic authority restoration — not another mindfulness module.

### 💸 3. CMS Gave Your Procedural Work a 2.5% Stealth Pay Cut — And Plans to Repeat It Every 3 Years
🌶️🌶️🌶️ **HOT** | **Read your contract. Now.**

**Source:** CMS CY2026 Physician Fee Schedule Final Rule, November 2025. Analysis: Nixon Peabody (Sept 2025); PYA (2025); MGMA (2025); Human Medical Billing (2025). | PL09
[Nixon Peabody](https://www.nixonpeabody.com/insights/alerts/2025/09/10/cms-proposes-efficiency-adjustment-to-the-valuation-of-physician-work-rvus) · [MGMA analysis](https://www.mgma.com/articles/2026-work-rvu-efficiency-adjustment-specialty-strategies-for-comp) · [PYA](https://www.pyapc.com/insights/2026-wrvu-changes-are-here-what-organizations-need-to-know-for-physician-compensation-planning/) · [Human Medical Billing](https://humanmedicalbilling.com/blog/2026-pfs-2-point-5-percent-efficiency-adjustment-stealth-cut-for-specialists/)

**Key outcomes:**
- CMS finalized a **–2.5% efficiency adjustment** to work RVUs for all non-time-based codes, effective **January 1, 2026**
- Rationale: "productivity gains from workflow improvements" — you got better, so they pay less per unit
- Time-based codes (E/M, psychiatry, behavioral health) are **exempt**
- Procedural, imaging, surgical, diagnostic specialists: **full brunt**
- CMS intends to apply this adjustment on a **3-year recurring cycle**

**Methods (HOT):** CMS CY2026 PFS Final Rule, November 2025; MEI productivity factor; 5-year lookback; applies to ~all non-time-based CPT codes

**Hot take:** CMS built a mechanism that automatically penalizes procedural physicians for becoming more efficient. Do your job faster and better? Congratulations — your wRVU is worth less. For physiatrists with significant procedural volume (EMG, injections, spasticity management), this is a direct comp cut if your hospital hasn't renegotiated your comp-per-wRVU rate upward. If your contract hasn't been revisited since October 2025, your employer is quietly keeping the difference. This is not hypothetical — it is now in effect. Review your contract language on wRVU valuation.

### 🏥 6. Physician CEOs = Lower Pneumonia Deaths. The Citation Is Right Here.
🌶️🌶️ **MEDIUM** | **Clinical mortality vs. margin — pick your metric**

**Source:** Peltier D, Becker H. "Physician-leaders and hospital performance revisited." *Social Science & Medicine.* 2020;250:112896. PMID: 32087485 | PL01
[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32087485/) · [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0277953620300502) · [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/339046360_Physician-leaders_and_hospital_performance_revisited)

**Key outcomes:**
- Physician-CEO hospitals: **lower in-hospital pneumonia mortality** (p<0.05) and **higher patient satisfaction** (p<0.01)
- Business/economics-trained CEOs: better financial performance (p<0.10) and higher hip/knee surgical volume metrics
- Signal is real but nuanced — not a sweep across all quality dimensions

**Methods:** Cross-sectional OLS regression; 370 German hospitals; 2016 data; CEO educational background as primary exposure; in-hospital mortality, patient satisfaction, financial performance as outcomes

**Hot take:** Give a hospital a physician CEO and fewer people die of pneumonia. Give it a business school CEO and the hip replacement margin looks better. These are not equivalent outcomes, and hospital boards that treat them as equivalent have made an explicit values choice. The Peltier & Becker finding is from 2020 and has not been replicated in a large US sample — which is itself a story about what healthcare research funding prioritizes. Until someone funds that study, this remains the best available peer-reviewed evidence. Use it.

### ⚖️ 7. Texas Drew a CPOM Line. Find Out If Your State Is Next.
🌶️🌶️ **MEDIUM** | **MSO structures are under legal scrutiny**

**Source:** Texas SB 951 / HB 3410 (2025). Effective June 9, 2025 (noncompetes); Phase 1 January 1, 2026 (new MSO restrictions); Phase 2 January 1, 2029 (pre-existing MSOs). Source on Healthcare; US of Care; TrueEval. | PL06
[Source on Healthcare Price & Competition](https://sourceonhealthcare.org/corporate-practice-of-medicine-bills-full/) · [US of Care one-pager](https://unitedstatesofcare.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Corporate-Practice-of-Medicine-One-Pager.pdf) · [TrueEval CPOM analysis](https://trueeval.com/blog/cpom-compliance-multi-state-telehealth-2025)

**Key outcomes:**
- Texas SB 951/HB 3410: **first-in-the-nation** legislation targeting MSO (Management Services Organization) structures that enable corporate control of physician practices without technically employing them
- Phase 1 (Jan 1, 2026): new MSOs must preserve true physician clinical control
- Phase 2 (Jan 1, 2029): all pre-existing MSOs must comply
- Physician **noncompete provisions** effective June 9, 2025

**Methods:** Texas 89th Legislative Session; SB 951/HB 3410; legal analysis: TrueEval, US of Care, Source on Healthcare Price & Competition

**Hot take:** Texas — not historically a regulation-eager state — decided that PE-funded MSO structures had strayed so far from the CPOM doctrine's intent that statute was required. MSOs are specifically architected to technically comply with CPOM (no physician employment) while capturing clinical decision-making through billing, scheduling, staffing, and vendor contracts. Texas named that arrangement explicitly. If you are in an MSO arrangement and cannot clearly articulate who controls your clinical autonomy in writing, your state may be next — or you are already noncompliant and haven't been told.

### ⚖️ 8. Courts Rule: Hospital Boards Cannot Unilaterally Amend Medical Staff Bylaws
🌶️ **MILD** | **Your bylaws are a contract. Enforce them.**

**Source:** AMA Litigation Center. Avera Marshall Medical Staff v. Avera Marshall Regional Medical Center (Minnesota Supreme Court). Florida physician self-governance case. AMA Wire and AMA advocacy pages, 2024–2025. | PL04
[AMA — medical staff autonomy upheld](https://www.ama-assn.org/health-care-advocacy/judicial-advocacy/medical-staff-autonomy-upheld-state-supreme-court-ruling) · [AMA — Florida case](https://www.ama-assn.org/health-care-advocacy/judicial-advocacy/florida-case-puts-medical-staff-self-governance-under-fire) · [AMA — autonomy at stake](https://wire.ama-assn.org/practice-management/medical-staff-autonomy-stake-state-supreme-court-case)

**Key outcomes:**
- Minnesota Supreme Court (Avera Marshall): hospital governing board **unilaterally amended** medical staff bylaws, stripping physicians of "nearly all rights and responsibilities" in medical direction
- Court **upheld medical staff self-governance**, reversed lower courts' ruling that medical staff lacked standing to sue
- Ruling established that **medical staff bylaws constitute an enforceable contract** between the hospital and medical staff
- AMA Litigation Center filed amicus briefs in both Minnesota and Florida cases

**Hot take:** Your medical staff bylaws are not suggestions. The Minnesota Supreme Court confirmed they are a legally enforceable contract — and that medical staff have standing to sue when a hospital board decides to just rewrite them unilaterally. If your hospital's governing body has quietly amended your bylaws without a medical staff vote, you may have a breach of contract on your hands. Read your current bylaws. Compare them to the version you were credentialed under. Note any changes. Talk to your medical staff attorney. Avera Marshall is now your precedent.

## 📈 Numbers don't lie

### 💰 9. PE Buys Your Primary Care Practice. Then Watches the Good Doctors Leave.
🌶️🌶️ **MEDIUM** | **The revolving door is not a bug**

**Source:** "Private Equity Acquisition Of Primary Care Practices: Modest Growth In Clinicians Offset By Increased Clinician Exits." *Health Affairs.* 2026;45(3). DOI: 10.1377/hlthaff.2025.01159 | PL05
[Health Affairs](https://www.healthaffairs.org/doi/10.1377/hlthaff.2025.01159) · [Commonwealth Fund podcast](https://www.commonwealthfund.org/publications/podcast/2025/may/how-private-equity-deals-are-reshaping-your-health-care) · [NIHCM](https://nihcm.org/publications/private-equity-ownership-of-physician-practices-is-rising)

**Key outcomes:**
- Analysis of **451 US primary care practices** acquired by PE, 2018–2022
- Post-acquisition: **+0.39 clinicians/practice** (12.2% growth) — primarily advanced practice providers
- Also post-acquisition: **+0.22 clinician exits/practice** — largely APPs, plus original physician departures
- Net workforce stability: **worse** than pre-acquisition baselines

**Methods:** Difference-in-differences design; 451 PE-acquired primary care practices vs. matched non-acquired controls; 2018–2022; *Health Affairs* peer-reviewed, March 2026

**Hot take:** PE pitch: "Capital, staff, and scale." Health Affairs data: they added 0.39 new hires and then 0.22 of them left. What the industry calls "modest growth offset by exits" is clinical staff churn built on APP cycling while the physicians who built the practice and knew the patients longest walk out the door. The revolving door is not a bug — it is the financial model. Continuity of care is not a PE valuation metric. Point 0.22 at anyone who tells you PE acquisition "stabilizes" a practice.

The resident/fellow union wins (real contracts, higher pay, defined hours) are the proof of concept now being actively exported to attending organizing. SEIU Doctors United launched in 2025 with an explicit strategy to leverage existing resident unions as a pipeline into attending recruitment. If your CMO thinks this can't reach community hospitals in the Midwest, they haven't looked at a map of the NLRB's current docket.

### 🩺 12. More Primary Care Doctors = Fewer Deaths. Simple. Consistently Ignored.
🌶️ **MILD** | **Every admin role added is a physician slot not filled**

**Source:** PMID 39455301. "Primary care physician density and mortality in the United States." *PubMed.* Published October 2024. | PL01
[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39455301/)

**Key outcomes:**
- Higher primary care physician density per 100,000 population **associated with lower all-cause and cardiovascular mortality**
- Association statistically significant across multiple multivariable model specifications in a county-level ecological analysis

**Methods:** Ecological analysis; US county-level data; multivariable regression; published October 2024

**Hot take:** Healthcare systems keep converting primary care physician FTEs into administrative coordinators, compliance officers, and utilization management reviewers. PMID 39455301 says: every primary care physician you remove from patient-facing care correlates with measurable population-level mortality increases. The next time a hospital proposes another administrative expansion that doesn't add a clinician, this is the trade-off they're making. Name it.
