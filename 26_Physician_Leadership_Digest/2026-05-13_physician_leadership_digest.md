## 📰 Hot off the press

### 1. 🌶️🌶️🌶️ HOT — CMS Just Quietly Cut Procedural Physician Pay Without Changing a Single Workday

[CMS CY 2026 Medicare Physician Fee Schedule Final Rule (CMS-1832-F)](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/calendar-year-cy-2026-medicare-physician-fee-schedule-final-rule-cms-1832-f) — CMS, November 2025
*CMS Fact Sheet · November 2025 | PL09*

**Key outcomes:** A 2.5% "efficiency adjustment" reduces work RVUs for nearly all non–time-based CPT codes — surgical, procedural, imaging, and diagnostic. Affects ~7,000 physician services, or **91% of all services** across specialties. Time-based services (E&M, psychiatry, primary care) are exempt. Net effect: a procedural or surgical specialist doing identical work on January 1, 2026 as December 31, 2025 generates fewer wRVUs, may miss productivity thresholds, and may trigger bonus clawbacks — without changing clinical effort by a single RVU. Confirmed by [PYA (Dec 2025)](https://www.pyapc.com/insights/2026-wrvu-changes-are-here-what-organizations-need-to-know-for-physician-compensation-planning/), [MGMA specialty breakdown](https://www.mgma.com/articles/2026-work-rvu-efficiency-adjustment-specialty-strategies-for-comp), and [Nixon Peabody alert (Sep 2025)](https://www.nixonpeabody.com/insights/alerts/2025/09/10/cms-proposes-efficiency-adjustment-to-the-valuation-of-physician-work-rvus).

**Methods:** CMS Final Rule CMS-1832-F, effective January 1, 2026.

**Hot take:** Your administrator didn't cut your pay. CMS did — and your administrator may be counting on you not knowing the difference. If your contract is wRVU-based and your hospital isn't "shadowing" 2026 wRVUs back to 2025 values for internal compensation purposes, you are absorbing a stealth pay cut for doing the same job. Ask your CMO what the plan is. In writing. Before your next comp review.

### 2. 🌶️🌶️🌶️ HOT — 25 Studies. One Verdict: Prior Authorization Causes Measurable Patient Harm.

PMID [40912445](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40912445/)
*American Journal of Medicine · 2026 Jan;139(1):24-32 (epub Sep 3, 2025) | PL07*

**Key outcomes:** Systematic review of 25 U.S. studies across asthma, IBD, biologic therapies, psychiatric/SUD medications, anticoagulants, outpatient antibiotics, and oncology. Prior authorization consistently associated with: care delays, disease exacerbations, preventable hospitalizations, prolonged inpatient stays, and **lower disease-free survival in cancer**. In oncology, delays of even 1–3 weeks from guideline-based treatment correlate with worse disease control and lower survival.

**Methods:** Systematic review; 25 U.S. studies. Lead author: Jacob Murphy et al. — Johns Hopkins University School of Medicine (Brandyn D. Lau, Renee F. Wilson, Sarah J. Conway, Peter M. Hill, Pamela T. Johnson). DOI: 10.1016/j.amjmed.2025.08.018.

**Hot take:** The argument "prior auth just makes people wait a little" is now dead. Hopkins buried it with 25 studies in a peer-reviewed journal. When the next denial lands on your desk, attach PMID 40912445 to your peer-to-peer request. The evidence is on your side. The insurer is banking on you not citing it.

### 3. 🌶️🌶️🌶️ HOT — Physician Union Petitions Surged 11-Fold in 2023–2024. They're Winning 77% of Certifications.

PMID [39693063](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39693063/)
*JAMA · January 2025 | PL08*

**Key outcomes:** 77 union petitions with physician members filed 2000–2024. Annual petition rate: 2.1/year (2000–2022) → **23.3/year (2023–2024)** — an 11-fold increase. Certification rate: 62% overall; **77% in 2023–2024**. 7,064 physicians represented total. Employer breakdown: 49% hospitals, 38% community health centers, 13% nonhospital corporate (Optum, PE-backed). Top motivations: working conditions (85%), lack of voice in management (81%), patient care concerns (54%).

**Methods:** Retrospective analysis of all NLRB petitions 2000–2024 with physician members. Authors: Rooke-Ley H, Richman BD, Bowling AB, Nikolov NJ, Schulman KD. JAMA, January 2025.

**Hot take:** Physicians who spent 20 years saying "unions aren't for doctors" are now filing at 11× the historical rate and winning 3 of 4 certifications. The top driver isn't money — it's autonomy and voice. Your administration's strategy of eliminating physician input while expecting professional loyalty may be building the next bargaining unit.

### 4. 🌶️🌶️🌶️ HOT — Nonprofit Hospital CEOs Got 3× the Raise Workers Did Over 14 Years. The IRS Has the Receipts.

[Pay Gap Between Nonprofit Hospital CEOs And Employees Grew, 2009–23](https://www.healthaffairs.org/doi/10.1377/hlthaff.2024.01494) — Health Affairs, 2024
*Health Affairs · 2024 | PL02*

**Key outcomes:** Analysis of **1,424 U.S. nonprofit hospitals** (IRS Form 990 + Medicare cost reports). CEO-to-worker pay ratio: 10.2× (2009) → **12.0× (2023)** — a 17.6% widening. Average CEO pay: +27.5% ($814K → $1.04M). Average worker pay: +9.8%. For-profit extreme: HCA CEO Sam Hazen $23,799,137 total compensation (2024) vs. median HCA employee $60,820 — **391:1 ratio**.

**Methods:** Retrospective longitudinal, 1,424 nonprofit hospitals, 2009–2023. IRS Form 990 + Medicare cost reports. Authors: University of Chicago, Brown University, RAND Corporation. DOI: 10.1377/hlthaff.2024.01494.

**Hot take:** Your hospital is a *nonprofit*. Its CEO's compensation is public record — tonight, on ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer (nonprofits.propublica.org). Find your CEO's Schedule C compensation, divide by median physician pay at your shop. Decide if you want that ratio in your next Medical Executive Committee meeting.

## ⚔️ Receipts: Physician-led wins

### 5. 🌶️🌶️ MEDIUM — Physician CEO: OR 1.59 for Top Hospital Rankings. Only 22% of Hospitals Have One.

[Leadership Practices, Organization Structure, and Other Factors Associated with Higher 2022–2023 US News and World Report Ranking](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11606-024-09028-7) — Journal of General Internal Medicine, December 2025
*J Gen Intern Med · 2025 | PL01*

**Key outcomes:** Physician CEO leadership independently associated with higher hospital quality ranking: **OR 1.59 (95% CI 1.27–2.00, P<0.001)**. For-profit status negatively associated: OR 0.44 (P=0.005). Only **22% of institutions (118/546)** were physician-led. Group practice model also positive (OR 1.25, P=0.042). N=546 institutions, 1,676 hospitals.

**Methods:** Cross-sectional analysis, 2022–2023 US News & World Report rankings. N=546 institutions, 1,676 hospitals. Multiple logistic regression. PMC12686240. DOI: 10.1007/s11606-024-09028-7. Published December 2025.

**Hot take:** Physician-led hospitals have 59% better odds of top quality rankings. 78% of hospitals aren't physician-led. OR 1.59 is your one-slide argument for every board conversation about CMO authority and clinical governance. Bring the confidence intervals.

### 7. 🌶️🌶️ MEDIUM — Private Equity Physicians: Barely Half as Likely to Stay, Significantly Less Satisfied.

PMID [38466275](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38466275/)
*JAMA Internal Medicine · March 2024 | PL05*

**Key outcomes:** Cross-sectional survey; N=529 physicians; 5.5% (N=29) in PE-acquired practices. PE-employed: **44.8% likely to remain** vs. **77.8% in non-PE settings**. Significantly lower professional satisfaction and clinical autonomy. BMJ 2023 systematic review (55 studies, 8 countries): PE "most often associated with mixed to harmful quality impacts and higher costs." Hospital-physician consolidation: **17% higher commercial office-visit prices** (GAO-25-107450).

**Hot take:** PE promises "management support so you can focus on medicine." The physicians who took that deal are leaving at nearly twice the rate. Before signing with a PE-backed practice, ask for 3-year physician retention data. If they can't produce it, that IS the answer.

## 📈 Numbers don't lie

### 8. 🌶️🌶️ MEDIUM — After-Hours EHR Work Nearly Triples Burnout Odds. OR 2.43. This Is Not a Resilience Problem.

PMID [38865188](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38865188/)
*JMIR Medical Informatics · 2024 | PL03*

**Key outcomes:** Systematic review + meta-analysis; 37 studies; **N=66,556 clinicians**. Pooled burnout prevalence: **40.4% (95% CI 37.5%–43.2%)**. After-hours EHR work: **OR 2.43 (95% CI 2.31–2.57)** for burnout.

**Methods:** PubMed, Embase, Web of Science; January 2009–December 2022. Authors: Wu Y, Wu M, Wang C, Lin J, Liu J, Liu S. JMIR Med Inform 2024;12:e54811. DOI: 10.2196/54811.

**Hot take:** OR 2.43 is a case-control odds ratio, not a survey sentiment score. After-hours EHR work nearly triples burnout odds, and your organization *designed* the inbox system. Bring this number to every wellness committee until the conversation moves from mindfulness apps to workflow redesign.

## 🥊 Tactics this week

**ISO Week 20 → Category 4: Get the CPE. It's the Credential Admin Actually Respects.**

**Why this matters this week:** The JGIM data (item 5) show physician CEO leadership is associated with OR 1.59 for top hospital quality rankings — but only 22% of hospitals are physician-led. The CPE (Certified Physician Executive) is the formal signal that you are operating in leadership's domain on professional terms.

**The concrete step:** Visit **[aapl.org](https://www.aapl.org)** — the American Association for Physician Leadership. AAPL awards the CPE credential requiring AAPL-accredited education, a leadership assessment, and peer review portfolio. Cost: roughly $5,000–$15,000 in coursework vs. $60,000–$120,000 for an MBA. AAPL membership alone ($600–$800/year) provides the *Physician Leadership Journal*, governance and negotiation white papers, national physician executive network, and CME credit. For Jon's profile — physiatrist, SCI Program Director, MSU adjunct faculty — CPE combined with existing leadership experience is a documented pathway to CMO, VPMA, or governance board roles.

**Time to do it:** 15 minutes to explore aapl.org and join as a member; 30 minutes to review CPE requirements.
